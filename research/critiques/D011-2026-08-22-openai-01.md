## Frozen target

D011 conditionally claims, in the fixed-interface planar basic persistent
`SO_0(1,3)` rank-three sector, that the shell block has rank ten with
`rank(dQ)=5`; that `Q=qT_{03}^*` has coadjoint stabilizer
`span(T03,T12)` but only `T12` is a structural null; that complete removal
requires five effective first-class directions and five transverse moment
equations; and that the survivor obeys
`rank >= 10-2 min(r,c)`. Interface-only exterior zero reduction is empty for
`Q != 0`, whereas a total bulk-interface zero level may cancel the nonzero
interface moment. The bulk Hamiltonian action, differentiability, closure,
coisotropy, and corner polarization are expressly not derived.

## Load-bearing inference

The most load-bearing local inference is the distinction between the
two-dimensional stabilizer of the *value* `Q` and the one-dimensional kernel of
the rank-ten shell two-form. It is what turns six Lorentz parameters into five
effective directions and singles out the radial fifth pair.

## Reconstruction

In D006's local `(v,e)` chart the shell form has matrix

\[
 \Omega_I=\begin{pmatrix}A_Q&-D\\D^T&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 D=dQ,\quad (A_Q)_{IJ}=\langle Q,[T_I,T_J]\rangle .
\]

At `Q=qT03*`, four generators move the coadjoint orbit. Both `T03` and `T12`
commute with `T03`, so both lie in `ker A_Q`, giving the fixed-value stabilizer.
But `rank D=5` and `ann(im D)=span(T12)`. Hence a pure group direction `x` can
be null only if both `A_Qx=0` and `D^Tx=0`: this selects `x=T12`. For `x=T03`,
`A_Qx=0` but `D^Tx` evaluates nontrivially on the area variation
`delta Q=(delta q)T03*`. Thus `T03` is paired with the radial variation while
`T12` is the sole structural group null. This compactly supports the local
five-pair count and the stabilizer-versus-kernel distinction.

## Falsification attempts

### 1. Stabilizer versus kernel/effective-action counting

This attack does not overturn the local algebra. D011 correctly avoids
subtracting the full two-dimensional coadjoint stabilizer from the effective
rank: fixing `Q` and varying `Q(e)` are different operations. The result is,
however, only a statement about the projected shell block in the declared
chart. It does not by itself show that five corresponding transformations are
admissible, differentiable gauge transformations of the matched total phase
space. D011 mostly preserves this distinction in its necessary conditions.

### 2. Survivor-rank bound under singularity or bulk mixing

This attack succeeds against the theorem as stated. Equation (21) is not
established merely from the definitions of `r` and `c`. In a total reduction,
an orbit can project onto an interface configuration direction while the
differential of its Hamiltonian constraint lies partly or wholly in bulk
directions; conversely, a constraint can cut an interface momentum direction
while its gauge orbit is represented in the bulk. Without a clean invariant
splitting and a coisotropic first-class normal form, there is no intrinsic
"survivor rank of the interface block," and the number of interface directions
removed need not occur in matched pairs counted by `min(r,c)`.

Even dimensionally, the supplied reasoning only says that a matched usable
generator can remove at most two directions. It does not prove that unmatched
projected orbit or constraint directions remove zero interface directions,
which is required for the lower bound `10-2 min(r,c)`. The sentence that the
inequality survives orbit-type singularities is therefore too strong. A safe
version must either (a) assume a clean coisotropic local splitting in which
each effective generator pairs one declared interface configuration with one
declared interface momentum, or (b) replace (21) by a bound formulated for the
full reduced tangent complex, with explicit intersection/projection ranks.
Accordingly equations (21), the associated “every missing generator leaves at
least two directions” statement, and the conclusion's unconditional use of
the bound need theorem-level revision.

### 3. Counterexample C and logical underdetermination

This attack also succeeds. Appending a cotangent Hamiltonian `G_-` factor and
writing `p+J_I=0` proves that the total zero level can be nonempty, but it does
not prove that reduction removes the interface five-pair block. In the standard
free cotangent-extension construction, adding `T^*G` and then reducing by the
diagonal `G` can return a reduced space locally symplectomorphic to the original
space: the compensator absorbs the moment value without eliminating the
original physical pairs. D011's clause “if the clean maximal-rank conditions
hold, it removes” assumes precisely the projected-rank and kernel properties
that Counterexample C is meant to exhibit.

Thus C is a valid witness for possible *level cancellation*, but not yet an
opposite canonical outcome to spectator model B. The broad conclusion that
interface data alone are underdetermining remains plausible and is already
motivated by the missing bulk generator, but C does not demonstrate it without
an explicit action, constraint surface, and reduced-form calculation (or extra
constraints beyond the bare cotangent extension).

## Findings

- **Preserved result:** the six-generator local action has four coadjoint-orbit
  directions, one radial `T03`-paired direction, and one structural `T12` null.
- **Preserved result:** from D006's moment map and `Q != 0`, the interface-only
  exterior zero level is empty; D005 momentum matching gives the stated sign of
  the required bulk cancellation.
- **Required revision:** the singularity-robust bound (21) lacks the splitting/
  coisotropy hypotheses needed to assign paired removals to this block.
- **Required revision:** Counterexample C establishes nonempty total level, not
  removal of all five interface pairs; its claimed opposite outcome is
  conditional on the desired conclusion.
- **Residual risk:** even after correcting these points, actual removal remains
  conditional on the undeclared bulk Hamiltonian and corner data, as D011
  itself acknowledges.

## Machine-check candidates

1. Build finite-dimensional linear Hamiltonian product normal forms with an
interface symplectic block of rank ten and mixed bulk-interface constraint
differentials. Compute `r`, `c`, and the reduced tangent-complex rank. Expected
outcome: (21) holds under an explicitly paired clean splitting, while mixed
models expose that `r,c` alone do not determine a lower bound. This would not
settle nonlinear differentiability or the physical bulk theory.
2. For the precise cotangent extension proposed in (24), calculate the zero
level, quotient, and induced two-form rather than assuming maximal projected
rank. Expected outcome for the standard diagonal cotangent compensator is that
level cancellation alone does not delete the original interface symplectic
content. This would test Counterexample C, not whether an actual Palatini bulk
completion can remove the block.

## Outcome

`revision-required`

The stabilizer/kernel reconstruction is sound, but two theorem-bearing bridges
are not: the advertised survivor-rank bound needs clean first-class splitting
hypotheses (or reformulation on the full tangent complex), and Counterexample C
does not establish pair removal without assuming the needed reduction
properties.
