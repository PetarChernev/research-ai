## Reconstruction

Variation of `Sφ` gives `φ̈−∇²φ+V′=0` and a nonnegative conserved background energy. For locally kink-matched resting data, `q″=V′(q)` cancels the planar terms at `r=L`; consequently `φ̈=2q′/L` and the zero level set has `ℓ̈(0)=−2/L`. Exponential vacuum tails make the spherical energy finite, with leading value `4πσL²`. Neither this conservation law nor the initial acceleration requires a static bubble.

The spectator measure is `R d⁴x dθ`. Its Fourier modes therefore obey

`R⁻¹∂μ(R∂^μψn)−n²R⁻²ψn=0`.

In a constant phase, `χn=√R ψn` yields canonical mass squared `n²/R²`, so `Rin>Rout` gives the stated homogeneous window. Circle symmetry keeps different `n` sectors decoupled even when `R` evolves. Thus this action supplies distinct local dispersion relations, not conversion of an exterior zero mode into an interior excited circle mode.

## Falsification attempts

### 1. Finite sphere versus homogeneous threshold

**Attack:** an arbitrarily weak or small three-dimensional well need not bind a mode, despite lower interior mass. The frozen radial operator has quadratic form

`Q[u]=∫r²R(|u′|²+R⁻²|u|²)dr / ∫r²R|u|²dr`.

For a profile bounded by `Rin`, this gives `Q≥Rin⁻²`; the asymptotic continuum begins at `Rout⁻²`. An approximately constant interior ball of radius `a` admits a Dirichlet trial mode with quotient approximately `Rin⁻²+π²/a²`, plus plateau errors. Binding therefore needs enough spatial extent relative to the mass-squared contrast, not just `L≫δ`. As `f→∞`, the contrast closes while the wall remains: a fixed-size bubble loses this operational witness.

**Result:** serious restriction, but already acknowledged by D028. It does not invalidate its scoped homogeneous result. The spectral lower bound also presumes the chosen initial smoothing does not overshoot `+v`; monotone non-overshooting initial data should be fixed in any adopted check, rather than assumed for all subsequent evolution.

### 2. Lifetime and probe preparation

**Attack:** the frozen spectrum might not be measurably realized before contraction. At early times `ℓ(t)=L−t²/L+…`, so a snapshot is useful only on suitably shorter times than `L`. Frequencies close to the interior threshold have small group velocity; crossing or resolving a near-threshold state can take too long. For low cavity levels at large `L`, frequency spacings can scale as `1/(m_in L²)`, so `ωL≫1` alone does not establish adiabatic following or spectral resolution. Exact `n` conservation independently forbids populating `n=1` from an incident `n=0` wave.

**Result:** no contradiction to the initial-value construction; a real operational witness still needs a specified interior source or initially populated packet and a demonstrated observation-time hierarchy. A local packet of extent much smaller than `L` need not resolve the entire cavity spectrum, so failure of global spectral resolution would not rule out every operational experiment. D028 correctly leaves finite-lifetime accessibility open.

### 3. Normalization, frame, and energy accounting

**Attack:** treating `n/R` as the entire mass on a varying background or importing a gravitational radion normalization would change the bridge. With `s=ln(R/R₀)` and `χ=√R ψ`, the exact canonical equation is

`□χ−[n²/R²+(□s)/2+(∂s)²/4]χ=0`.

The derivative terms disappear only in constant phases. For a sharp radius contrast, spatial gradients scale with `v/(fδ)`, not merely `1/δ`; a cutoff check based solely on wall thickness would miss hidden contrast dependence. The original weighted action nevertheless has positive instantaneous spectator energy. Background `Eφ` alone is conserved only in the spectator-free or test-field approximation; with backreaction, it is the combined Noether energy that is conserved.

**Result:** Fourier normalization, signs, and fixed-base energy convention are consistent. D028 explicitly states the full variable-radius equation, gradient restriction, probe-energy caveat, and absence of Einstein-frame claims. No normalization or energy obstruction was found within those assumptions.
