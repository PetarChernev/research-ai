## Frozen target

D026 now claims only that, for a `C1` tangential connection trace
`a=i_Y^*A` on `Y=Sigma x S1`, with local triviality and ordinary descent along
the smooth projection `p:Y->Sigma`,

\[
 a\text{ descends along }p
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 H_u[a]=1,\qquad \iota_{\partial_\theta}F_a=0.
\]

It explicitly excludes a connection-sheaf/effectivity assertion on the
rank-jumping quotient `X_+`, unrestricted collar-normal data, reduction-field
descent, action stationarity, and canonical or PDE conclusions. For
`a=cT_{43}dtheta`, it claims admission exactly on the identity-holonomy lattice
and exclusion of every nonzero constant `delta chi T_{43}dtheta` at an admitted
lattice point.

## Load-bearing inference

The load-bearing inference is the equivalence between kernel-pair connection
descent along `p` and the two intrinsic conditions `H=1` and
`i_v F_a=0`. This is what makes the polar lattice and tangent exclusion a
classification rather than a stipulated boundary condition, while its domain
must remain strictly on `Y`.

## Reconstruction

If descent data `(a,gamma)` exist, a vertical circle maps to a constant loop
downstairs, so its holonomy is the identity; curvature is the pullback of
downstairs curvature up to the descent frame, hence has zero vertical
contraction. Conversely, identity vertical holonomy lets one solve the circle
parallel-transport ODE in a single-valued periodic frame with `a_theta=0`.
Then `F_{theta mu}=partial_theta a_mu`, so `i_vF=0` makes the remaining
coefficients independent of `theta`; the trace is therefore a pullback from
`Sigma`. At the declared `C1` level, the ODE and its parameter dependence are
sufficient, and the descended coefficients can be read at the fixed base
angle, where they retain `C1` regularity.

For `a=cT_{43}dtheta`, this yields `H=exp(2pi cT_{43})` and
`gamma_c=exp[cT_{43}(theta_2-theta_1)]`. The latter has the stated cocycle
orientation and affine sign and is single-valued exactly when `H=1`.

## Falsification attempts

### 1. Reapply the full-collar counterexample — repair holds

Take `A=f(theta)ds` with nonconstant periodic `f` (understood as
Lie-algebra-valued, or scalar times a fixed generator). Its tangential trace is
zero and therefore passes the boundary criterion, while
`F_A=f'(theta)dtheta wedge ds` can obstruct ordinary collar extension. This
still defeats any attempted promotion to full singular-collar descent.

The revision now fences the example correctly: `TrConn_lr(p)` contains only
`a=i_Y^*A`; Assumptions 2 and 5, Lemma 1, the validity regime, unresolved
concerns, and the conclusion all expressly deny control of normal coefficients
or a connection on `X_+`. The polar result uses only the tangential trace
`cT_{43}dtheta`, so the counterexample does not defeat that result. No stale
positive full-collar effectivity claim was found.

### 2. Try to rescue the constant mode through variable cocycle data — fails

Linearizing the exact criterion gives
`delta H=0` and `i_v D_a delta a=0`; equivalently, linearizing the affine
descent equation with `xi=delta gamma gamma^{-1}` gives D026's equations
(14)--(15). At an allowed polar background, the commuting constant mode obeys

\[
 H^{-1}\delta H=2\pi\,\delta\chi\,T_{43}.
\]

Variable `delta gamma` cannot change the fact that every descended fiber has
identity holonomy. Nor can a periodic infinitesimal presentation parameter
produce the mode, since `-partial_theta epsilon=delta chi T_{43}` has zero
circle integral only for `delta chi=0`. The explicit finite map `h_c` correctly
maps `(a,gamma_c)` to `(0,1)` at lattice `c`, but is an isomorphism of descent
presentations rather than an automorphism of the original fixed cocycle; it
does not create a tangent between lattice points. No loophole was found.
