# Literature note: Instability of the Kaluza-Klein vacuum

## Question investigated

Which exact geometric regularity condition makes a compact Kaluza--Klein circle pinch smooth, what Lorentzian bubble results, and what boundary contribution is required in the corresponding gravitational action? Does this fixed-dimensional example realize the project's exact open-stratum dimension change or first-order transition dynamics?

## Search strategy

On 2026-08-19, the title and DOI were checked through Crossref, the DOI/publisher record, OpenAlex, and INSPIRE. The full twelve-page journal scan was read through an accessible reprint; the stable DOI, not the reprint host, is recorded as the canonical locator. Equations 3--6 and 10 and their surrounding text were extracted directly. Backward references to Tangherlini and Euclidean black-hole methods were noted so that the higher-dimensional Schwarzschild metric and gravitational surface term were not misattributed to Witten.

## Claims supported

- **Literature-supported smooth-pinch ingredient:** the Euclidean five-dimensional Schwarzschild geometry is nonsingular at the minimum radius only when the shrinking compact coordinate has a period fixed by local polar regularity. Matching the asymptotic Kaluza--Klein circle fixes the Schwarzschild parameter to `R^2`.
- **Literature-supported Lorentzian example:** analytic continuation gives a nonsingular, geodesically complete five-dimensional solution in which the compact circle smoothly shrinks at an expanding bubble. A four-dimensional observer unable to resolve the circle sees an excised expanding region, while the fifth-dimensional cap removes the would-be boundary.
- **Literature-supported boundary-action warning:** Eq. 10 includes a surface term to cancel second-derivative terms. Since the bounce has zero scalar curvature, only the surface term contributes, giving Euclidean action `I=pi R^2/(4G)` and a leading decay factor `exp[-pi R^2/(4G)]` in the paper's conventions.
- **Literature-supported stability distinction:** `M^4 x S^1` has no exponentially growing linear modes in the spectrum described on p. 482, yet it is semiclassically unstable when the non-vacuum topology and spin structure of the bounce are admitted. Linear stability of one phase therefore does not settle nonperturbative access to a smooth circle pinch.

## Claims contradicted

- No exact C001--C003 claim is contradicted: the example is always a smooth five-dimensional manifold and does not have an open intrinsically four-dimensional exterior.
- The source contradicts the neighboring inference that a smooth circle cap is automatically a stable particle-like defect. In this example it is the bounce mediating catastrophic decay of the Kaluza--Klein vacuum.
- It also contradicts the practice of evaluating this Ricci-flat saddle from the bulk Einstein--Hilbert term alone: the bulk term vanishes and the quoted nonzero action is entirely a surface contribution.
- The distinction from the project is geometric, not a convention difference: vanishing orbit size at a bolt is not the same as quotienting away a physical coordinate on an open region.

## Exact evidence location

- pp. 481--482: definition of the five-dimensional vacuum `M^4 x S^1`, circle radius `R`, and statement that the small-oscillation spectrum has no imaginary-frequency growing modes.
- p. 483, Eqs. 1--2: Euclidean Kaluza--Klein vacuum and its polar-coordinate form.
- p. 483, Eq. 3 and the paragraph through Eq. 4: Euclidean five-dimensional Schwarzschild metric; near-tip coordinate change; absence of a conical singularity requires compact-coordinate period `2 pi sqrt(alpha)`; matching period `2 pi R` sets `alpha=R^2`. The text immediately after Eq. 4 calls the resulting space nonsingular and geodesically complete with `r >= R`.
- pp. 484--485, Eqs. 5--6: analytic continuation to the Lorentzian bubble metric and statement of nonsingularity/geodesic completeness.
- p. 486, paragraphs beginning “The Kaluza-Klein space…”: the compact fifth direction shrinks to zero at `r=R` and smoothly seals the would-be four-dimensional boundary.
- pp. 487--489: zero-energy/asymptotic argument, changed initial-surface topology, and the assumption that non-vacuum topology is included; the spin-structure restriction is explained on pp. 488--490.
- p. 490, Eq. 10 and following paragraph: five-dimensional Einstein action “+ surface term,” statement that the scalar curvature vanishes, surface contribution `pi R^2/(4G)`, and semiclassical regime `R` much larger than the Planck length.
- Appendix, pp. 490--491, Eqs. 11--14: argument for a negative mode of the Euclidean bounce.

## Assumptions / regime

- Pure five-dimensional Einstein gravity asymptotic to `M^4 x S^1`, with asymptotic circle radius `R` and five-dimensional gravitational constant written as `2 pi R G`.
- Euclidean saddle-point/semiclassical treatment, followed by a specific real Lorentzian analytic continuation.
- Inclusion in the gravitational path integral of manifolds whose topology differs from that of the vacuum; Witten explicitly identifies this as a contestable assumption.
- No elementary fermions obstructing the required asymptotic antiperiodic spin structure.
- Quantitative semiclassical rate only for `R` well above the Planck length.
- Fixed five-dimensional smooth manifold throughout. Circle orbits collapse on a bolt/bubble locus, not throughout an open four-dimensional phase.
- Metric second-order gravity; Eq. 10 names but does not display or derive the surface-term integrand, and no first-order coframe boundary variation is performed.

## Unresolved issues

- The exact surface-term formula and sign convention are not printed in Eq. 10; they must be traced to the originating metric boundary-term source before transferring the normalization.
- The first-order Palatini/coframe boundary term appropriate to a shrinking circle and any corner/bolt contribution remain to be derived.
- Whether a quotient can convert the fixed-dimensional bolt into an open exactly four-dimensional stratum without retaining fiber holonomy or resolution data.
- The paper establishes a global instability, not a well-posed transition initial-boundary value problem or a complete perturbation spectrum of a stationary defect.
- Stable publisher full text was not openly accessible during extraction; the evidence was checked from a complete third-party scan tied to the verified DOI.
