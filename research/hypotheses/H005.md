# H005: Hopf topology does not force statistics

## Question addressed

research/QUESTION.md

## Statement

The standard Hopf-map Z2 loop and odd-fiber spinorial rotor phase do not suffice to derive fermionic exchange in the full dimensional theory: admissible geometric histories may trivialize the loop, or the FR character may remain an independent quantization choice not fixed by fiber momentum.

## Motivation

Finkelstein–Rubinstein topology generally permits characters of a configuration-space fundamental group; it does not by itself select one. A spinorial phase of an internal/collective rotor also need not determine the permutation character of extended defects. Moreover, enlarging a Hopf-map subspace to all allowed dimensional geometries can kill its loop. This null alternative prevents the project from conflating half-integer angular momentum, a noncontractible rotation loop, and fermionic exchange.

## Assumptions

- The standard Hopf-map and rotor results may hold in their restricted spaces; the hypothesis concerns their extension and coupling.
- The full theory permits at least the geometric fluctuations, overlaps, and rank changes required by its dynamics.
- Quantum states may admit independent flat line-bundle choices unless a microscopic term or constraint removes them.
- No fundamental spin-statistics theorem is imported before locality, causal QFT, and the physical configuration space are established.

## Predictions

- At least one of the following occurs: the inclusion kills the Hopf `\mathbb Z_2`; exchange is not the same full-space class as single-defect rotation; or odd `k` fails to select a unique character on that class.
- A fixed odd right-fiber weight can still yield a lowest `j=1/2` rotor multiplet while bosonic and fermionic exchange quantizations remain mathematically available.
- Additional microscopic input—a topological term, anomaly, fundamental fermion, or explicit quantization postulate—is required to select fermionic statistics.

## What would support it

- An explicit contraction through an admissible overlap/rank-changing configuration.
- A bundle-classification calculation showing two inequivalent flat characters remain at fixed odd `k`.
- A counterexample model with odd-`k` half-integer spin but bosonic exchange under otherwise identical assumptions.

## What would falsify it

- A complete microscopic reduction proves that fixed odd `k` uniquely fixes the quantum bundle and its common `-1` holonomy on nontrivial rotation and exchange loops in the full physical space.
- The full admissible-space inclusion is injective and no independent flat twist is compatible with the action, constraints, and regularity conditions.

## Discriminating experiments/derivations

1. Classify quantum line bundles/characters over the exact configuration space at fixed `k`, rather than infer exchange from a one-defect rotor phase.
2. Construct overlap and core-merger homotopies in the full geometric category and compute the induced map from the Hopf subspace.
3. Audit the cited Hopf-soliton literature for the exact scope of rotation/exchange parity and whether the fermionic character is derived or imposed.

## Current evidence

- `research/results/verification/C004-2026-08-19.md` is inconclusive on the full claim but sharpens H005: functorial inclusion need not preserve nontriviality; an enlargement from `S^2` to `D^3` contracts every Hopf loop; and the physical rotation class depends on the domain/target action. Admissibility of these countermodels is not yet defined.
- The verifier did not test C005. The distinction between an allowed and dynamically selected FR character remains a logical gap, not a demonstrated failure.

## Open problems

- Specify full-space admissibility before testing loop contraction.
- Determine whether the microscopic symplectic form couples fiber momentum to the FR loop.
- Separate a failure of this mechanism from a failure of spin-statistics in the eventual local low-energy QFT.
