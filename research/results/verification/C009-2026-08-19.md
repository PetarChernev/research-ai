## Reconstruction

### 1. Kernel pair and whether the equalizer is ad hoc

For `q:M->X`, the set-theoretic kernel pair is

\[
R_q=M\times_XM
=\{(u,s,\theta;u,s,\theta):s>0\}
\cup
\{(u,s,\theta_1;u,s,\theta_2):s\leq0\}.
\]

Give it the categorical fiber-product diffeology. Necessity of

\[
p_1^*\alpha=p_2^*\alpha
\]

for the pullback of a form on `X` is immediate.

For this final quotient diffeology, sufficiency can also be proved directly. Every plot `P:O->X` locally has a smooth lift `P_tilde:O->M`. Given an equalizing form `alpha` on `M`, define the form on that plot by `P_tilde^* alpha`. On an overlap, two lifts form a plot `(P_tilde_1,P_tilde_2)` of `R_q`; the equalizer identity makes the two pullbacks agree. The local forms therefore glue and are compatible with smooth reparametrization. Conversely, local liftability makes pullback by `q` injective on plotwise forms.

Thus, under the precise final diffeology and fiber-product diffeology in C009,

\[
q^*\Omega^k_{\mathrm{diff}}(X)
=\operatorname{Eq}\bigl(\Omega^k(M)\rightrightarrows\Omega^k(R_q)\bigr).
\]

The complex is not merely ad hoc in this category. This formal descent statement is different from identifying the equalizer with a weaker infinitesimal notion of “basic form”; the regular-foliation theorem summarized in the Hector--Macías-Virgós--Sanmartín-Carbón note is not needed and would not cover the jumping fibers.

### 2. Necessary and sufficient one-form conditions, including `s=0`

Use the globally defined invariant angular one-form, denoted `dtheta`, and write

\[
\alpha=a_\mu(u,s,\theta)dx^\mu+b(u,s,\theta)d\theta,
\qquad (x^\mu)=(u^0,u^1,u^2,s).
\]

On the open part `s<0` of the full kernel-pair branch,

\[
p_1^*\alpha
=a_\mu(u,s,\theta_1)dx^\mu+b(u,s,\theta_1)d\theta_1,
\]

\[
p_2^*\alpha
=a_\mu(u,s,\theta_2)dx^\mu+b(u,s,\theta_2)d\theta_2.
\]

The variables `theta_1` and `theta_2` and their tangent directions are independent. Equality requires and is implied by

\[
a_\mu(u,s,\theta)\text{ independent of }\theta,
\qquad b(u,s,\theta)=0
\quad(s<0).
\]

Smoothness extends these conditions to `s=0`. In fact, the angular part of each `a_mu` and all of `b` vanish on an open half-slab, so their normal jets at zero are flat. This handles the possible concern that an off-diagonal point of the relation at `s=0` has fewer crossing plots: the interior conditions plus smoothness already fix all coefficient values there. On `s>0` the relation is diagonal and imposes no condition. Hence the same conditions with `s<=0` are necessary and sufficient, with no additional first-order interface relation.

Exterior differentiation preserves the equalizer because it commutes with pullback.

### 3. Evaluation of the requested forms

Let

\[
A=A_\mu(u,s)dx^\mu,\qquad
\eta=d\theta+A,\qquad F=dA.
\]

Then:

1. `eta` has vertical coefficient `b=1`, so it fails the equalizer on every collapsed fiber. It does descend on the regular `s>0` stratum, but not through a neighborhood meeting `s<=0`.
2. `rho_i eta` has vertical coefficient `rho_i` and horizontal coefficient `rho_i A`. Both vanish for `s<=0`, including at the infinitely flat interface. Therefore `rho_i(dtheta+A)` descends.
3. `A` has no vertical leg and is angle-independent, so it descends as a local one-form. Since `d` preserves descent, `F=dA` descends as a two-form.
4. A local mixed connection potential

   \[
   \omega^{4a}=w_\mu^{4a}(u,s)dx^\mu+\chi^{4a}(u,s)d\theta
   \]

   has a descending horizontal term. Its vertical term descends only if `chi^{4a}=0` for `s<=0`; smoothness then makes its interface jet flat. A factor such as `rho_i` is sufficient. Angle independence alone does not save a nonzero vertical leg.

The exact coframe derivative is

\[
d[\rho_i(d\theta+A)]
=d\rho_i\wedge(d\theta+A)+\rho_i F.
\]

Both terms vanish on `s<=0` because the smooth exact-collapse profile and its derivative vanish there. This is a special consequence of the `C-infinity` flat profile, not a generic finite-regularity interface statement.

### 4. U(1) gauge and the exterior bump

Under the declared vertical change of trivialization,

\[
\theta\mapsto\theta-\alpha,
\qquad A\mapsto A+d\alpha,
\]

one has `eta->eta` and `F->F`. Therefore a nonzero `F` cannot become zero under ordinary U(1) gauge, including large local representatives; curvature is unchanged pointwise.

Choose a nonconstant smooth `chi(s)` compactly supported in `(-epsilon,0)` and set

\[
A'=\chi(s)du^1,
\qquad F'=\chi'(s)ds\wedge du^1.
\]

For `[chi]=L^-1`, `A'` and `F'` have the declared dimensions as integrated forms. Since a nonconstant compactly supported bump has `chi'` nonzero somewhere, `F'` is nonzero and `(A',F')` is not gauge-equivalent to `(0,0)`. Yet `rho_i A'=0` everywhere the fields differ, so the coframe `rho_i(dtheta+A')` is exactly the same as for `A=0`. This establishes the distinction between U(1) gauge and the kernel of the coframe parametrization.

### 5. SO(1,4) connection and mixed curvature

For an independent local Lorentz connection,

\[
R=d\omega+\omega\wedge\omega,
\qquad
\omega' =\Lambda\omega\Lambda^{-1}-d\Lambda\,\Lambda^{-1},
\qquad
R'=\Lambda R\Lambda^{-1}.
\]

Conjugation is invertible. Consequently nonzero **total** curvature can never be gauge-transformed to zero curvature.

The phrase “mixed curvature” needs a more careful invariant definition. On the rank-four exterior of this explicit benchmark, the map supplied by the coframe has a nondegenerate internal image `W` of signature `(-,+,+,+)`. It therefore has a unit spacelike internal normal `n`, unique up to sign, and an orthogonal projector

\[
P^A{}_B=\delta^A{}_B-n^A n_B
\]

onto `W`. Define the coframe-relative mixed curvature by

\[
K^B:=n_A R^{AC}P_C{}^B.
\]

When both `E` and `R` undergo the same Lorentz transformation, `n`, `P`, and `K` transform covariantly. Hence `K=0` versus `K!=0` is frame-independent. In a gauge with `n=e_4`, `K` is the collection `R^{4a}`; under the residual `SO(1,3)` it transforms invertibly as a Lorentz vector of two-forms.

By contrast, the fixed labels `R^{4a}` alone are not invariant under the full `SO(1,4)`. A constant Lorentz rotation exchanging two spacelike internal labels can move a component out of the label-`4` slot while transforming the coframe normal at the same time. Thus C009 is correct only for nonzero total curvature or the coframe-relative `K`, not for an unqualified coordinate component.

For the explicit example

\[
\omega^{40}=\chi(s)du^1,
\qquad \omega^{04}=-\omega^{40},
\]

with all other independent components zero, the quadratic term does not contribute and

\[
R^{40}=\chi'(s)ds\wedge du^1.
\]

It is horizontal, angle-independent, and descended componentwise in the selected local frame. On the support of the bump, `n=e_4`, so the relative mixed curvature `K` is nonzero. It cannot be gauge-equivalent to the zero connection, since the latter has zero curvature.

### 6. Rank-four normal and the three notions of removal

No inverse spacetime coframe is needed to define `W=im(E)` and its orthogonal normal in this explicit nondegenerate rank-four sector. Nevertheless, the construction requires more than the bare integer “rank four”:

- the internal image must be nondegenerate;
- a smooth local choice of the sign of `n` is needed if `n` itself, rather than only `P`, is used;
- on the intrinsic three-dimensional interface stratum, the restricted coframe has rank three and there is no unique one-dimensional internal normal of this kind.

The curvature bump is supported in the open rank-four exterior, so these caveats do not invalidate that example. They do prevent exporting the projector argument to arbitrary degenerate rank-four coframes or to the intrinsic tangent of `Sigma` without more hypotheses.

Finally, three operations must remain distinct:

1. **Coframe-only omission:** if the state contains only `E`, exterior `A` and `F` were never independent state variables. Different auxiliary `A` representatives can map to the same `E`.
2. **Ordinary gauge reduction:** U(1) and Lorentz transformations act on retained connection data. They leave `F` invariant or conjugate `R`; they do not identify nonzero curvature with zero curvature.
3. **Forgetful equivalence:** if the state initially retains `A` or `omega^{4a}`, quotienting by the kernel of a projection that discards exterior connection data is an additional declared equivalence. No action, first-class generator, zero charge, or presymplectic null direction has been supplied to make it gauge.

A field equation or boundary condition could instead restrict admissible solutions to zero exterior curvature. That is dynamical exclusion, not gauge erasure or equality in the bare quotient calculus.

## Falsification attempts

1. **Equalizer-versus-genuine-form attack.** I attempted to separate the equalizer from standard quotient-diffeological forms. Local liftability of every quotient plot and the fiber-product condition provide effective descent, so they coincide for the exact final diffeology used here. They need not coincide with other proposed calculi or with all invariant forms on an arbitrary resolution.
2. **Interface-condition attack.** At off-diagonal kernel-pair points over `s=0`, crossing tangent plots are restricted. Nonetheless, the full open branch at `s<0` forces the coefficient conditions, and smoothness fixes their limits and jets at zero. No extra or missing one-form condition was found.
3. **Bare-angular-form attack.** Adding horizontal `A` cannot cancel the independent `dtheta_1-dtheta_2` legs on the full collapsed relation. `dtheta+A` therefore fails exactly as claimed.
4. **Weighted-angular-form attack.** Multiplication by `rho_i` kills both vertical and horizontal coefficients on the entire collapsed branch. Infinite flatness also removes a possible `d rho` interface residue after differentiation.
5. **Vertical mixed-component attack.** A theta-independent `chi dtheta` still fails unless `chi` vanishes on `s<=0`. This is a material restriction; “theta-independent mixed components descend” is true only for the horizontal part (or a suitably vanishing vertical part).
6. **U(1) pure-gauge attack.** The exterior bump has `F'=chi' ds wedge du1`, so it cannot be written as a gauge transform of zero on any neighborhood containing a point where `chi'!=0`.
7. **Lorentz pure-gauge attack.** The explicit connection has nonzero curvature. Since curvature transforms by invertible conjugation, no full `SO(1,4)` transformation sends it to the zero connection.
8. **Component-label counterexample.** A full Lorentz frame change can make the fixed components carrying label `4` vanish while moving the coframe normal and curvature to other labels. This falsifies any unqualified reading of “`R^{4a}` itself is invariant.” The covariant normal/projector formulation survives.
9. **Rank-only normal attack.** Rank four alone does not guarantee a normalized internal normal if the coframe image is null/degenerate. The explicit benchmark's induced `(-,+,+,+)` image does guarantee one on the open exterior; the intrinsic interface rank-three restriction does not.
10. **Gauge-versus-forgetting attack.** Neither the point kernel pair nor `Pi_cof/Pi_4` was derived as the orbit relation of regular first-class constraints. D001 sections 7--9 reinforces this exact logical gap. Calling the omission “gauge” would exceed the evidence.

## Checks

- **Dimensions and units:** `[theta]=1`, `[A]=[omega]=1` as integrated one-forms, `[rho]=L`, and `[E^4]=L`. Coefficients of `A=chi du1` require `[chi]=L^-1`; then `[chi']=L^-2`, while `F` and `R` are dimensionless integrated two-forms. All sums and products are dimensionally consistent.
- **Signs and normalization:** With `theta->theta-alpha` and `A->A+dalpha`, `dtheta+A` is exactly invariant. Antisymmetry `omega^{04}=-omega^{40}` is consistent, and the single-generator example has no hidden `omega wedge omega` contribution to `R^{40}`.
- **Symmetries and conservation laws:** U(1) curvature is invariant and Lorentz curvature is covariant, so zero versus nonzero total curvature is gauge-invariant. No conserved charge, constraint generator, or boundary symmetry has been derived; the kernel and forgetful relations must not be promoted to gauge.
- **Limiting or exactly soluble cases:** On `s>0`, `q` is locally one-to-one and all listed forms restrict normally. On `s<0`, the vertical coefficient must vanish. At `s=0`, profile flatness makes both `rho_i` and `d rho_i` zero. Setting `chi` constant makes the explicit local curvature zero and removes the obstruction, as expected.
- **Numerical reproducibility and convergence:** Not applicable; all descent and gauge checks are exact. No numerical code, tolerance, data, or random seed was involved.
- **Hidden parameter dependence:** The result depends on the final quotient diffeology, fiber-product diffeology, smooth exact-collapse profiles, a local trivial circle bundle, horizontal/angle-independent connection representatives, nondegenerate rank-four coframe image for the normal decomposition, and the declared ordinary gauge groups.

## Findings

1. **Terminology/index correction required:** D002's phrase “coframe kernel normal `n^A`” should be “unit internal normal to the nondegenerate rank-four image of the coframe.” The coframe kernel is a spacetime direction and is not `n^A`. The projector should be written with internal indices, for example `P^A_B=delta^A_B-n^A n_B`.
2. **Gauge-invariant scope correction required:** an individual fixed-label component `R^{4a}` can be moved by full `SO(1,4)`. The nonremovability claim is valid for nonzero total curvature and for the coframe-relative projection `K=n contraction R contraction P` when `E` transforms too.
3. **Rank restriction required:** the normal/projector argument is valid on this benchmark's nondegenerate rank-four exterior (and, if using the quotient derivation tangent, its rank-four interface germ), not on the intrinsic rank-three tangent of `Sigma` and not for arbitrary null rank-four coframes.
4. **Descent statement supported:** with the declared final and fiber-product diffeologies, the equalizer is the actual quotient-diffeological de Rham complex by a direct local-lift argument. It remains only one chosen calculus and is not a result about ambient horn forms.
5. **Form list supported with the stated horizontal qualification:** `eta` fails globally, `rho_i eta`, `A`, and `F` descend, and vertical mixed legs require vanishing on the collapsed branch.
6. **Curvature examples supported:** both bumps are smooth, dimensionally correct, equalizer-admissible where claimed, invisible to the coframe on their exterior support, and not gauge-equivalent to zero when their curvature is nonzero.
7. **Physical interpretation remains unresolved:** coframe-only omission, quotienting a retained connection by a forgetful kernel, ordinary gauge reduction, and dynamical enforcement are inequivalent operations in the available evidence.
