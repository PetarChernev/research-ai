# Verification report for C007

## Claim tested

Ledger status at the start of verification: `derived`.

> For the local quotient X=[U x (-epsilon,epsilon) x S1]/~ that identifies each full circle exactly when s<=0, the two flat-profile maps F_i=(u,s,rho_i cos(theta),rho_i sin(theta)) with rho_1=ell exp[-(L/s)^2] and rho_2=ell exp[-(L/s)^4] on s>0 and zero otherwise induce homeomorphisms onto their horn images; X has regular strata of dimensions five and four, a three-dimensional interface locally homeomorphic to R^{1,2} x C(S1 disjoint_union point), and at that interface local covering dimension five, quotient-algebra derivation dimension four, and ambient-embedding derivation dimension six.

I tested only this local topological and differential-algebraic claim. I did not assess C001, dynamics, integration, causality, or whether any of the three dimension notions should be physical.

## Independence statement

I reconstructed the fibers, quotient topology, local cone, covering dimension, and both cotangent quotients directly from the definitions. In particular, I used a quotient-map argument and the local-ring identity

\[
\mathfrak m_{A/I}/\mathfrak m_{A/I}^{2}
 \cong \mathfrak m_A/(\mathfrak m_A^2+I)
\]

rather than accepting D002's dimension table. No originating code, symbolic output, numerical data, or director summary was used.

The ledger wording, the quotient and smooth-algebra definitions, the profiles, the ambient space, and the convention for derivation tangents were necessarily shared with D002. ## Reconstruction

### 1. Exact fibers and the quotient/image map

Let

\[
M=U\times I\times S^1,\qquad
F_i(u,s,\theta)=(u,s,\rho_i(s)\cos\theta,\rho_i(s)\sin\theta).
\]

The first four displayed coordinates retain `(u,s)`. If `s>0`, then `rho_i(s)>0`, so equality of the last two coordinates is equality of the points of `S^1`. If `s<=0`, the last two coordinates are `(0,0)` for every angle. Thus the fibers of either `F_i` are exactly the declared equivalence classes, with no extra identification at positive `s` and no missing identification at `s=0`.

The image is

\[
H_i=\{(u,s,x,y):x^2+y^2=\rho_i(s)^2\}.
\]

It is closed relative to the open ambient slab `N=U x I x R^2`, hence Hausdorff and locally compact. For compact `K subset H_i`, `pr_B(K)` is compact in the open base `B=U x I`. Compactness in an open slab is not lost at its omitted boundary: such a `K` cannot accumulate at `s=+-epsilon` without a limit in `H_i`. Moreover,

\[
F_i^{-1}(K)\subset \operatorname{pr}_B(K)\times S^1
\]

is closed in the compact set on the right. Therefore `F_i` is proper. A proper continuous map from the locally compact Hausdorff `M` to the Hausdorff locally compact `H_i` is closed; since it is surjective, it is a quotient map. The fiber factorization therefore produces a continuous bijection `bar F_i:X->H_i` whose inverse is continuous. This independently recovers both homeomorphisms.

### 2. Strata and the link

For `s>0`, no angle is collapsed, so the quotient is locally

\[
U\times(0,\epsilon)\times S^1
\]

and has dimension five. For `s<0`, the circle is removed and the quotient is `U x (-epsilon,0)`, of dimension four. At `s=0`, the set of quotient points is just `U`, of dimension three.

Fix an interface point and shrink in `u`. In the normal quotient, positive `s` contributes `(0,delta) x S^1`, negative `s` contributes one copy of `(0,delta)`, and all points over normal radius zero are identified. This is exactly

\[
C_\delta(S^1\sqcup\{*\}).
\]

The needed uniform normal neighborhood exists because the inverse image of an interface neighborhood is an open saturated set containing the compact circle over `s=0`. A finite subcover gives one common positive radius for all angles. Hence a neighborhood in `X`, not the interface by itself, is homeomorphic to

\[
\mathbb R^3\times C(S^1\sqcup\{*\}).
\]

The Lorentzian notation `R^{1,2}` designates one timelike coordinate but does not alter this topological statement.

### 3. Local covering dimension

The cone has two closed branches meeting only at the tip: `C(S^1)` is a two-dimensional disk germ and `C({*})` is a one-dimensional ray germ. Their products with a three-dimensional `u`-neighborhood have dimensions five and four. The closed-sum theorem for metrizable covering dimension gives the upper bound five.

For the lower bound, every interface neighborhood contains, on its positive branch, a compact subset homeomorphic to a product of a three-cube, a positive normal interval, and an arc (or circle) in `S^1`, of covering dimension five. Monotonicity for closed subspaces gives the lower bound. Thus the neighborhood germ has covering dimension five.

### 4. Quotient-smooth cotangent quotient

At an interface point take `u=0,s=0`. A quotient-smooth germ is represented on a saturated resolution neighborhood by a smooth `g(u,s,theta)` that is angle-independent for `s<=0`. Compactness of the collapsed circle permits shrinking to a product neighborhood, so angular averaging is legitimate. Set

\[
\bar g(u,s)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int g(u,s,\theta)d\theta,
\qquad h=g-\bar g.
\]

Then `h=0` for `s<=0`. Smoothness implies that `h` is flat in the normal direction at zero. Hadamard division gives `h=s k` with smooth `k`, and the extension has `k=0` for `s<=0`. Both `s` and `k` vanish at the interface germ, so `h` lies in `m_q^2`.

The ordinary first-order expansion of the base function `bar g` then gives

\[
[g-g(0)]=\sum_{\alpha=0}^2
(\partial_{u^\alpha}\bar g)_0[u^\alpha]
+(\partial_s\bar g)_0[s]
\quad\text{in }\mathfrak m_q/\mathfrak m_q^2.
\]

The four derivative functionals in the three `u` directions and the `s` direction are well-defined derivations and pair as the identity matrix with these four classes. Thus there is neither a missing base direction nor an additional first-order angular direction:

\[
\dim(\mathfrak m_q/\mathfrak m_q^2)=4,
\qquad \dim\operatorname{Der}_x(C_q^\infty,\mathbb R)=4.
\]

### 5. Ambient-restriction cotangent quotient and hidden relations

Let `A` be the local ring of ambient smooth germs at `p=(u,0,0,0)` and `I_i` the ideal of germs vanishing on the horn. The horn restriction ring is `A/I_i`. Any `Phi in I_i` obeys:

1. `Phi(u,s,0,0)=0` on the negative half-axis, so all three `u` derivatives and the `s` derivative vanish at `p`;
2. at each small positive `s`, `Phi` vanishes at both `(x,y)=(+rho_i,0)` and `(-rho_i,0)`, so the mean-value theorem and continuity give `partial_x Phi(p)=0`;
3. the analogous pair on the `y` axis gives `partial_y Phi(p)=0`.

Hence every element of `I_i` has zero one-jet. Hadamard's second-order lemma gives `I_i subset m_A^2`. It follows that

\[
\frac{\mathfrak m_{A/I_i}}{\mathfrak m_{A/I_i}^2}
\cong\frac{\mathfrak m_A}{\mathfrak m_A^2+I_i}
\cong\frac{\mathfrak m_A}{\mathfrak m_A^2},
\]

which has the six independent classes `(u^0,u^1,u^2,s,x,y)`.

This also directly searches for hidden first-order horn relations. The obvious relation `x^2+y^2-rho_i(s)^2=0` has zero differential at the tip. More generally, a putative linear relation would be the differential of an ambient vanishing germ, and the preceding axis tests force all six coefficients to zero. Therefore both ambient-restriction algebras have derivation dimension six.

## Falsification attempts

1. **Open-slab properness attack.** I tried compact sets approaching `s=+-epsilon` and `u`-slab boundaries. They are not compact in the relative horn topology unless the missing boundary limit is avoided. The compact-base-times-compact-fiber argument therefore survives.
2. **Nonclosed-image attack.** The image equation is the zero set of the continuous function `x^2+y^2-rho_i(s)^2` within `N`; no omitted limit point occurs inside `N`.
3. **Wrong-fiber attack.** Positivity of `rho_i` for every `s>0` prevents any positive-side angular collapse. Exact vanishing for every `s<=0` collapses the whole circle, including at the interface.
4. **Link attack.** Replacing the link by only `S^1` loses the negative ray; replacing it by an interval adds nonexistent normal directions. The disconnected link `S^1 sqcup point` is forced by the two punctured branches.
5. **Covering-dimension attack.** The singular stratum's dimension three and the quotient derivation dimension four do not bound neighborhood covering dimension. The positive five-manifold branch supplies a closed five-dimensional test subset in every interface neighborhood.
6. **Hidden quotient direction attack.** Angle-dependent quotient-smooth germs can be nonzero for every `s>0`, but their flat support makes them products of two maximal-ideal germs. They vanish in `m_q/m_q^2`.
7. **Hidden ambient relation attack.** Neither the defining horn equation nor any smooth consequence has a nonzero linear term. The two antipodal-axis mean-value tests rule out linear `x` or `y` relations that the topological tangent cone might misleadingly suggest.
8. **Category-confusion attack.** The six-dimensional result is for locally ambient-extendible functions, not for the final quotient algebra and not for the conically smooth tangent classifier. Changing the smooth category changes the answer and is outside the exact claim.

No numerical calculation or external theorem specific to these horns was needed. The neighboring Ayala--Francis--Tanaka artifact supports use of disconnected compact links in conical local models but does not establish any of the derivation dimensions, so it was not used as proof.

## Checks

- **Dimensions and units:** The topology and local-ring arguments need only `rho_i>0` for `s>0`, `rho_i=0` for `s<=0`, and continuity at zero. With `[ell]=[L]=[s]=L`, both profile exponents are dimensionless and `(x,y)` have length dimension.
- **Signs and normalization:** The sign convention `s>0` for the circle branch and `s<0` for the ray branch is used consistently. The `2pi` angular normalization affects averaging but not any dimension count.
- **Symmetries and conservation laws:** Circle rotations preserve each fiber and each horn. No conservation law or gauge reduction is present in C007, and none was inferred from the quotient relation.
- **Limiting or exactly soluble cases:** At fixed positive `s` the fiber is exactly `S^1`; at fixed negative `s` it is a point; at zero it is the common cone tip. These three cases reproduce dimensions five, four, and three for the strata.
- **Numerical reproducibility and convergence:** Not applicable; all checks are exact topology and local algebra. No code, tolerance, or data were used.
- **Hidden parameter dependence:** The conclusions require `U` to be an open subset of `R^3`, `I` open, `S^1` compact, and each profile to be positive on the entire positive half-slab. The derivation dimensions depend essentially on the declared quotient and ambient-restriction algebras.

## Findings

1. **Wording requiring correction:** the ledger sentence can grammatically say that the three-dimensional interface itself is locally homeomorphic to `R^{1,2} x C(S^1 sqcup point)`. That is false dimensionally. The correct statement, proved in D002, is that **a neighborhood of the interface in `X`** has that local model; the interface is locally `R^{1,2}`.
2. **Category restriction:** dimensions `4` and `6` are not competing computations in one canonical tangent theory. They belong to two explicitly different local smooth algebras. Neither result verifies a physical tangent notion.
3. **No hidden first-order horn relation was found:** the ambient vanishing ideal is contained in the square of the ambient maximal ideal, while every quotient angular remainder lies in the square of the quotient maximal ideal.
4. **The topological argument survives the open-slab issue:** properness and closedness do not require compactifying `U` or `I`.
5. **The numerical values in the claim reconstruct successfully:** regular strata `(5,4)`, interface stratum `3`, local covering dimension `5`, quotient derivations `4`, and ambient-restriction derivations `6`.
