## Reconstruction

### 1. Based mapping-space components

Fix basepoints `x_infinity in S^3` and `n_infinity in S^2`, and let

\[
\mathcal M_* = \operatorname{Map}_*((S^3,x_\infty),(S^2,n_\infty))
\]

with the compactly generated compact-open topology. Its components are indexed by

\[
\pi_0(\mathcal M_*)=[S^3,S^2]_* = \pi_3(S^2)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

After fixing orientations and normalizing the Hopf map to charge `+1`, write the charge-`Q` component as `mathcal M_Q` and choose an actual map `f_Q` as the basepoint for its fundamental group. Since `Map_*(S^3,S^2)` is the threefold loop space `Omega^3 S^2`, its components are homotopy equivalent by loop multiplication with representatives of `Q` and `-Q`. Therefore

\[
\pi_1(\mathcal M_Q,f_Q)\cong \pi_1(\Omega^3S^2)_0
\cong \pi_4(S^2)\cong\mathbb Z_2
\]

for every integer `Q`.

The qualification is material: `pi_1` is based at a *map* `f_Q`, not merely at `n_infinity`; equivalences between distinct charge components require a choice. Here the resulting abstract group has no nontrivial automorphism, but there is still no canonical identification of configurations across components. This conclusion applies directly to continuous based maps in the stated topology. Smooth maps have the same weak homotopy type by approximation, but an energy-completed Sobolev space, a singular-map completion, or a gauge quotient requires a separate theorem. Auckly--Speight explicitly use compact-open topology and flag the Sobolev issue in section 2; their Theorem 6 specializes to `M=S^3` as `Z_2` in every based component.

### 2. Hopf lifts: existence, ambiguity, and degree

Let `p:S^3 -> S^2` be the Hopf fibration `S^1 -> S^3 -> S^2`. For any `f:S^3 -> S^2`, the pullback circle bundle has first Chern class

\[
c_1(f^*S^3)=f^*c_1(S^3\mathbin{\to}S^2)\in H^2(S^3;\mathbb Z)=0.
\]

Hence it is trivial and `f` has a lift `g:S^3 -> S^3` with `p o g=f`. A constant fibre multiplication makes the lift based at any selected point over `n_infinity`.

The lift is not unique. If `g` and `g'` are two lifts, there is a unique map `u:S^3 -> S^1` such that `g'=g u` (for the chosen right circle action). For based lifts, `u(x_infinity)=1`. Since `pi_3(S^1)=0`, `u` is based-null-homotopic, so all based lifts are homotopic through lifts and have the same degree. The set of actual lifts is nevertheless an infinite-dimensional torsor for `Map_*(S^3,S^1)`, not a canonically selected constant `U(1)` phase.

The homotopy exact sequence of the Hopf fibration gives isomorphisms

\[
p_*:\pi_3(S^3)\xrightarrow{\sim}\pi_3(S^2),\qquad
p_*:\pi_4(S^3)\xrightarrow{\sim}\pi_4(S^2).
\]

With `H(p)=+1`, it follows that

\[
Q(f)=H(p\circ g)=\deg(g).
\]

Reversing the orientation used for either invariant changes the displayed sign, but not any parity conclusion. Equivalently, composition with `p` gives a componentwise mapping-space fibration. Its fibre is `Map_*(S^3,S^1)`, which is contractible because every based map has a unique based lift to the convex universal cover `R`. Thus the Hopf projection induces an isomorphism on `pi_1` in each corresponding degree/charge component. Krusch--Speight section 2, Theorems 1--2 and Corollary 3, gives this mapping-space result, and the paragraph following Theorem 2 fixes the same `H(p)=+1` convention and identifies lift degree with Hopf charge.

This proves existence and homotopy uniqueness of lifts, but not a canonical physical fibre collective coordinate. That distinction is the boundary relevant to, but not a decision on, C005.

### 3. Class of a pure `2 pi` spatial rotation

Let `rho_t`, `0 <= t <= 1`, be the nontrivial loop in `SO(3)` rotating physical `R^3` through `2 pi`, extended to its one-point compactification and fixing `x_infinity`. For an internal-scalar Hopf field, the pure spatial-rotation loop is

\[
\Gamma_f(t)(x)=f(\rho_t^{-1}x).
\]

For a lift `f=p o g`, this loop lifts to `g o rho_t^{-1}`. For the identity degree-one self-map of `S^3`, the corresponding loop represents the unstable `J` image

\[
J:\pi_1(SO(3))\longrightarrow\pi_4(S^3),
\]

which is the isomorphism `Z_2 -> Z_2`. Postcomposition by a degree-`Q` map acts as multiplication by `Q` on this `Z_2`, and Hopf projection is an isomorphism on `pi_4`. Consequently

\[
[\Gamma_f]=(Q\bmod 2)\,\eta_4\quad\text{in}\quad
\pi_1(\mathcal M_Q)\cong\mathbb Z_2.
\]

Thus a *pure precomposition* `2 pi` rotation is nontrivial exactly for odd `Q`; at `Q=1` it is the order-two generator. Krusch--Speight equation (4.5),

\[
N=\frac{Q}{2\pi}(Q\alpha-\beta),
\]

gives `N=Q^2` for `(alpha,beta)=(2 pi,0)`, the same parity because `Q^2=Q mod 2`.

There is an apparent sentence-level typo in that paper on arXiv v2, section 4, page 8: it literally says the lifted rotation is *contractible* iff `Q` is odd. This conflicts with the question posed immediately before it, the conclusion immediately after it, and equation (4.5) on page 10. The consistent result is noncontractible iff `Q` is odd; I did not use the erroneous sentence.

### 4. Exchange versus one-lump rotation in the restricted charge-two sector

There is a precise restricted statement. Choose two identical charge-one maps localized in disjoint balls inside a coordinate ball, constant outside those balls. Their lifts can be multiplied pointwise to make a degree-two lift, and then projected by `p`. Define:

1. `R`: hold one localized unit fixed and apply the pure-domain `2 pi` rotation above to the other;
2. `E`: interchange the two supports around a contractible path, transporting a chosen local framing without an added twist.

Because the total field has no particle labels, the interchange closes. In the full fixed-total-charge component `mathcal M_2`, Pontryagin--Thom framed cobordism changes `E` into `R`. Right multiplication by the static degree-one lift is a homeomorphism between degree-one and degree-two `S^3`-valued mapping-space components, so `R` remains the generator; Hopf projection preserves it. Hence

\[
[E]=[R]\ne 0\quad\text{in}\quad\pi_1(\mathcal M_2)\cong\mathbb Z_2.
\]

Auckly--Speight section 5, Figure 6 and its following paragraphs, constructs the framed cobordism between exchange and rotation for two unit Skyrmions; the Hopfion paragraph later in section 5 transfers the charge-zero exchange generator to a charge-two Hopfion exchange generator. Their section 4.2, Figure 1, also warns that two visually plausible frame-transport conventions for exchange have different homotopy classes.

The equality therefore requires all of the following:

- identical, physically unlabelled units (for labelled units, exchange is not a loop);
- a specified no-twist transport of the soliton/Hopf framing;
- local exchange around a contractible path, or additional spin-structure data for nonlocal exchange;
- the internal-scalar, pure-precomposition definition of spatial rotation;
- a configuration space large enough to contain the relevant fixed-total-charge field homotopy, including merged/non-resolved lumps and the Pontryagin--Thom cobordism;
- a quotient and gauge action that do not identify or remove the generator.

It is not a theorem about every space informally called a “two-defect space.”

### 5. What functoriality does and does not give for C004

Suppose an actual inclusion were defined,

\[
i_Q:\mathcal M_Q\longrightarrow\mathcal C_Q,
\]

where `mathcal C_Q` is the physical generalized-geometric sector. If the physical loops are exactly the images of the restricted loops, then functoriality preserves their equality:

\[
i_{2*}[E]=i_{2*}[R].
\]

Functoriality does **not** preserve nontriviality. The homomorphism from `Z_2` can be either injective or zero. Charge conservation labels path components (`pi_0`); it does not constrain the kernel on `pi_1`. A continuous retraction `r_Q:mathcal C_Q -> mathcal M_Q` with `r_Q o i_Q` homotopic to the identity would suffice for injectivity, as would an extended mod-two loop invariant detecting the generator. No such map or invariant is defined in the supplied artifacts.

## Falsification attempts

1. **Rotation-convention counterexample.** If the target `S^2` is geometric rather than an internal scalar, an active physical rotation about an axis fixing `n_infinity` may act on both domain and target. A simultaneous target `2 pi` rotation contributes the other odd class. In equation (4.5), `(alpha,beta)=(2 pi,2 pi)` gives `N=Q(Q-1)` (and the opposite sign convention gives `Q(Q+1)`), always even. In particular, the diagonal geometric `2 pi` loop at `Q=1` is contractible although pure precomposition is not. C004 does not define which action its “physical rotation” means.

2. **Rigid two-particle countermodel.** In the unlabelled rigid framed-particle space `(F_2(R^3) x SO(3)^2)/S_2`, exchange and individual frame rotation are distinct: the monodromy homomorphism of the labelled covering sends exchange to the transposition and rotation to the identity. In the labelled cover, exchange is not closed. The equality appears only after enlarging to a field space whose homotopies impose the framed-cobordism relation. Core overlap alone does not specify that enlargement.

3. **Explicit enlarged-space contraction.** Include `S^2=partial D^3` and enlarge to

   \[
   \mathcal Y=\operatorname{Map}_*((S^3,x_\infty),(D^3,n_\infty)).
   \]

   If `gamma(t)` is any loop in `mathcal M_Q` based at `f_Q`, then

   \[
   H(r,t,x)=(1-r)\gamma(t,x)+r f_Q(x)
   \]

   is a based contraction in `mathcal Y`. It fixes `x_infinity` and the loop basepoint. This models an allowed history in which a normalized Hopf direction can lose magnitude or become undefined at rank loss. Hopf charge is not defined during this contraction, so whether it is admissible is exactly a missing rule, not something that may be assumed away.

4. **Charge preservation is insufficient.** Even if sectors remain labelled by `Q`, attaching a two-cell to each `mathcal M_Q` along its generator leaves `pi_0` and the charge label intact while killing `pi_1`. This abstract counterexample shows that “Hopf charge remains meaningful” must be strengthened to an explicit topological control condition.

5. **Gauge and quotient attack.** A local frame-gauge orbit can contain the rotation loop, in which case passage to the orbit space kills it. A homotopy quotient can instead add fundamental-group classes. Likewise, permutation quotienting is needed to close exchange but can change `pi_1`. The gauge group, stabilizers, and quotient construction are absent.

6. **Regularity/energy attack.** The cited theorems concern all continuous maps with compact-open topology. Restricting to a bounded-energy sublevel, only stable solutions, exactly two identifiable cores, or a rank-stratified completion need not preserve the computed homotopy. No deformation-retraction or density theorem is supplied for the intended physical space.

No conflict was found between the two primary sources in their common restricted category, apart from the explicit rotation sentence typo noted above. Neither source treats dimensional rank change or the proposed gravitational configuration space.

Conservation of `Q` controls components, not injectivity on fundamental groups.

## Findings

1. **Central failure of definition:** no admissible one- or two-defect configuration space, topology, inclusion map, rank-changing history class, gauge quotient, or physical rotation action is defined in the supplied artifacts. The first and decisive clause of C004 therefore cannot be evaluated.
2. **Nontriviality is not inherited by enlargement:** an explicit convex-target enlargement contracts the loop, and even preservation of charge sectors does not force injectivity.
3. **“Physical rotation” is convention-sensitive:** pure precomposition is nontrivial at `Q=1`, while a natural diagonal geometric rotation is trivial.
4. **Exchange equality is conditional:** it holds in the all-based-map charge-two sector for identical unlabelled framed textures with the specified local no-twist exchange, but fails as a general statement about rigid or labelled two-defect spaces.
5. **Strongest restricted-space result:** in `Map_*^Q(S^3,S^2)` with compact-open topology, every component has `pi_1=Z_2`; every map lifts through the Hopf fibration, all based lifts are homotopic, and lift degree equals Hopf charge up to the declared orientation sign; a pure spatial `2 pi` rotation has class `Q mod 2`; and the standard local exchange of two unit textures equals the nontrivial one-unit rotation class in the full charge-two mapping component.

The topology/quantization boundary is sharp: even if C004's common nonzero `Z_2` class survives, topology only supplies possible characters of that class. Whether odd fibre momentum or microscopic dynamics selects a `-1` character, and any claim about the lowest `j`, belongs to C005 and was not tested or decided here.

## Outcome

`inconclusive`.

The restricted Hopf-map theorem survives independent reconstruction and serious convention checks, but it proves neither injectivity into the proposed physical space nor survival under arbitrary allowed rank-changing histories. Plausible enlargements support either outcome, and one natural interpretation of physical rotation changes the `Q=1` class. Because the full spaces and actions are undefined, assigning either verification or contradiction would fill the principal gap by assumption.
