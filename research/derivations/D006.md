# D006: Presymplectic rank and charge obstruction in the fixed-interface edge sector

## Target claim

This artifact performs exactly one bounded subproblem: the `2+1` split of the
**interface/edge action only** in the basic persistent `SO(1,3)` sector of
`D005`.  It compares

1. **A:** a fixed reference connection `a` which transforms geometrically as
   background data; and
2. **B:** a gauge-orbit reference
   `a=u a_0u^{-1}-du u^{-1}` with fixed seed `a_0`.

The target background is the planar rank-three branch

\[
 b=2\pi\gamma=\kappa _4^{-1},\qquad H(K)=0. \tag{1}
\]

The requested discriminator is whether the added sector is already shown by
the available action to be regular, zero-charge, first-class gauge (supporting
the surviving form of H002), or is necessarily a physical edge pair/residual
second-class sector (supporting restricted H003).

**Sharp result.** The interface action determines its own cut potential and
rank, but does **not** determine either advertised final Dirac classification.
For A its on-shell edge potential is zero, but a geometrically transforming
fixed reference is a covariance spurion, not a canonical variable; whether
transformations changing it are gauge is undeclared. For B the cut form has an
exact rank-ten residual block (five canonical pairs per spatial point) and a
generically nonzero temporal-corner moment map proportional to `Q`. Whether
the two bulk Lorentz Gauss systems remove that block cannot be decided without
the omitted bulk symplectic form and Gauss generators. Thus the outcome is
**(iii) inconclusive from D005**, with a nontrivial warning in favor of
restricted H003 but not a proof of (ii).

No bulk Hamiltonian, moving-interface term, full `SO(1,4)` sector,
hyperbolicity calculation, or F0-C claim is attempted.

## Assumptions

### Physical and geometric assumptions

1. `Sigma=I_t x C` is a fixed timelike interface.  `C` is an oriented spatial
   two-manifold with local coordinates `(x^1,x^2)`.  Its possible boundary is
   `partial C`; the initial and final temporal cuts are `C_i,C_f`.
2. The marked circle has already been integrated with ordinary normalization,
   so `b=2 pi gamma`.  All fields are basic and theta-independent.  The
   vertical period and normalization are fixed.  Mixed `SO(1,4)` and nonbasic
   modes are absent.
3. The only action split is the interface part of D005 (24), (30):

   \[
   S_I=\int_\Sigma\left[
      K^{ab}\wedge Q_{ab}
      +\mu_a\wedge\Delta e^a
      +\lambda_{ab}\wedge\Delta\omega^{ab}\right]. \tag{2}
   \]

   Here the first equality uses
   `K wedge Q=(b/4) epsilon K e_+ e_+`.  The bulk actions and, in particular,
   their canonical kinetic and Gauss terms are not split.
4. Nevertheless, when evaluating the declared branch, the algebraic boundary
   equations of the **combined** variational problem from D005 (39) are used:

   \[
   \Delta e=\Delta\omega=0,\quad
   \lambda=-Q,\quad \mu=0,\quad Q=g\cdot P^-,\quad H(K)=0. \tag{3}
   \]

   This distinction is essential: `S_I` by itself, with both connection traces
   freely varied, would set both `Q+lambda=0` and `lambda=0`, hence `Q=0`, and
   has no rank-three stationary background.  Equation (3) exists only because
   the omitted exterior bulk variation supplies `P^-`.
5. Coframes and connection traces remain independent first-order variables.
   No torsion equation, inverse coframe, time gauge, or bulk constraint is
   imported.

### Temporal and corner boundary conditions

6. For deriving Euler equations, variations vanish on `C_i,C_f`.  Two spatial
   choices are compared:
   - **compact-support gauge:** parameters and variations vanish near
     `R x partial C`;
   - **nonvanishing-corner gauge:** parameters may be nonzero there and on a
     temporal cut, and the displayed cut moment maps are retained.
7. No boundary action on `R x partial C`, corner polarization, fixed value of
   `g` or `u`, seed-frame gauge group, or reference stabilizer is silently
   chosen.  Their absence is part of the obstruction.
8. Small Lorentz transformations are considered. Large gauge sectors and
   global bundle obstructions are outside scope.

### Conventions

9. `epsilon_0123=+1`, the metric is `diag(-,+,+,+)`, antisymmetric pairs are
   ordered as in D005, and

   \[
   Q_{ab}={b\over4}\epsilon_{abcd}e_+^c\wedge e_+^d. \tag{4}
   \]

10. Field-space exterior differentiation is denoted `delta`. Right Maurer--
    Cartan forms obey

    \[
    \xi=\delta g g^{-1},\quad \zeta=\delta u u^{-1},\quad
    \delta\xi={1\over2}[\xi\wedge\xi],\quad
    \delta\zeta={1\over2}[\zeta\wedge\zeta]. \tag{5}
    \]

11. The presymplectic convention is `Omega_C=delta Theta_C`. Signs therefore
    follow D005 (20), (32), (58)-(59). Reversing this convention reverses
    `Omega_C` and all Hamiltonians together, but changes no rank or null space.

## Derivation

### 1. Exact `2+1` decomposition of the multiplier terms

Write any one-form `A=A_tdt+A_A dx^A` and any two-form

\[
X={1\over2}X_{AB}dx^A\wedge dx^B+dt\wedge X_{tA}dx^A. \tag{11}
\]

Since

\[
\Delta\omega=\omega_+-g\varpi g^{-1}+dg g^{-1}, \tag{12}
\]

the only time derivative in `lambda wedge Delta omega` is

\[
\left(\lambda\wedge\Delta\omega\right)_{dt12}
=\left\langle\lambda_C,\dot g g^{-1}\right\rangle
+\hbox{terms without }\dot g. \tag{13}
\]

Likewise, in A,

\[
\left(K\wedge Q\right)_{dt12}
=\left\langle Q_C,\dot g g^{-1}\right\rangle
+\hbox{terms without }\dot g, \tag{14}
\]

because the fixed `a` has no field-space velocity.  `mu wedge Delta e`
contains no derivative of any interface field.  The mixed components
`lambda_{tA}` and `mu_{tA}` are algebraic multipliers; only `lambda_C` enters
the edge momentum.

For B, `delta a=-D_a zeta`, equivalently the time derivative of `u` contributes

\[
\left(K\wedge Q\right)_{dt12}
=\langle Q_C,\dot g g^{-1}+g\dot u u^{-1}g^{-1}\rangle
+\hbox{nonvelocity terms}. \tag{15}
\]

Equations (13)-(15) are exact; there is no expansion about (10).

### 2. Pulled-back presymplectic potentials

D005 (32), (58)-(59), restricted to a temporal cut, gives immediately

\[
\boxed{\Theta_C^{A}=\int_C\langle\Pi_C,\xi\rangle .} \tag{16}
\]

For B one obtains

\[
\boxed{\Theta_C^{B}=\int_C\left[
 \langle\Pi_C,\xi\rangle+\langle Q_C,Z\rangle\right].} \tag{17}
\]

Using (9), this can also be written

\[
\boxed{\Theta_C^{B}=\int_C\left[
 \langle\lambda_C,\delta g g^{-1}\rangle
 +
 \langle Q_C,\delta v v^{-1}\rangle\right].} \tag{18}
\]

This change of variables is locally invertible: `(g,u)<->(g,v)`. It cleanly
separates the two first-order blocks.

### 3. Exact two-forms

For a right-trivialized group variable with potential
`int <p,delta h h^-1>`, (5) gives

\[
\Omega_{(h,p)}(\delta_1,\delta_2)=\int_C\big[
 \langle\delta_1p,\chi_2\rangle-
 \langle\delta_2p,\chi_1\rangle+
 \langle p,[\chi_1,\chi_2]\rangle\big], \tag{19}
\]

where `chi_i=delta_i h h^-1`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\Omega_C^A(1,2)=\int_C\big[
 \langle\delta_1\Pi,\xi_2\rangle-
 \langle\delta_2\Pi,\xi_1\rangle+
 \langle\Pi,[\xi_1,\xi_2]\rangle\big]_C.} \tag{20}
\]

In `(g,v)` coordinates,

\[
\boxed{\Omega_C^B=\Omega_{(g,\lambda)}+\Omega_{(v,Q(e_+))}.} \tag{21}
\]

This equality includes the Maurer--Cartan commutator terms and is not an
abelianized approximation.

Neither `mu` nor any connection coefficient has an interface kinetic term.
They are null directions of the interface two-form before constraints.

### 4. Pointwise rank on the planar background

On (10), pullback of (4) gives

\[
Q_{03,C}={b\over2}\,dx^1\wedge dx^2,
\qquad Q_{ab,C}=0\quad(ab\ne03), \tag{22}
\]

where the factor `1/2` follows from the two ordered placements `(c,d)=(1,2)`
and `(2,1)`. Since `b=1/kappa_4` and the triad is nondegenerate, `Q_C` is
nonzero.

At one spatial point, the map from the eight spatial coframe components to six
bivector components,

\[
dQ_C:\ \delta e_A^a\longmapsto
{b\over2}\epsilon_{abcd}\delta e_C^c\wedge e_C^d, \tag{23}
\]

has rank five.  A direct basis argument is as follows. Before the internal
Hodge map, `e^1 wedge e^2` varies into

\[
12,\ 02,\ 32,\ 10,\ 13, \tag{24}
\]

and not into `03`; these five bivectors are independent. The four-dimensional
internal Hodge map is invertible, so (23) also has rank five. Equivalently, the
linearized decomposable-bivector (Pluecker) condition removes one of six
directions. In the `Q` basis the absent variation is `delta Q_12`.

Consequently:

* Off shell, `delta lambda_C` makes `delta Pi_C` arbitrary. Thus
  \[
  \operatorname{rank}\Omega_C^A=12 \tag{25}
  \]
  per spatial point in the `(g,Pi)` block. Variations of `e` compensated by
  `delta lambda=-delta Q`, all `mu` variations, and all connection variations
  are null directions.
* In B, the `(g,lambda)` block has rank 12. The restricted
  `(v,Q(e))` block has rank 10. To see the latter without genericity
  assumptions, its matrix has the form
  \[
  \begin{pmatrix}A_Q&-dQ\\ (dQ)^T&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
  (A_Q)_{IJ}=\langle Q,[T_I,T_J]\rangle. \tag{26}
  \]
  `dQ` has rank five. Its one-dimensional left kernel is generated at (22) by
  `T_12`; moreover `[T_12,T_03]=0`, so `A_Q T_12=0`. Hence (26) has four null
  directions (the `T_12` direction and the three-dimensional kernel of `dQ`)
  in a fourteen-dimensional `(v,e_A^a)` space, and
  \[
  \boxed{\operatorname{rank}\Omega_C^B=12+10=22} \tag{27}
  \]
  before imposing (3).

Now pull back to the combined branch (3). In A, `Pi=0` and tangent variations
obey `delta Pi=0`, so

\[
\boxed{\iota_{(3)}^*\Theta_C^A=0,
\qquad \iota_{(3)}^*\Omega_C^A=0.} \tag{28}
\]

In B, `Pi=0` and `lambda=-Q` cancel only the `g` block. Equations (17)-(18)
give

\[
\boxed{\iota_{(3)}^*\Theta_C^B=\int_C\langle Q_C,Z\rangle,
\qquad \operatorname{rank}\iota_{(3)}^*\Omega_C^B=10} \tag{29}
\]

on the `(u;g,e_A)` variables through the rank-five momentum map
`Ad_(g^-1)Q(e)` (or after a local frame choice, on `(u,e_A)`), before any bulk
Gauss reduction. Thus B has five nonzero presymplectic pairs on the
declared planar cut. `H(K)=0` changes neither (22) nor this rank: it constrains
the connection difference, which has no interface kinetic term here.

### 5. Primary constraints

Introduce right-trivialized momentum densities `p_g,p_u` and ordinary momenta
for all remaining interface fields.  The exact primary constraints generated
by the interface Lagrangian are, for A,

\[
\boxed{\Phi_g:=p_g-\Pi_C\simeq0,\quad
p_{e_+}\simeq p_{e_-}\simeq p_{\omega_+}\simeq p_\varpi\simeq
p_\lambda\simeq p_\mu\simeq0.} \tag{30}
\]

For B there is in addition

\[
\boxed{\Phi_u:=p_u-\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}Q_C\simeq0.} \tag{31}
\]

The mixed temporal components of `lambda` and `mu` also have zero momenta and
impose the spatial matching equations. The spatial components `lambda_C`
serve as `g` momentum through (30).

The elementary primary matrix already reproduces the presymplectic ranks:
`p_lambda` and `Phi_g` form a nondegenerate rank-12 defining block. In B, after
that standard first-order block is eliminated, `p_e` and `Phi_u` have rank ten
through (23), leaving the four null directions described below (26). These are
primary second-class **defining constraints** of the first-order
parameterization; their existence alone is not a physical obstruction. A
Dirac verdict requires secondary constraints and the total Gauss generators.

The latter cannot be obtained from `S_I` consistently on the desired
background. Varying `omega_+` in `S_I` gives `Pi=0`, while varying `varpi` in
`S_I` gives `g^{-1}lambda g=0`; together they imply `Q=0`, contradicting (22).
The bulk exterior momentum changes the second equation to
`P^-+g^{-1}lambda g=0`, which is precisely the omitted ingredient allowing
(3). Therefore stopping at the interface primary matrix and declaring its
nulls first class would be mathematically invalid.

### 6. Lorentz moment maps

Let infinitesimal independent frame transformations be `alpha_+` and
`alpha_-`, with

\[
\delta_\alpha g g^{-1}=\alpha_+-\operatorname{Ad}_g\alpha_- . \tag{32}
\]

For A, contraction of (16) gives the cut moment maps

\[
\boxed{J_+^A=\Pi_C,\qquad
J_-^A=-\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\Pi_C.} \tag{33}
\]

They vanish on (3). This is necessary for zero-charge gauge, but not
sufficient: a transformation changing fixed `a` maps one background problem
to another. In one fixed-reference phase space only the stabilizer
`D_a alpha_-=0` (with the imposed reference boundary conditions) is an actual
symmetry unless `a` is promoted or the family is quotiented by declaration.

For B, `u -> h_-u` gives `zeta=alpha_-`, hence `Z=Ad_g alpha_-`. Contracting
(17) yields

\[
\boxed{J_+^B=\Pi_C,\qquad
J_-^B=-\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\lambda_C.} \tag{34}
\]

On (3),

\[
J_+^B=0,\qquad J_-^B=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}Q_C\ne0. \tag{35}

The omitted exterior bulk contribution is exactly what is needed to combine
the second expression with `P^-` into the full exterior Gauss/boundary moment
map. Without that contribution one cannot decide whether (35) is a charge or
one term in a first-class constraint.

The `u`-orbit variation itself gives the exact equation from D005 (58),

\[
D_{\bar a}Q=0, \tag{36}
\]

and temporal-cut potential `int_C<Q,Z>`. In `2+1` language (36) contains the
time derivative of `Q_C`; it is an evolution equation for the first-order
pair, not an algebraic cut constraint that makes (29) vanish.

### 7. Compact support and corner charges

For parameters and variations compactly supported away from every component of
`partial Sigma`, D005 (58)-(59) contributes no boundary integral. The local
Euler equations (3), (36) follow, and there is no **corner** charge by the
chosen support condition. This does not make a nonzero cut two-form gauge-null.

If transformations are allowed on a temporal corner `C`, their exact
Hamiltonian candidates obtained by contracting (16)-(17) are

\[
{\cal Q}_A[\alpha_+,\alpha_-]
=\int_C\langle\Pi_C,
\alpha_+-\operatorname{Ad}_g\alpha_-\rangle, \tag{37}
\]

\[
{\cal Q}_B[\alpha_+,\alpha_-]
=\int_C\left[
\langle\Pi_C,\alpha_+\rangle
-\langle\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\lambda_C,\alpha_-\rangle
\right]. \tag{38}
\]

On (3), (37) vanishes, while

\[
\boxed{{\cal Q}_B[0,\alpha_-]
=\int_C\langle\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}Q_C,\alpha_-\rangle} \tag{39}
\]

is generically nonzero. The independent orbit displacement has the same
candidate with `alpha_-` replaced by `zeta`. For the planar triad, choosing a
nonzero `03` component paired with (22) gives an explicit nonzero value.

If `C` has a spatial boundary, converting a full Gauss generator to a
`partial C` charge requires the omitted bulk/interface Gauss density and a
choice of lateral boundary term. Equations (37)-(39) therefore establish
nonzero **temporal-corner** moment maps, but do not invent a one-dimensional
`partial C` charge. Fixing `u` or its parameter at the corner kills (39) by a
boundary condition; adding a counterterm can change the polarization. Neither
choice appears in D005.

### 8. Decision against H002 and restricted H003

The three requested outcomes can now be tested precisely.

1. **Outcome (i) is not established.** A has zero pulled-back edge form (28),
   but arbitrary Lorentz transformations are not gauge transformations of one
   fixed-reference problem unless the transformation of `a` is included in a
   declared phase space/equivalence relation. B fails the zero-charge test
   before a bulk Gauss cancellation: (29) and (39) are nonzero.
2. **Outcome (ii) is not established as unavoidable.** B exposes five edge
   pairs and a nonzero candidate charge, but the omitted core/exterior Gauss
   constraints may in principle be first class and remove them. Conversely,
   the primary second-class blocks in (30)-(31) are the ordinary defining
   constraints of a first-order action and are not by themselves a fatal
   second-class remnant.
3. **Therefore outcome (iii) holds.** The minimum missing datum is the
   **total Lorentz generator and boundary polarization on a cut**: explicitly,
   the pullback of the core and exterior bulk symplectic potentials, their
   Gauss constraints and boundary terms, together with (a) the admissible
   reference-changing transformations in A, or (b) the seed-frame gauge action
   and temporal/lateral corner condition for `u` in B. Only then can one take
   the rank of the total constraint matrix and quotient its null directions.

This is not a request for the full bulk Hamiltonian or F0-C analysis. It is the
minimum adjacent datum logically required to classify the already displayed
edge block.

## Dimensional check

With `[e]=L`, `[b]=L^-2`, and connections, `g,u` dimensionless,

\[
[Q]=[\lambda]=[\Pi]=1,\qquad [\mu]=L^{-1}. \tag{40}
\]

Thus (16)-(18) and (37)-(39) are dimensionless, as a classical action
potential or generator must be. `Omega=delta Theta` has the same physical
dimension. The relation `b=kappa_4^{-1}` is consistent because
`[kappa_4]=L^2`. No hidden `2 pi` occurs: ordinary circle integration was
absorbed only in `b=2 pi gamma`.

## Limiting-case checks

1. **Zero triad area.** If `Q_C=0`, B's residual block and charge vanish. This
   is exactly the degenerate rank-at-most-one branch excluded by the target.
2. **No Stueckelberg field.** Removing `u` reduces (17) to (16), and the rank
   drops by ten on the shell, as expected.
3. **Fixed `u` at temporal corners only.** This removes the endpoint variation
   but not the interior cut rank (29); boundary fixing is not a gauge proof.
4. **`K=0`.** This solves `H(K)=0` but changes neither `Q` nor any cut formula.
5. **Nonzero kernel `K`.** D005's six-component kernel likewise leaves the
   rank unchanged, confirming that no hidden flatness assumption entered.
6. **Orientation reversal.** Reversing `C` or the marked circle reverses
   `Theta`, `Omega`, and charges together. Ranks and null directions are
   unchanged; matching positive `kappa_4` requires the corresponding sign
   change of `b`.
7. **Gauge covariance.** Equations (16)-(18) pair coadjoint and adjoint
   quantities. The rank is invariant under conjugation. The failure in A is
   not algebraic noncovariance but the distinction between covariance of a
   family and gauge redundancy in one fixed-background phase space.
8. **Interface action alone.** Its forced `Q=0` contradiction is a direct
   control showing why the combined branch cannot be canonically classified
   after deleting all bulk information.

## Unresolved concerns

- The full moment maps may acquire bulk pieces which cancel (35) and change
  every secondary-constraint bracket. Their omission is conclusion-critical,
  not a technical inconvenience.
- A different corner counterterm changes `Theta_C` by a field-space exact term
  and can change which transformations are Hamiltonian under chosen boundary
  conditions, though it cannot erase the need to state those conditions.
- The seed-frame right action on `u` and allowed transformations of `a_0` are
  absent from D005. Different choices can turn a relative frame into gauge or
  leave it physical.
- Special backgrounds with additional Lorentz stabilizers can lower rank. The
  planar rank-three branch used here already has the stated `T_12` null; no
  generic-background extrapolation is made.
- Calling the rank-ten B block a physical edge sector before total Gauss
  reduction would overstate the evidence. Calling it pure gauge because the
  Lagrangian is covariant would make the opposite error.

## Conclusion

**Exact derived interface-sector result.** On a spatial interface cut, the
fixed-reference and Stueckelberg potentials are (16) and (17). The fixed case
has off-shell rank 12 and pulls back to zero on `Pi=0`. The Stueckelberg case
contains an additional exact block `Omega_(v,Q(e))`; on the planar rank-three
background `rank(dQ)=5`, so this block has rank 10 and remains rank 10 after
the combined algebraic shell (3) is imposed. Its nonvanishing temporal-corner
moment map is (39). `H(K)=0` does not alter these results.

**Classification.** The available action does not decide first class versus
physical/second class. A is only covariant as a family with transforming fixed
background data, so zero pullback does not by itself establish gauge. B has
five presymplectic pairs and a nonzero candidate corner charge, but their fate
depends on bulk Lorentz Gauss terms deliberately omitted from this subproblem.
Moreover, the interface action alone has no rank-three stationary branch:
without exterior `P^-` it forces `Q=0`.

\[
\boxed{\text{Outcome (iii): inconclusive from D005; minimum next datum is the
total cut Lorentz generator/boundary polarization plus declared reference or
seed/corner gauge data.}} \tag{41}
\]

This neither supports H002's required regular zero-charge reduction nor proves
restricted H003's unavoidable physical edge pair. It identifies the precise
