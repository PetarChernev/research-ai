# D020: Inverse-source endpoint regularity

## Question addressed

Does the D019 common-warp geometry with either prescribed D002 smooth-flat
positive circle profile admit a bounded, locally integrable,
null-energy-condition-respecting effective source near the exact-collapse
endpoint?

## Branch charter

Using only the frozen D002/D004/D019 conventions, study
`E^i=w(s)dx^i` (`i=0,1,2`), `E^3=ds`, `E^4=rho(s)dtheta` on `s>0`, with
`kappa_5>0`, finite constant `Lambda_5`, `rho` separately equal to
`ell exp[-(L/s)^2]` and `ell exp[-(L/s)^4]`, and arbitrary positive `w` whose
extension by `w=1` for `s<=0` is `C-infinity` flat. Assume zero spin current,
so the open-core torsion-free connection in D019 applies. Define the effective
orthonormal tensor exactly by
`G_AB+Lambda_5 eta_AB=kappa_5 T_AB`. Derive every diagonal component and the
all-null NEC reduction. Decide, for every allowed `w` and each declared
profile, whether all independent orthonormal Riemann components and `T_AB` are
bounded, whether each absolute component is locally `L1` against the
five-volume `w^3 rho ds dtheta d^3x` on compact tangential cells, and whether
the NEC holds near `s=0`. Keep boundedness distinct from volume integrability
and show the exact profile asymptotics and signs. State whether these three
properties can coexist. Do not choose or reverse-engineer a matter Lagrangian,
introduce torsion/spin sources, alter the shell, relax regularity, add
anisotropy/off-diagonal fields, or claim a universal no-go. Expose finite
machine-check candidates, but stop after an auditable exact/asymptotic verdict
and its limitations.

## Distinction from sibling branches

This is the sole analytical branch in the bounded cycle. Unlike D019/C022, it
does not solve a source-free boundary-value problem: it fixes each D002 profile
and computes the effective tensor that the geometry would require. Unlike a
matter-model branch, it neither proposes fields nor infers a local covariant
matter action from that tensor. Its discriminants are pointwise orthonormal
boundedness, absolute five-volume integrability, and the all-null NEC, kept
separate. Distinct sessions would be procedural rather than independent
evidence; no sibling output was used.

## Target claim

For each exponent `nu=2,4`, set

\[
 \rho_\nu(s)=\ell\exp[-(L/s)^\nu],\qquad s>0,                 \tag{1}
\]

where `ell,L>0`. For every allowed common warp `w`, derive the exact diagonal
effective tensor and decide the following properties on some punctured
neighborhood `0<s<epsilon`:

1. **bounded curvature:** every independent orthonormal Riemann component is
   bounded;
2. **bounded stress:** every orthonormal component `T_AB` is bounded;
3. **local stress integrability:** on every compact tangential cell, each
   `|T_AB|` is `L1` against the absolute five-volume, equivalently its radial
   integral with weight `w^3 rho ds` is finite;
4. **NEC:** `T_AB k^A k^B>=0` for every orthonormal null vector `k^A`.

The desired first property below means the conjunction of bounded curvature
and bounded stress. The target is exact classical geometry plus controlled
`s downarrow 0` asymptotics; there is no perturbative expansion.

## Assumptions

1. The open-core coframe, signature, and orientation are

   \[
   E^i=w(s)dx^i\ (i=0,1,2),\quad E^3=ds,\quad
   E^4=\rho(s)d\theta,\quad
   \eta_{AB}=\operatorname{diag}(-,+,+,+,+),                 \tag{2}
   \]

   with `theta` of period `2pi`, `w,rho>0` for `s>0`, and proper-normal
   coordinate `s`. The tangential cell in `(x^0,x^1,x^2,theta)` is compact.
2. `w` extends as `1` for `s<=0`, and this extension is `C-infinity`. Thus
   `f:=w-1` and every derivative of `f` have zero one-sided jet at `s=0`.
   The two `rho_nu` are exactly (1), with their zero extensions from D002.
3. The D004 curvature convention is
   `R^{AB}=d omega^{AB}+omega^A{}_C wedge omega^{CB}`. The connection is
   independent before variation, but zero spin current and open-core
   nondegeneracy imply the D019 torsion-free connection on `s>0`. No inverse
   coframe is evaluated at `s=0`.
4. `kappa_5>0` and `Lambda_5` is any finite real constant. The effective
   covariant orthonormal tensor is a **definition** on the open core:

   \[
   \boxed{\kappa _5T_{AB}:=G_{AB}+\Lambda _5\eta_{AB}.}       \tag{3}
   \]

   It is the tensor required by this metric if an Einstein equation is to
   hold. It does **not** establish that a covariant local matter action, matter
   fields, or a well-posed source dynamics exists.
5. No matter Lagrangian, spin/torsion source, D005 alteration, distributional
   junction, finite-regularity attachment, anisotropic/off-diagonal field,
   lapse extension, or alternative action is introduced.

## Notation

\[
 A={w'\over w},\qquad B={\rho'\over\rho},\qquad
 p={w''\over w},\qquad q=AB,\qquad r={\rho''\over\rho}.       \tag{4}

\[
 F:=2p+A^2+2q+r,\qquad H:=3(A^2+q),\qquad

## Derivation

### 1. Open-core curvature

**Exact inherited relation.** The zero-spin-current connection and curvature
are, from D019 (11)--(12),

\[
 \omega^{i3}=AE^i=w'dx^i,\qquad
 \omega^{43}=BE^4=\rho'd\theta,                               \tag{6}
\]

\[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 R^{ij}&=-A^2E^{ij},& 0\leq i<j\leq2,\\
 R^{i3}&=-pE^{i3},& i&=0,1,2,\\
 R^{i4}&=-qE^{i4},& i&=0,1,2,\\
 R^{34}&=-rE^{34}.&&
 \end{aligned}}                                               \tag{7}
\]

Thus, up to the standard antisymmetries, the independent orthonormal sectional
components are `-A^2` (three planes), `-p` (three), `-q` (three), and `-r`
(one). In the convention
`R^{AB}=(1/2)R^{AB}{}_{CD}E^C wedge E^D`, the displayed coefficient is
`R^{AB}{}_{AB}`; lowering a timelike first index can change an individual sign
but not boundedness.

**Exact contraction.** D019 (14) gives

\[
 G_{ij}=F\eta_{ij},\qquad G_{33}=H,\qquad G_{44}=J,            \tag{8}
\]

with no off-diagonal components.

### 2. Every effective stress component

Substituting (8) into the exact definition (3) gives

\[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 T_{00}&=-{F+\Lambda _5\over\kappa _5},\\
 T_{11}=T_{22}&={F+\Lambda _5\over\kappa _5},\\
 T_{33}&={H+\Lambda _5\over\kappa _5}
 ={3(A^2+q)+\Lambda _5\over\kappa _5},\\
 T_{44}&={J+\Lambda _5\over\kappa _5}
 ={3(p+A^2)+\Lambda _5\over\kappa _5},\\
 T_{AB}&=0\quad(A\ne B).
 \end{aligned}}                                               \tag{9}
\]

This includes all 15 symmetric orthonormal entries: five diagonal entries and
ten exact zeros. Notice the fixed equation of state
`epsilon=-P_1=-P_2`; no source model was assumed to obtain it.

### 3. NEC for every orthonormal null vector

Let `k^A` be any nonzero orthonormal null vector. Then

\[
 (k^0)^2=(k^1)^2+(k^2)^2+(k^3)^2+(k^4)^2.                    \tag{10}
\]

Using diagonality and `epsilon+P_1=epsilon+P_2=0` exactly,

\[
 T_{AB}k^Ak^B
 ={H-F\over\kappa _5}(k^3)^2
 +{J-F\over\kappa _5}(k^4)^2.                               \tag{11}
\]

The coefficients are

\[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 H-F&=2A^2+q-2p-r,\\
 J-F&=p+2A^2-2q-r.
 \end{aligned}}                                               \tag{12}
\]

Because `(k^3)^2` and `(k^4)^2` can be chosen independently by taking null
vectors in the `0-3` and `0-4` planes, respectively, (11) proves the exact
all-null reduction

\[
 \boxed{\mathrm{NEC\ for\ every\ null\ }k
 \iff H-F\geq0\ \hbox{and}\ J-F\geq0.}                       \tag{13}
\]

Null vectors wholly in the `0-1-2` subspace saturate the NEC exactly. The
finite cosmological constant cancels from (11)--(13), as it must because
`eta_AB k^A k^B=0`.

### 4. Exact profile ratios and signs

For either `nu=2` or `nu=4`, logarithmic differentiation of (1) gives the
exact identities

\[
 \boxed{B_\nu={\rho_\nu'\over\rho_\nu}
 =\nu L^\nu s^{-\nu-1}>0,}                                   \tag{14}
\]

\[
 \boxed{r_\nu={\rho_\nu''\over\rho_\nu}
 =\nu^2L^{2\nu}s^{-2\nu-2}
 -\nu(\nu+1)L^\nu s^{-\nu-2}.}                              \tag{15}
\]

Separately,

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \nu & B_\nu & r_\nu\\ \hline
 2 & 2L^2s^{-3} & 4L^4s^{-6}-6L^2s^{-4}\\
 4 & 4L^4s^{-5} & 16L^8s^{-10}-20L^4s^{-6}.
 \end{array}                                                   \tag{16}
\]

The leading terms in (15) are positive. More explicitly,

\[
 r_\nu>0\quad\hbox{whenever}\quad
 s^\nu<{\nu\over\nu+1}L^\nu,                               \tag{17}
\]

and in either case `r_nu -> +infinity` as `s downarrow0`.

### 5. Rigorous consequences of smooth flatness of the common warp

**Flat-germ lemma.** Let `f=w-1`, extended by zero on `s<=0`. Since this
extension is `C-infinity`, every endpoint derivative of every `f^(k)` is zero.
Taylor's theorem applied to `f^(k)` therefore gives, for every pair of
nonnegative integers `k,N`,

\[
 f^{(k)}(s)=O(s^N)\qquad(s\downarrow0).                       \tag{18}
\]

Continuity and `w(0)=1` give `1/2<w<3/2` after shrinking the neighborhood.
Hence division by `w` is harmless there, and

\[
 A=O(s^N),\qquad p=O(s^N),\qquad A^2=O(s^N)                  \tag{19}
\]

for every `N`. Equation (14) is a fixed polynomial divergence, so choosing
`N` larger than `nu+1+M` in (18) yields, for every fixed `M`,

\[
 q=AB=O(s^M).                                                  \tag{20}
\]

More generally, every product of a derivative of `w-1` with any fixed power
of `B_nu` or `s^{-1}` tends to zero faster than any prescribed power. This is
the precise reason the polynomially divergent connection coefficient `B`
cannot compete with `r`; it is not an informal `0 times infinity` argument.

Combining (5), (15), and (19)--(20),

\[
 F=r_\nu+o(s^M)\ \text{for every fixed }M,\qquad
 H=o(s^M),\qquad J=o(s^M),                                   \tag{21}
\]

where the first statement means `F-r_nu` is flat. Consequently,

\[
 H-F=-r_\nu+\text{flat},\qquad
 J-F=-r_\nu+\text{flat}.                                     \tag{22}
\]

## Asymptotic verdicts

### 1. Pointwise bounded curvature and stress

From (7), (15), and (19)--(20), all curvature families except the `3-4`
section tend to zero, while

\[
 R^{34}{}_{34}=-r_\nu\longrightarrow-\infty.                 \tag{23}
\]

Thus bounded curvature fails for every allowed `w`, for both profiles.

From (9) and (21), with finite `Lambda_5` and positive `kappa_5`,

\[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 T_{00}&=-{r_\nu\over\kappa _5}+O(1)\longrightarrow-\infty,\\
 T_{11}=T_{22}&={r_\nu\over\kappa _5}+O(1)\longrightarrow+\infty,\\
 T_{33}&\longrightarrow{\Lambda _5\over\kappa _5},\\
 T_{44}&\longrightarrow{\Lambda _5\over\kappa _5}.
 \end{aligned}}                                               \tag{24}
\]

Therefore bounded stress also fails. The divergent energy density is negative
and the two persistent spatial pressures are positive. The limiting signs of
`T_33,T_44` are the sign of `Lambda_5` (or zero when `Lambda_5=0`).

### 2. Absolute local stress integrability

The absolute coframe volume is exactly

\[
 |\mathrm{vol}_5|
 =w^3\rho_\nu\,|dx^0\wedge dx^1\wedge dx^2\wedge ds\wedge d\theta|.
                                                                    \tag{25}
\]

After integrating over a compact tangential cell and the finite circle, only
the radial weight `w^3 rho_nu ds` remains. Near zero, `w^3` is bounded above
and below. By (17), `r_nu>0`, and the exact identity `rho_nu r_nu=rho_nu''`
gives

\[
 \int_0^\epsilon \rho_\nu |r_\nu|ds
 =\int_0^\epsilon\rho_\nu''ds
 =\rho_\nu'(\epsilon)-\rho_\nu'(0)=\rho_\nu'(\epsilon)<\infty. \tag{26}
\]

The endpoint derivative is zero by D002 flatness. Equations (19)--(21) show
that all remaining stress terms are bounded (indeed the warp-dependent ones
are flat), so their product with `rho_nu` is integrable. Therefore

\[
 \boxed{\int_0^\epsilon w^3\rho_\nu|T_{AB}|ds<\infty
 \quad\text{for every }A,B,}                                  \tag{27}
\]

for both profiles and every allowed `w`. This is absolute componentwise `L1`,
not cancellation between the negative `T_00` and positive pressures. It does
not imply pointwise boundedness.

### 3. NEC

Equations (17), (19)--(20), and (22) imply that, after shrinking `epsilon` if
needed,

\[
 H-F<0,\qquad J-F<0.                                          \tag{28}
\]

The null vectors proportional to `e_0+e_3` and `e_0+e_4` therefore have
strictly negative contractions. The NEC is violated in both independent
normal/circle channels throughout a sufficiently small punctured neighborhood,
although all `0-1-2` null directions continue to saturate it.

### 4. Combined decision

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
\rho & \text{bounded curvature} & \text{bounded stress}
& \text{componentwise local }L^1 & \text{NEC for all nulls}\\ \hline
\ell e^{-(L/s)^2} & \text{no} & \text{no} & \text{yes} & \text{no}\\
\ell e^{-(L/s)^4} & \text{no} & \text{no} & \text{yes} & \text{no}
\end{array}                                                     \tag{29}
\]

Every entry is uniform over the declared class of positive smooth-flat common
warps. Hence the three desired properties—bounded curvature/stress, local
integrability, and NEC—**cannot coexist near `s=0` in this frozen ansatz for
either D002 profile**. The precise qualified result is stronger than merely
“the stress is singular”: the singular stress is absolutely volume-integrable,
but pointwise unbounded and NEC-violating.

## Approximations

Equations (3)--(17) and (25)--(27) are exact. Equations (18)--(24) and (28) are
one-sided asymptotic statements obtained from exact flat-germ estimates. There
is no perturbative parameter, retained finite order, numerical fit, or
linearization. The phrases `O(1)`, `flat`, and `o(s^M)` do not discard a
conclusion-relevant term: (18) holds for arbitrary `N`, whereas the positive
divergence (15) has a fixed finite polynomial order.

## Validity regime

- The verdict holds only on the nondegenerate open core `s>0`, asymptotically
  near its one-sided endpoint, for the two exact profiles (1), every declared
  common warp, proper-normal gauge, and the torsion-free D019 connection.
- Boundedness and NEC are stated in a geometrically selected orthonormal frame.
  A regular local Lorentz transformation cannot remove the divergent sectional
  curvature or turn a negative null contraction positive. Singular boosts are
  not admissible frame changes for a bounded orthonormal-component criterion.
- Proper-normal gauge fixes the radial coordinate. A smooth
  orientation-preserving reparametrization with nonzero finite endpoint
  Jacobian preserves boundedness, `L1` integrability, and NEC. A singular
  reparametrization would alter the frozen regularity problem.
- The result does not assert a distributional tensor at `s=0`. It concerns the
  punctured core and its one-sided behavior. No junction conservation law or
  D005 source is inferred.
- The effective tensor (3) is covariant and conserved on `s>0`, but its
  existence as a tensor is not existence of a local covariant matter action.
  Conversely, failure in this ansatz does not exclude sourceful geometries with
  anisotropy, off-diagonal fields, torsion, another action, or another
  regularity/topology.

## Dimensional check

With `[s]=[L]=[ell]=[rho]=mathsf L`, dimensionless `w,theta`, and
`[kappa_5]=mathsf L^3`,

\[
 [A]=[B]=\mathsf L^{-1},\qquad
 [p]=[q]=[r]=[\Lambda _5]=\mathsf L^{-2}.                     \tag{30}
\]

Thus every term in (5), (9), and (12) has the required common dimension, and

\[
 [T_{AB}]=\mathsf L^{-5},                                     \tag{31}
\]

as appropriate for five-dimensional stress-energy. The radial measure
`w^3 rho ds` has dimension `L^2` before the three tangential coordinate
integrals; the full five-volume has `L^5`, so an integrated stress component is
dimensionless in natural units. Both terms in each expression (15)--(16) have
dimension `L^-2`.

## Limiting-case checks

1. **Unwarped control.** For `w=1`, `A=p=q=0`; (9) becomes
   `T_00=-(r+Lambda_5)/kappa_5`,
   `T_11=T_22=(r+Lambda_5)/kappa_5`, and
   `T_33=T_44=Lambda_5/kappa_5`. This agrees with a direct product of flat
   `2+1` spacetime and a two-surface of Gaussian curvature `-r`.
2. **Ordinary polar control.** For `rho=s`, `r=0`; with `w=1` and
   `Lambda_5=0`, all curvature and effective stress vanish and the NEC
   saturates. This exactly soluble polar axis is excluded because its zero
   extension is not `C1`, confirming that the failure is tied to the frozen
   smooth-flat endpoint rather than to every circle closure.
3. **Constant-radius control.** For constant `rho` and `w=1`, `r=0`; only the
   finite cosmological effective tensor remains. It saturates the NEC because
   a cosmological term has zero null contraction.
4. **Signs.** Equations (14)--(17) show `rho'>0` and `rho''>0` sufficiently
   near zero, while `R^{34}{}_{34}=-r<0`. The product-space Einstein sign then
   gives the negative energy density in (24). Both explicit null contractions
   are negative, so the NEC conclusion does not rely on interpreting one
   diagonal component alone.
5. **Normalization.** The factor `kappa_5^{-1}` and unit coefficient of
   `Lambda_5` follow from D004/D019. A constant-curvature vacuum in D019 has
   `R^{AB}=(Lambda_5/6)E^{AB}` and makes (3) zero, providing the convention
   control.
6. **Conservation.** The torsion-free Bianchi identity gives
   `nabla^A G_AB=0`; finite constant `Lambda_5` and metric compatibility imply
   `nabla^A T_AB=0` exactly on `s>0`. Equivalently, D019 (34),
   `H'+3A(H-F)+B(H-J)=0`, is the reduced identity. Conservation does not imply
   boundedness or NEC.

## Qualified volume-weighted curvature observation

An obvious stronger-looking but still volume-weighted diagnostic also remains
finite. From the ten independent sectional coefficients in (7), the
Kretschmann scalar in these conventions is

\[
 R_{ABCD}R^{ABCD}
 =4\left(3A^4+3p^2+3q^2+r^2\right).                          \tag{32}
\]

For either profile, `rho_nu r_nu^2` is an exponential times a fixed
polynomial in `s^{-1}` and is integrable by D002 (9); all warp terms are even
smaller. Hence the volume-weighted curvature square is locally finite on a
compact tangential cell despite the pointwise divergence (23). The same is
true for the obvious polynomial curvature densities of fixed degree. This is a
qualified finiteness observation only: it neither restores bounded curvature,
nor supplies a matter action, nor weakens the NEC violation, and it does not
broaden the target to higher-curvature dynamics.
