# D019: Minimal warped circle-collapse background

## Question addressed

Does the minimal source-free cohomogeneity-one extension with one common persistent warp factor admit a nontrivial smooth-flat exact circle-collapse background compatible with the D005 fixed-seed shell?

## Branch charter

Work only with the five-dimensional open-core coframe E^i=w(s)dx^i for i=0,1,2, E^3=ds, E^4=rho(s)dtheta, where w>0 and rho>0 for s>0, w=1 and rho=0 for s<=0 with C-infinity-flat attachment of w-1 and rho at s=0. Keep the source-free D004 Palatini action with symbolic constant Lambda_5, a flat four-dimensional exterior with symbolic Lambda_4, fixed timelike interface, and the D005 fixed-seed shell a0=0, g=u=1, b=kappa_4^{-1}. Derive the torsion equation, unique open-core connection, curvature, every independent coframe equation, endpoint implications, and all shell substitutions. Decide exact existence or nonexistence, including any first integral and all exceptional branches. Separate this restricted Poincare-slice warp test from sourceful, anisotropic, lapse-warped, mixed/nonbasic, moving-interface, finite-regularity, and polar-axis escapes. Do not perform Hamiltonian preservation or claim a universal no-go. Stop once the exact existence result, assumptions, checks, and candidate executable obligations are auditable.

## Distinction from sibling branches

it varies one common warp factor for all `2+1` interface directions while
retaining proper-normal gauge, a diagonal circle, the source-free D004 action,
and the D005 fixed-seed shell.  It therefore tests whether isotropic persistent

route is a direct first-order Cartan/Euler-form reduction, not a Hamiltonian or
numerical search.  No sibling output was inspected during this first pass;
distinct GPT sessions would in any event be procedural separation, not

## Derivation

### 1. Connection equation and torsionlessness on the open core

**Exact equation of motion.**  Because (1) is invertible at every `s>0`, write
`T^A=(1/2)T^A{}_{BC}E^{BC}` in (6).  Contraction with the dual frame gives the
injective Cartan map

\[
 T^A{}_{BC}+\delta^A_C T^D{}_{BD}-\delta^A_B T^D{}_{CD}=0.       \tag{8}
\]

Tracing in five dimensions first kills the torsion trace and then every
component, exactly as in D018 (7)--(8):

\[
 \boxed{T^A=0\quad(s>0).}                                      \tag{9}
\]

This conclusion is not imposed or extended by division to `s=0`.

### 2. Unique torsion-free connection and curvature

**Exact coframe identities.**  The only derivatives are

\[
 dE^i=A E^{3i},\qquad dE^3=0,\qquad dE^4=B E^{34}.              \tag{10}
\]

Cartan's equation `dE^A+omega^A{}_B wedge E^B=0`, metric
antisymmetry, and nondegeneracy uniquely give

\[
 \boxed{\omega^{i3}=A E^i=w'dx^i,\qquad
 \omega^{43}=B E^4=\rho'd\theta,}                              \tag{11}
\]

with `omega^{3i}=-omega^{i3}`, `omega^{34}=-omega^{43}`, and all
other independent pairs zero.  The signs can be checked without an inverse:
`omega^i{}_3 wedge E^3=-A E^{3i}=-dE^i` and
`omega^4{}_3 wedge E^3=-B E^{34}=-dE^4`.  Timelike `i=0` causes no
extra sign because the contracted index in these two Cartan terms is the
spacelike index `3`.

Using the D004 curvature convention, direct differentiation and the quadratic
connection terms yield

\[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 R^{ij}&=-A^2E^{ij}, &&0\leq i<j\leq2,\\
 R^{i3}&=-pE^{i3}=-w''dx^i\wedge ds,\\
 R^{i4}&=-qE^{i4}=-w'{\rho'\over\rho}\,dx^i\wedge\rho d\theta,\\
 R^{34}&=-rE^{34}=-\rho''ds\wedge d\theta .
 \end{aligned}}                                                \tag{12}
\]

For example, `R^{ij}=omega^i{}_3 wedge omega^{3j}=-A^2E^{ij}`;
`R^{i3}=d(w'dx^i)=-w''dx^i wedge ds`; and
`R^{34}=d(-rho'dtheta)=-rho''ds wedge dtheta`.  Thus all signs in
(12) are fixed in coordinate forms before introducing the ratios (3).

### 3. All five independent D004 coframe Euler forms

**Exact ordered-pair enumeration.**  Substitution of (12) into (7), including
both orders of every antisymmetric pair, gives

\[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 {\cal G}^{(+)}_0&=-{1\over\kappa_5}
 (2p+A^2+2q+r+\Lambda_5)\Xi_0,\\
 {\cal G}^{(+)}_1&=-{1\over\kappa_5}
 (2p+A^2+2q+r+\Lambda_5)\Xi_1,\\
 {\cal G}^{(+)}_2&=-{1\over\kappa_5}
 (2p+A^2+2q+r+\Lambda_5)\Xi_2,\\
 {\cal G}^{(+)}_3&=-{1\over\kappa_5}
 (3A^2+3q+\Lambda_5)\Xi_3,\\
 {\cal G}^{(+)}_4&=-{1\over\kappa_5}
 (3p+3A^2+\Lambda_5)\Xi_4.
 \end{aligned}}                                                \tag{13}
\]

The `Lambda_5` coefficient is one, not a dimension-dependent rescaling: the
24 cosmological permutations in (7) produce
`-Lambda_5 Xi_A/kappa_5`.  As a sign and multiplicity audit, setting
`A=p=q=0` gives `G_i=-(r+Lambda_5)Xi_i/kappa_5` and
`G_3=G_4=-Lambda_5 Xi/kappa_5`, exactly D018 (13)--(14) after its

For comparison only, after invertibility has already been used on `s>0`, the

Thus (13) is equivalent on the open core to
`G_AB+Lambda_5 eta_AB=0`; the Euler forms carry the corresponding overall

rank-loss-safe statement, and neither inverse coframe nor Einstein components

### 4. Scalar equations, regular coordinate ODEs, and first integrals

Since every `Xi_A` is nonzero on `s>0`, (13) is equivalent there to

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 2p+A^2+2q+r+\Lambda_5&=0,                                    &&\tag{15a}\\
 3(A^2+q)+\Lambda_5&=0,                                       &&\tag{15b}\\
 3(p+A^2)+\Lambda_5&=0.                                       &&\tag{15c}
 \end{aligned}
\]

These ratio equations are useful only on the positive core.  Multiplication by
the appropriate positive factors gives a system smooth at the one-sided
endpoint:

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 2ww''\rho+w'^2\rho+2ww'\rho'+w^2\rho''
       +\Lambda_5w^2\rho&=0,                                  &&\tag{16a}\\
 3(\rho w'^2+ww'\rho')+\Lambda_5w^2\rho&=0,                    &&\tag{16b}\\
 3(ww''+w'^2)+\Lambda_5w^2&=0.                                 &&\tag{16c}
 \end{aligned}
\]

No inverse coframe is needed to take the one-sided limit of (16).

Subtracting (15b) from (15c) gives the exact core relation

\[
 p=q\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 w''\rho-w'\rho'=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \boxed{\left({w'\over\rho}\right)'=0}.                       \tag{17}
\]

Therefore the often-suspected relation `(w'/rho)'=0` is valid here, but it is
derived from two independent Euler forms and uses only `rho>0`; it is not an
ansatz.  On the connected core,

\[
 \boxed{w'=C\rho}                                               \tag{18}
\]

for one finite constant `C` (dimension `L^-2`).  No division by `w'` was made,
so the `C=0` branch is retained.

There is a second exact reduction.  With `y=w^2`, (16c) is the linear equation

\[
 \boxed{y''+{2\Lambda_5\over3}y=0.}                            \tag{19}
\]

The remaining independent equation can be written, using (15b)--(15c), as

\[
 r=3A^2+{\Lambda_5\over3}.                                    \tag{20}
\]

The branches are now explicit:

1. **`C=0` (`w'=0`).**  Then `w` is constant.  Equation (15c) forces
   `Lambda_5=0`, and (15a) forces `r=0`, i.e. `rho''=0`.
2. **`C!=0`.**  Positivity of `rho` makes `w'` nowhere zero and
   `rho=w'/C`.  Equation (19) determines `w^2`; differentiating (16c) shows
   `w'''/w'=3A^2+Lambda_5/3`, so (20) follows.  Thus the local nonzero-`C`
   solutions are exactly the intervals on which a positive square root of a
   solution of (19) has `w'/C>0`.  This branch has not been lost by dividing by
   `w'`.

For `Lambda_5>0`, `y` is trigonometric; for `Lambda_5=0`, affine; and for
`Lambda_5<0`, hyperbolic/exponential.  These local punctured-core families will
be eliminated by the endpoint data, not by an incomplete branch choice.

### 5. Smooth-flat endpoint and exact nonexistence

Taking `s downarrow 0` in the regular equation (16c), using (2), gives

\[
 \boxed{\Lambda_5=0}.                                          \tag{21}
\]

Equation (16c) then says `(ww')'=0`.  Its integration constant has endpoint
value `w(0)w'(0)=0`, and `w>0`, hence

\[
 \boxed{w'=0,\qquad w=1\quad(s>0).}                            \tag{22}
\]

Thus the endpoint selects the `C=0` branch of (18); the `C!=0` family is
impossible.  Equivalently, (19) with `y(0)=1,y'(0)=0` and (21) has the unique
solution `y=1`.

With (21)--(22), (16a) reduces to `rho''=0`.  The connected-core solution is

\[
 \rho(s)=cs+d.                                                  \tag{23}
\]

Continuity gives `d=rho(0)=0`, and `C^1` attachment gives
`c=rho'(0)=0`.  Therefore

\[
 \boxed{\rho\equiv0,}                                          \tag{24}
\]

contradicting `rho>0` on `s>0`.  Only the displayed finite subset of the full
flat jet is needed: `w'(0)=w''(0)=0` fixes `Lambda_5` and the warp integration
constant, while `rho(0)=rho'(0)=0` removes the affine circle branch.

\[
 \boxed{\Lambda_5=\Lambda_4=0,\quad w=1,\quad\rho=0;
 \text{ hence no solution with }w>0,\rho>0\text{ exists}.}     \tag{35}
