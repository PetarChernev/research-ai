# D002: Two-resolution local circle-collapse benchmark

## Question addressed

What topology, smooth structures, differential forms, and surviving connection
data arise in one exact local quotient that collapses a spacelike circle on an
open four-dimensional stratum?

## Branch charter

Retrospective lifecycle metadata: construct and compare two infinitely flat
resolution profiles of the same quotient, separating topology, quotient
smoothness, ambient smoothness, and gauge-invariant surviving data.

## Distinction from sibling branches

This artifact predates breadth-first exploration and was not a sibling branch.
It is the local kinematical/category benchmark; it deliberately excludes the
integration, variational, and canonical questions treated sequentially in
D003-D006.

## Target claim

This artifact constructs one bounded, exact kinematical benchmark for a local
spacelike-circle collapse. It proves the following three propositions,
recorded in the ledger as C007--C009.

**Benchmark proposition A (topology and dimensions; C007).** For the quotient
that collapses each circle over `s<=0`, the two flat-profile blow-down maps
defined below induce homeomorphisms onto their horn images. The quotient has a
regular five-dimensional stratum, a regular four-dimensional stratum, and a
three-dimensional interface; neighborhoods in `X` of interface points have
local model `R^{1,2} x C(S^1 disjoint_union {*})`. At the interface, stratum
dimension, derivation-tangent dimension, and local covering dimension are
different notions.

**Benchmark proposition B (smooth-structure inequivalence; C008).** The common
quotient smooth algebra and two ambient-embedding-induced smooth algebras form
a strict chain, and the canonical homeomorphism between the two embedded horns
is smooth in only one direction.

**Benchmark proposition C (descent and surviving data; C009).** Quotient forms
defined by the exact equalizer over the kernel pair remove the bare angular
one-form `dtheta+A`, but admit `rho(dtheta+A)`, horizontal `A`, `F=dA`, and
horizontal theta-independent mixed connection forms. Consequently the
coframe has rank five on the core and rank four on the resolved exterior, but
the bare quotient does not by itself erase an independently included
Ehresmann curvature or coframe-relative mixed connection curvature on the
nondegenerate rank-four exterior. Ordinary `U(1)` or Lorentz gauge cannot map
the displayed nonzero curvatures to zero. Such data are absent in a
coframe-only state, can be discarded by an explicitly enlarged forgetful
equivalence, or can be excluded by an additional equation/boundary condition;
these operations are not conflated.

All three propositions are proved exactly for the local object and categories
declared below. They are not claims of integration, causality, variational
completeness, dynamics, or global C001.

## Assumptions

### Mathematical and physical assumptions

1. `U` is an open coordinate slab in `R^3` with coordinates
   `(u^0,u^1,u^2)`. The `u^0` direction is designated timelike. Let
   `I=(-epsilon,epsilon)`, with `epsilon>0`, and let `s in I` be spacelike.
2. The smooth resolution manifold is
   
   \[
   M:=U\times I\times S^1,
   \qquad \theta\sim\theta+2\pi . \tag{1}
   \]
   No physical ambient bulk is inferred from this calculational manifold or
   from the Euclidean embedding coordinates introduced below.
3. All maps and fields called smooth on `M` are `C^infty`. Exact collapse is
   imposed on the closed half-slab `s<=0`; no small-radius approximation is
   substituted there.
4. Two positive length scales `ell,L` are fixed, and the profiles are exactly
   
   \[
   \rho _1(s)=
   \begin{cases}
   \ell e^{-(L/s)^2},&s>0,\\
   0,&s\leq0,
   \end{cases}
   \qquad
   \rho _2(s)=
   \begin{cases}
   \ell e^{-(L/s)^4},&s>0,\\
   0,&s\leq0.
   \end{cases} \tag{2}
   \]
   These are the profiles requested in the task; no alteration is needed.
5. The internal metric convention for the coframe test is
   `eta_AB=diag(-,+,+,+,+)`. The fifth internal direction is spacelike.
6. `A` is a smooth, theta-independent base one-form when it is introduced.
   The mixed spin-connection test first treats theta-independent horizontal
   components and then states separately what happens to a `dtheta` leg.
7. No action, equations of motion, symplectic form, or boundary charge is
   assumed. Therefore no equivalence below is called gauge unless an explicit
   ordinary group action is given. The larger kernel/forgetful relations are
   named as such.

### Conventions and boundary conditions

- Coordinates have length dimension. `theta` and integrated angles are
  dimensionless. The normalization `int_{S^1}dtheta=2pi` is fixed.
- The slab and interval are open. There is no variational boundary condition
  at `s=+-epsilon`; this is a local construction and no integration functional
  is defined.
- The interface is `s=0`. Its induced signature from the persistent coframe
  is `(-,+,+)`, so it is timelike in this local model.
- A derivation tangent at `x` means
  `Der_R(C_x^infty,R)`, equivalently the dual of `m_x/m_x^2` for the declared
  local smooth algebra. It is not the Ayala--Francis--Tanaka tangent
  classifier.
- “Local covering dimension” means Lebesgue covering dimension of sufficiently
  small topological neighborhoods. It is not a tangent rank.
- Smoothness of a map between differential subspaces means pullback
  smoothness for their locally ambient-extendible function sheaves.

## Notation

Let `B:=U x I`, let `pi:M->B` be the projection, and let `z=e^{itheta}` denote

\[
(u,s,\theta)\sim_q(u',s',\theta')
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
u=u',\ s=s',\ \text{and}\
\begin{cases}
\theta=\theta'\pmod {2\pi},&s>0,\\
\text{no condition on }\theta,\theta',&s\leq0.
\end{cases} \tag{3}
\]

The quotient with the quotient topology is

\[
X:=M/\!\sim_q,
\qquad q:M\longrightarrow X. \tag{4}
\]

The ambient coordinate space used only to display each horn is

\[
N:=U\times I\times\mathbb R^2,
\qquad (u,s,x,y), \tag{5}
\]

and the two blow-down maps and images are

\[
F_i(u,s,\theta)
 :=(u,s,\rho_i(s)\cos\theta,\rho_i(s)\sin\theta),

\]

The three strata are denoted

\[
X_5:=q\{s>0\},\qquad X_4:=q\{s<0\},\qquad

The kernel pair is

\[
R_q:=M\times_XM,
\qquad p_1,p_2:R_q\rightrightarrows M. \tag{8}

## Derivation

### 1. Exact flatness and the two distinct rates

For `p>0`, every derivative of `e^{-(L/s)^p}` on `s>0` is a finite sum of a
power of `s^{-1}` times the same exponential. For every integer `n>=0` and
every real `m`,

\[
\lim_{s\downarrow0}s^{-m}e^{-(L/s)^p}=0. \tag{9}
\]

It follows directly that both zero extensions in (2) are `C^infty` and that

\[
\rho_i^{(n)}(0)=0\quad\text{for every }n\geq0. \tag{10}
\]

Thus the maps (6) are smooth on all of `M`. The profiles nevertheless have
different punctured-neighborhood rates:

\[
k(s):=\frac{\rho_2(s)}{\rho_1(s)}
=e^{-(L/s)^4+(L/s)^2}\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\frac{\rho_1}{\rho_2}\longrightarrow+\infty. \tag{11}
\]

The zero extension `k(s)=0` for `s<=0` is also smooth. Indeed, sufficiently
near zero the negative quartic term in its exponent dominates the positive
quadratic term, and the estimate (9) applies after every differentiation.
Equation (11), rather than any finite jet at the interface, will separate the
two embedding-induced structures.
The ordering `rho_2<rho_1` is used only after shrinking to a sufficiently
small positive neighborhood of the interface; it need not hold on an
unrestricted slab with `s>L`.

### 2. Quotient/image homeomorphisms

The fibers of each `F_i` are exactly the equivalence classes (3): for `s>0`,
`rho_i(s)>0` and the ordered pair `(cos theta,sin theta)` determines the circle
point; for `s<=0`, both final coordinates vanish for every `theta`. Hence there
is a unique continuous bijection

\[
\bar F_i:X\longrightarrow H_i,
\qquad F_i=\bar F_i\circ q. \tag{12}
\]

It remains to prove that this bijection is a homeomorphism rather than merely
a continuous bijection. The image is the relative closed set

\[
H_i=\{(u,s,x,y)\in N:x^2+y^2=\rho_i(s)^2\}. \tag{13}
\]

For any compact `K subset H_i`, its base projection is compact and

\[
F_i^{-1}(K)\subset \operatorname{pr}_B(K)\times S^1 \tag{14}
\]

is closed. The right-hand side is compact, so `F_i` is proper. Since `M` and
`H_i` are locally compact Hausdorff spaces, the continuous proper surjection
`F_i` is closed and therefore a quotient map. Consequently, for any
`V subset H_i`,

\[
V\text{ open}
\Longleftrightarrow F_i^{-1}(V)\text{ open}
\Longleftrightarrow q^{-1}(\bar F_i^{-1}V)\text{ open}
\Longleftrightarrow \bar F_i^{-1}V\text{ open}. \tag{15}
\]

This proves

\[
\boxed{\bar F_i:X\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}H_i
\text{ is a homeomorphism for }i=1,2.} \tag{16}
\]

The canonical horn homeomorphism is therefore

\[
h_{12}:=\bar F_2\bar F_1^{-1}:H_1\longrightarrow H_2. \tag{17}
\]

On `H_1` it has the explicit expression

\[
h_{12}(u,s,x,y)=
\begin{cases}
(u,s,k(s)x,k(s)y),&s>0,\\
(u,s,0,0),&s\leq0,
\end{cases} \tag{18}
\]

and its continuous inverse preserves `(u,s,theta)` while replacing `rho_2`
by `rho_1`.

### 3. Regular strata, local cone, and three dimensions at the interface

On `s>0`, the last two coordinates of (6) form a nonzero circle, and `F_i`
has rank five. Thus `X_5` is a regular five-manifold locally diffeomorphic to
`U x (0,epsilon) x S^1`. On `s<0`, the angle has been removed and `X_4` is a
regular four-manifold diffeomorphic to `U x (-epsilon,0)`. Finally,
`Sigma` is homeomorphic to `U`, hence is a three-manifold. It is a singular
interface in `X`: every one of its neighborhoods meets regular strata of two
different dimensions and is not a manifold neighborhood.

Let

\[
L_\Sigma:=S^1\sqcup\{*\}, \tag{19}
\]

and let `C_delta(L_Sigma)` be the truncated open cone. Near any point of
`Sigma`, define a map from the cone by

\[
(r,e^{i\theta})\mapsto q(u,+r,\theta),\qquad
(r,*)\mapsto q(u,-r,\theta_0),\qquad
r=0\mapsto q(u,0,\theta_0). \tag{20}
\]

The value of `theta_0` is immaterial on the last two expressions. The map and
its inverse are continuous by the quotient topology; compactness of `S^1`
ensures that a saturated neighborhood of the whole circle over `s=0`
contains a uniform conical neighborhood. Therefore the exact local
topological model is

\[
\boxed{X\text{ near }\Sigma\cong
\mathbb R^{1,2}\times C(S^1\sqcup\{*\}).} \tag{21}
\]

The `S^1` cone branch is a two-dimensional disk near its tip, while the
singleton cone branch is a ray attached at the same tip. Hence a neighborhood
is a five-dimensional piece and a four-dimensional piece glued along a
three-dimensional piece. Every interface neighborhood contains an open
five-manifold subset, so its covering dimension is at least five. Conversely,
the finite closed-union theorem for metric covering dimension applied to the
disk branch and ray branch gives an upper bound of five. Thus

\[
\dim_{\rm cover,loc}(X,x)=5,
\qquad x\in\Sigma. \tag{22}
\]

The interface stratum dimension, by contrast, is

\[
\dim\Sigma=3. \tag{23}
\]

The derivation dimension requires a choice of smooth algebra and is computed
next; it is not determined by (21) alone.

### 4. Three smooth-structure candidates

#### 4.1 Common final/quotient structure

The final diffeology `D_q` declares `p:O->X` to be a plot precisely when it
locally has a smooth lift `p_tilde:O->M` with `p=q p_tilde`. Its smooth real
functions are equivalently

\[
C_q^\infty(X)
=\{f:X\to\mathbb R:f\circ q\in C^\infty(M)\}. \tag{24}
\]

Writing `g=f q`, condition (24) says exactly that `g` is smooth on `M` and is
theta-independent for `s<=0`. Smoothness then forces every nonconstant angular
Fourier coefficient to be flat at `s=0`.

At an interface point, translate the base coordinates so that the point is
`u=0,s=0`. Let `m_q` be the maximal ideal of quotient-smooth germs vanishing
there. For a representative `g`, define its angular average and remainder by

\[
\bar g(u,s):=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}g(u,s,\theta)d\theta,
\qquad h:=g-\bar g. \tag{25}
\]

The remainder is zero for `s<=0`. Hadamard's lemma therefore gives
`h=s k` with smooth `k`, and `k=0` for `s<=0`; in particular both `s` and `k`
belong to `m_q`. Thus `[h]=0` in `m_q/m_q^2`. Ordinary first-order Taylor
expansion of `bar g` now gives

\[
[g-g(0)]=\sum_{\alpha=0}^2
(\partial_{u^\alpha}\bar g)_0[u^\alpha]
+(\partial_s\bar g)_0[s]
\quad\text{in }m_q/m_q^2. \tag{26}

The four base directional derivatives are well-defined derivations and are
dual to these four classes. Hence

\[
\boxed{\dim\operatorname{Der}_x(C_q^\infty,\mathbb R)=4,
\qquad x\in\Sigma.} \tag{27}

Angular flat functions do not add derivation directions because they are in
`m_q^2`, even though they can distinguish points on every `s>0` circle.

#### 4.2 Two ambient-embedding-induced structures

For `i=1,2`, declare a function on `X` to be `C_i^infty` near `x` when its
transport to `H_i` is the restriction of a smooth function on an open
neighborhood in `N`. This is the standard differential-subspace sheaf, and
it is a different definition from (24).

At an interface image point `p=(u,0,0,0)`, let `Phi` be an ambient smooth germ
that vanishes on `H_i`. Varying `u` and approaching along the negative `s`
axis gives

\[
\partial_{u^\alpha}\Phi(p)=0,
\qquad \partial_s\Phi(p)=0. \tag{28}

For `s>0`, `Phi` vanishes at both
`(u,s,+rho_i(s),0)` and `(u,s,-rho_i(s),0)`. The one-dimensional mean-value
theorem supplies a point between them where `partial_x Phi=0`; taking
`s downarrow 0` yields `partial_x Phi(p)=0`. The pair of points on the `y`
axis similarly gives `partial_y Phi(p)=0`. Thus every ambient germ vanishing
on `H_i` has zero differential at `p`.

The ambient coordinate germs therefore remain six independent generators of
the cotangent quotient `m_i/m_i^2`, and Hadamard's lemma gives the matching
upper bound. Consequently

\[
\boxed{\dim\operatorname{Der}_x(C_i^\infty,\mathbb R)=6,
\qquad x\in\Sigma,\quad i=1,2.} \tag{29}

For comparison, the complete local dimension table is

| location | stratum dimension | quotient derivation dimension | embedded derivation dimension | local covering dimension |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `X_5`, `s>0` | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| `X_4`, `s<0` | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| `Sigma`, `s=0` | 3 | 4 | 6 | 5 |

This table is an exact example of why “the dimension at the singular point”
is not meaningful until the dimension notion and smooth category are fixed.

#### 4.3 Strict inequivalence and the bounded-derivative proof

The smooth zero extension of `k` in (11) makes

\[
\widetilde h_{12}(u,s,x,y):=(u,s,k(s)x,k(s)y) \tag{30}

an ambient smooth map whose restriction to `H_1` is (18). Therefore
`h_12:(H_1,C_1^infty)->(H_2,C_2^infty)` is smooth.

Its inverse is not smooth. The ambient coordinate `x` on `H_1`, transported
by `h_12^{-1}` to `H_2`, is the continuous function

\[
f_1(u,s,\theta)=
\begin{cases}
\rho_1(s)\cos\theta,&s>0,\\
0,&s\leq0.
\end{cases} \tag{31}

Suppose `f_1` had a smooth ambient extension `Phi` near an interface point of
`H_2`. At fixed `u` and every sufficiently small `s>0`,

\[
\Phi(u,s,+\rho_2(s),0)=+\rho_1(s),\qquad
\Phi(u,s,-\rho_2(s),0)=-\rho_1(s). \tag{32}

The mean-value theorem on the intervening ambient line segment gives a
`xi_s in (-rho_2,rho_2)` with

\[
\partial_x\Phi(u,s,\xi_s,0)=\frac{\rho_1(s)}{\rho_2(s)}
=e^{(L/s)^4-(L/s)^2}. \tag{33}

The right-hand side diverges, whereas `partial_x Phi` is bounded on a small
closed ambient neighborhood. This contradiction proves that `f_1` has no
locally ambient `C^1` extension, is not `C_2^infty`, and that the inverse is
not smooth:

\[
\boxed{h_{12}\text{ is a smooth homeomorphism in one direction but is not a
diffeomorphism of the embedded differential spaces.}} \tag{34}

The three candidates are genuinely distinct, not merely the two embedded
ones. Every ambient restriction pulls back through `F_i` to a smooth
fiber-constant function, so `C_i^infty subset C_q^infty`. Equation (30) gives
`C_2^infty subset C_1^infty`, and (31)--(33) make that inclusion strict. To
make the other inclusion strict, define

\[
\rho_0(s)=
\begin{cases}\ell e^{-L/s},&s>0,\\0,&s\leq0,\end{cases}
\qquad f_0=\rho_0(s)\cos\theta. \tag{35}
\]

Then `f_0` is quotient-smooth, while the same mean-value proof on `H_1`
would require a bounded ambient derivative to equal
`rho_0/rho_1=e^{(L/s)^2-L/s}->infinity`. Hence

\[
\boxed{C_2^\infty\subsetneq C_1^\infty\subsetneq C_q^\infty.} \tag{36}

#### 4.4 Check using literal ambient sub-diffeologies

The conclusion (34) is not an artifact of choosing locally extendible
functions instead of sub-diffeologies. Give each `H_i` the diffeology induced
from `N`. Fix `u` and define a curve in `H_2` by

\[
s(t)=t,\qquad
(x(t),y(t))=
\begin{cases}
\rho_2(t)(\cos\Theta(t),\sin\Theta(t)),&t>0,\\
(0,0),&t\leq0,
\end{cases}
\quad
\Theta(t):=e^{(L/t)^3}. \tag{37}

For each derivative order `n`, derivatives of the angular factor grow at
most as a power of `t^{-1}` times `e^{n(L/t)^3}`, which is dominated by
`e^{-(L/t)^4}`. Thus `(x(t),y(t))` is smooth and flat at zero, so (37) is an
ambient-subspace plot. Its image under `h_12^{-1}` has radial factor `rho_1`.
The norm of its derivative contains the orthogonal angular term

\[
\rho_1(t)|\Theta'(t)|
=3\ell L^3t^{-4}e^{-(L/t)^2+(L/t)^3}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{38}

It is not even `C^1`. Hence the inverse also fails to be smooth for the
literal sub-diffeologies. This plot does not locally lift smoothly through
`q` at `t=0`, illustrating that the final quotient diffeology and ambient
sub-diffeology are different constructions.

### 5. Admissible morphisms and two nonconflated equivalences

#### 5.1 Ordinary smooth/equivariant resolution morphisms

The resolution carries the right circle action

\[
R_\beta(u,s,\theta)=(u,s,\theta+\beta). \tag{39}

For this benchmark, an **ordinary resolution morphism** is a smooth map
`Phi:M_1->M_2` that is `U(1)`-equivariant, covers a smooth base map preserving
the signs of `s` and the interface, and induces a continuous map `bar Phi` on
the quotient:

\[
q_2\Phi=\bar\Phi q_1. \tag{40}

It is an ordinary resolution equivalence only when `Phi` and its inverse obey
the same conditions. If the ambient horn differential structure is declared
part of the object, the induced horn map must additionally be a differential
space diffeomorphism.

The identity map of the common cylinder `M` is a smooth equivariant
isomorphism of the two *bare resolution manifolds* and commutes with `q`.
However, its induced horn map is `h_12`, which fails the extra embedding
condition by (34). If the coframe profile is included as physical data, the
identity also fails to identify `E^4_1` with `E^4_2`. Thus ordinary equivalence
depends materially on which structures are included in a resolution object.

Ordinary local vertical transformations have the explicit action

\[
\theta\mapsto\theta-\alpha(u,s),\qquad
A\mapsto A+d\alpha. \tag{41}

They are smooth equivariant automorphisms and leave `dtheta+A` invariant.
They act nontrivially on the core quotient unless `alpha=0 mod 2pi` there.

#### 5.2 Kernel-pair and exterior forgetful equivalences

The pointwise equivalence defining the quotient is exactly the kernel pair
`R_q` in (8). Set-theoretically it consists of

\[
\begin{split}
R_q|_{s>0}&=\{(u,s,\theta;u,s,\theta)\},\\
R_q|_{s\leq0}&=\{(u,s,\theta_1;u,s,\theta_2)\}.
\end{split} \tag{42}

It removes all exterior fiber labels by definition. This relation is not the
orbit relation of the ordinary vertical action (39), because that action has
circle orbits also on the core. Nor is it generated by the kernel of ordinary
automorphisms acting trivially on `X`: a smooth `alpha` that vanishes for
`s>0` must also vanish at `s=0`, so such transformations do not connect the
different interface angles that (42) identifies. The relation has jumping
arrow dimension and has not been supplied with a Lie-groupoid action,
constraint generator, or presymplectic null distribution. It is therefore
called a **kernel equivalence**, not gauge.

For fields, a still larger but explicit **exterior forgetful equivalence** can
be defined as the kernel relation of a declared projection. Two useful
examples are:

1. `Pi_cof:(rho,A)->E^4=rho(dtheta+A)`. If two smooth potentials agree on the
   core but differ on `s<0`, they have the same coframe because `rho=0` there.
2. `Pi_4` retains the core fields and only the chosen four-dimensional
   exterior fields `(E^a,omega^{ab},...)`, while omitting exterior `A` and
   `omega^{4a}`. Configurations are `~_forget` precisely when their retained
   images under `Pi_4` agree (up to declared ordinary transformations).

These are well-defined equivalence relations because they are kernel pairs of
specified maps. They explicitly state their observables. They are not gauge
relations: no action or canonical analysis proves that their fibers are
orbits of zero-charge first-class generators. Also, `~_forget` over the
exterior does not identify the two core circumference profiles; doing that
would require an additional profile-forgetting declaration.

### 6. Exact equalizer complex and the requested forms

Define quotient differential forms for this benchmark by the exact equalizer

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_q^k(X):=
\operatorname{Eq}\!\left(
\Omega^k(M)\ \substack{\xrightarrow{\ p_1^*\ }\\[-2mm]
\xrightarrow[\ p_2^*\ ]{}}\ \Omega^k(R_q)
\right).} \tag{43}

Here `R_q` has its fiber-product diffeology, so the pullbacks are defined on
all plots. Exterior differentiation preserves (43) because pullback commutes
with `d`.

For the declared final quotient diffeology, (43) is also the actual pullback
image of the plotwise diffeological de Rham complex, not merely an ad hoc
subcomplex. Every plot `P:O->X` locally has a smooth lift
`P_tilde:O->M`. Given an equalizing form `alpha` on `M`, define the form on
that plot as `P_tilde^*alpha`. Two local lifts over an overlap define a plot
of the fiber product `R_q`, so the equalizer identity makes their pullbacks
agree. The local forms therefore glue and are compatible with smooth
reparametrization. Conversely, pullback by the subduction `q` is injective and
every quotient form necessarily equalizes the kernel pair. Hence

\[
q^*\Omega^k_{\rm diff}(X)=\Omega_q^k(X) . \tag{43a}
\]

This equality is for the precise final and fiber-product diffeologies declared
here; no claim is made for a different quotient calculus.

For functions, (43) gives exactly

\[
g(u,s,\theta_1)=g(u,s,\theta_2)\quad(s\leq0), \tag{44}

\]

which reproduces (24). For a general smooth one-form on `M`, write globally
using the invariant circle one-form

\[
\alpha=a_\mu(u,s,\theta)\,dx^\mu+b(u,s,\theta)d\theta,
\qquad (x^\mu)=(u^0,u^1,u^2,s). \tag{45}

On the full exterior branch
`(u,s,theta_1,theta_2)` of (42), equality of the `dtheta_1` and `dtheta_2`
coefficients and of the common base coefficients is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
a_\mu(u,s,\theta)\text{ is theta-independent and }b(u,s,\theta)=0
\quad\text{for }s\leq0.} \tag{46}

Smoothness supplies the corresponding flat matching at `s=0`. Conversely,
(46) gives equality on the exterior branch, while equality on the core
diagonal is automatic, so (46) is also sufficient.

Now let

\[
A=A_\mu(u,s)dx^\mu,
\qquad \eta:=d\theta+A,
\qquad F:=dA. \tag{47}

The requested tests follow immediately and exactly:

1. `eta=dtheta+A` has `b=1` and therefore **does not descend**.
2. `rho_i eta` has vertical coefficient `rho_i`, which is zero for `s<=0`,
   and horizontal coefficient `rho_i A`, also zero there. Hence
   
   \[
   E_i^4:=\rho_i(d\theta+A)\in\Omega_q^1(X). \tag{48}
   \]
3. The horizontal theta-independent form `A` obeys (46), so
   `A in Omega_q^1(X)`. Its curvature `F=dA` is also in `Omega_q^2(X)`.
4. A theta-independent mixed spin connection can be decomposed as
   
   \[
   \omega^{4a}=w^{4a}_\mu(u,s)dx^\mu
   +\chi^{4a}(u,s)d\theta. \tag{49}
   \]
   Theta independence alone is insufficient: the horizontal part descends,
   while the vertical part descends only if `chi^{4a}=0` for `s<=0` (or is
   multiplied by a smooth factor such as `rho_i` that vanishes there).

The exact derivative of the descended coframe form is

\[
dE_i^4=d\rho_i\wedge(d\theta+A)+\rho_iF. \tag{50}

\]

Because the profiles are flat, both `rho_i` and `d rho_i` vanish on the
exterior and at the interface. Thus (50) vanishes there. This benchmark does
not retain the nonzero `d rho wedge eta` interface term possible at lower
regularity; infinite flatness is a material assumption.

There is also a sharp mismatch with the embedded differential-form
candidate. If an ambient smooth one-form restricted to `H_i` pulled back to
`rho_i(dtheta+A)`, write its `dx,dy` coefficients as `P,Q`. Evaluation on
`partial_theta` (on which the horizontal `A` vanishes) and division by
`rho_i>0` would require

\[
-P(F_i)\sin\theta+Q(F_i)\cos\theta=1. \tag{51}

\]

Taking `s downarrow0` would require one fixed linear combination of
`sin theta,cos theta` to equal one for every angle, which is impossible.
Hence `E_i^4` is a valid quotient-equalizer form but is not the restriction of
an ordinary ambient smooth one-form on the horn. This is another reason not
to identify the quotient and embedded calculi.

### 7. Coframe regularity, rank, signature, and circumference

Set

\[
(E^0,E^1,E^2,E^3)=(du^0,du^1,du^2,ds),
\qquad E_i^4=\rho_i(d\theta+A). \tag{52}

\]

All five forms are smooth on `M`, and the quotient forms are well-defined by
(43). On `s>0`, evaluation on the vertical vector gives
`E_i^4(partial_theta)=rho_i>0`; together with the four base forms this gives
rank five. On `s<=0`, `E_i^4=0` and the resolved coframe has rank four with
kernel spanned by `partial_theta`. Thus

\[
\operatorname{rank}(E_i^A)=
\begin{cases}5,&s>0,\\4,&s\leq0.
\end{cases} \tag{53}

\]

The corresponding resolved tensor is

\[
g_i=-(du^0)^2+(du^1)^2+(du^2)^2+ds^2
+\rho_i^2(d\theta+A)^2. \tag{54}

\]

It has signature `(-,+,+,+,+)` on `X_5`. On the resolution exterior it is
degenerate in the vertical direction, while on the actual quotient stratum
`X_4` it induces the nondegenerate signature `(-,+,+,+)`. Restricted to
`Sigma`, `ds=0` on stratum tangents and the induced signature is `(-,+,+)`.

The opened-circle circumference is normalized exactly as

\[
\mathcal L_i(u,s)=\int_{S^1}E_i^4=2\pi\rho_i(s),
\qquad s>0. \tag{55}

\]

It differs between the two profiles near the interface. Therefore, if (54)
or (55) is a designated physical observable, the two profiles are distinct
core geometries, not two equivalent descriptions of one metric. They become
“the same resolution” only after a separate rule forgets this data. Both
circumferences vanish exactly on the exterior, and every interface jet of
each profile vanishes, so finite interface jets alone do not see their
difference.

At the interface, (52) has rank four on the four-dimensional quotient
derivation tangent (27) and rank three after pullback to the actual interface
stratum. In the embedded calculus, the four persistent ambient-restricted
forms have rank four on the six-dimensional derivation tangent (29), but
`E_i^4` itself is not an ambient-restricted one-form by (51); a rank of the
full five-form set is therefore not defined in that calculus without adding a
larger form module. This is a category mismatch, not a fifth value of the same
tangent rank.

### 8. Coframe-only, Ehresmann, and first-order connection states

#### 8.1 Coframe-only state

If the state contains only the forms `(E^A)` in (52), `A` is not an
independent exterior field. Any change `delta A` supported in `s<0` obeys

\[
\delta E^4=\rho_i\,\delta A=0,
\qquad \delta(dE^4)=d\rho_i\wedge\delta A+\rho_i d(\delta A)=0 \tag{56}

\]

there and at the flat interface. Such changes lie in the kernel of `Pi_cof`
defined in §5.2. In this coframe-only state, exterior `A` and `F` are absent as
independent observables. This is a state-space choice, not a consequence that
all mathematically allowed quotient one-forms vanish.

#### 8.2 Independent Ehresmann `U(1)` connection

If the resolution principal bundle `M->B` is supplied with the independent
Ehresmann connection `eta=dtheta+A`, then (41) is its ordinary vertical gauge
action. It gives

\[
\eta\mapsto\eta,
\qquad F=dA\mapsto F. \tag{57}

\]

Although `eta` itself fails the quotient equalizer, the base forms `A` and
`F` pass it. In particular, if `F` is nonzero on an exterior open set, no
ordinary vertical or `U(1)` gauge transformation can turn it into zero. On a
contractible local patch, `A` can be transformed to zero only when `F=0`.

This can be seen with an explicit exterior-supported example. Choose a smooth
bump `chi(s)` supported in `(-epsilon,0)` (with dimension `L^{-1}` in the
units below) and set

\[
A'=\chi(s)du^1,
\qquad F'=\chi'(s)ds\wedge du^1. \tag{58}

\]

For nonconstant `chi`, `(A',F')` is not gauge-equivalent to `(0,0)`, but both
give exactly the same coframe as `(0,0)` wherever their difference is
supported because `rho_i=0`. Thus only the coframe-forgetful kernel identifies
them.

#### 8.3 Independent first-order `SO(1,4)` connection

Now include an independent antisymmetric connection `omega^{AB}` with

\[
R^{AB}=d\omega^{AB}+\omega^A{}_C\wedge\omega^{CB}. \tag{59}

\]

Under `E'=Lambda E`, the ordinary Lorentz action is

\[
\omega'=\Lambda\omega\Lambda^{-1}-d\Lambda\,\Lambda^{-1},
\qquad R'=\Lambda R\Lambda^{-1}. \tag{60}

\]

Consequently a connection with nonzero total curvature cannot be Lorentz
gauge-equivalent to a flat connection. Individual fixed labels `R^{4a}` can
mix under a full frame change and are not by themselves full-`SO(1,4)`
invariants. On the open exterior of this benchmark, however, the rank-four
coframe has a nondegenerate internal image `W` of signature `(-,+,+,+)` and
therefore an internal unit spacelike normal `n^A`, unique up to sign. Define

\[
P^A{}_B=\delta^A{}_B-n^A n_B,
\qquad K^B:=n_A R^{AC}P_C{}^B . \tag{60a}
\]

When `E` and `R` transform together, `n`, `P`, and `K` transform covariantly,
so `K=0` versus `K!=0` is frame-independent. In the stabilizer frame with
`n=e_4` and `E^4=0`, `K` is the mixed collection `R^{4a}` and transforms
invertibly under the residual `SO(1,3)`. This construction applies to the
nondegenerate rank-four exterior (or a declared four-dimensional quotient
tangent), not to the intrinsic rank-three tangent of `Sigma`, where no unique
one-dimensional internal normal follows from the restricted coframe. A full
Lorentz transformation may also leave the chosen componentwise horizontal
presentation; the curvature statements, not fixed component labels, are the
gauge-covariant observables.

An explicit theta-independent exterior example is

\[
\omega^{40}=\chi(s)du^1,
\qquad \omega^{04}=-\omega^{40},\qquad
\omega^{AB}=0\text{ for all other independent pairs},
\qquad R^{40}=\chi'(s)ds\wedge du^1. \tag{61}

\]

It is horizontal and passes the equalizer in the displayed frame. On the bump
support the coframe-relative `K` is nonzero, so for nonconstant `chi` it is not
Lorentz gauge-equivalent to the zero connection. The rank-four coframe does
not remove it. It is absent if the state is coframe-only, discarded if `Pi_4`
explicitly forgets the mixed exterior connection, or excluded if a future
field equation or boundary condition forces it to vanish. These are three
different operations; no such dynamics has been derived here.
