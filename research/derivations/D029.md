# D029: Dynamical localization and confinement route

## Question addressed

Can autonomous matter dynamics select a transverse region accessible to a probe without changing the bulk geometry? This construction gives a moving planar localization layer and one explicit transverse spectrum. It does **not** give a finite three-space bubble or an intrinsically dimension-changing spacetime.

## Branch charter

Slot C. Let a varied trapping/confining order parameter control which spatial directions a probe can access in a fixed bulk; do not vary a compactification radius or merely impose a trap profile. Define an autonomous medium/probe action, derive one transverse spectrum or escape/response witness, and identify localization-region dynamics and probe-universality costs. State exact versus approximate confinement, fixed-bulk ontology, and failure of strict dimension claims. Stop after one tractable construction and roadmap.

## Distinction from sibling branches

The bulk is permanently five-dimensional, noncompact, and flat. A varied scalar order parameter changes a probe's mass potential, not its kinetic rank, a radius, adjacency, or the manifold. The region is a soliton selected by boundary conditions, rather than a prescribed spacetime-dependent coupling. This distinction follows the frozen branch matrix, not sibling outputs; none were inspected. Parallel GPT work is not independent evidence.

## Target claim

**Derived result, conditional on the model below:** a scalar wall has an exactly normalizable probe mode with four-dimensional dispersion, separated from bulk scattering states by a transverse mass-squared gap. Its localization center has autonomous motion. “Accessible dimension” means the directions represented by propagating states at specified energy and preparation, not intrinsic dimension: the latter is always total spacetime dimension `d=5`.

The single load-bearing witness is the bound-state/continuum spectrum. The construction relaxes the exact `d=4` exterior, absence of permanent bulk, compact extra direction, and finite localized-particle requirements.

## Assumptions

**Model assumptions:** use natural units and fixed Minkowski signature `(-,+,+,+,+)`. Two real classical scalars have canonical positive-energy kinetic terms. There is no gravity or gauge field. The quartic theory is a classical effective model below an unspecified ultraviolet cutoff, not a proposed UV-complete five-dimensional quantum theory.

Take positive constants `lambda,v`, nonnegative `m0^2`, and the particular positive coupling `g=lambda`. This tuning makes the one chosen spectrum elementary; universality is not assumed. In the static wall sector impose `phi(y→−∞)=−v`, `phi(y→+∞)=v`, with vanishing derivatives and `chi=0`. Variations preserve this sector and have suitable decay; probe bound states are square-integrable in `y`, while continuum states use scattering boundary conditions. The wall extends over all three ordinary spatial coordinates. Its energy is finite per three-volume, not finite in total.

## Notation

Write `X^A=(x^mu,y)`, `x^mu=(t,x^1,x^2,x^3)`. The medium is `phi`; `chi` is the probe; `Box_5=−∂t²+∇x²+∂y²`. Define inverse wall width `a=v sqrt(lambda/2)`, center `Z`, and transverse rest coordinate `s=y−Z`. A mode has ordinary momentum `k`, frequency `omega`, and four-dimensional mass squared `mu²=omega²−|k|²`. `M²(y)=m0²+g phi²(y)` is its local mass potential, not a metric component. `epsilon` counts small probe amplitude. `u` denotes a constant transverse wall velocity, not the vacuum value `v`.

## Derivation

### 1. Autonomous selector

**Exact model definition:**

\[
S=\int d^4x\,dy\left[-\frac12\partial_A\phi\partial^A\phi
-\frac\lambda4(\phi^2-v^2)^2
-\frac12\partial_A\chi\partial^A\chi
-\frac12(m_0^2+g\phi^2)\chi^2\right].
\]

Both fields are varied. Integration by parts gives the **exact equations**

\[
\Box_5\phi-\lambda\phi(\phi^2-v^2)-g\phi\chi^2=0,
\qquad(\Box_5-m_0^2-g\phi^2)\chi=0.
\]

For `chi=0` and a static planar medium, multiply
`phi''=lambda phi(phi²−v²)` by `phi'`. The boundary conditions set the integration constant to zero:

\[
\tfrac12\phi'^2=\tfrac\lambda4(\phi^2-v^2)^2,
\qquad \phi'=\sqrt{\lambda/2}(v^2-\phi^2).
\]

Separation yields the **exact solution**

\[
\phi_K(s)=v\tanh(as).
\]

Its position is a free integration constant, not an externally imposed trap center. Completing the static medium energy square gives
`∫dy [phi'−sqrt(lambda/2)(v²−phi²)]²/2` plus the boundary term `4av²/3`. Thus the monotone wall minimizes planar static energy in this sector. Tangential gradients add positive energy. This establishes an energetic wall control, not nonlinear asymptotic stability of every evolving configuration.

### 2. What actually evolves

Lorentz invariance supplies an **exact moving solution**, still with zero probe:

\[
\phi(t,y)=v\tanh\{a\gamma[y-Z_0-ut]\},
\qquad\gamma=(1-u^2)^{-1/2},\quad |u|<1.
\]

Initial position and velocity choose this solution; its trajectory then follows the equations. In particular, arbitrarily specified acceleration would not solve them.

For slow, long-wavelength bending, insert `phi_K(y−Z(x))`. Since
`∫dy phi_K'²=T=4av²/3`, the action through quadratic order in slopes is

\[
S_Z=\int d^4x[-T-\tfrac T2\partial_\mu Z\partial^\mu Z+\cdots],
\qquad \Box_4 Z=0
\]

at leading derivative order. The time-kinetic coefficient is positive. Profile relaxation enters beyond this retained approximation. This demonstrates translation and leading bending dynamics, not nucleation from the uniform vacuum or dynamical selection of a finite bubble. The topological sector itself was chosen as boundary data.

### 3. One probe-spectrum witness

**Linear-response approximation:** set `chi=epsilon chi_1+O(epsilon³)` and `phi=phi_K+O(epsilon²)`, with no independently excited medium perturbation. Probe backreaction first sources the medium at order `epsilon²`. In the wall rest frame take
`chi_1=e^(−i omega t+i k·x) f(s)` and its real part. Then

\[
Hf=\mu^2f,\qquad
H=-\partial_s^2+m_0^2+2a^2-2a^2\operatorname{sech}^2(as),
\]

using `g v²=lambda v²=2a²`. Let

\[
A=\partial_s+a\tanh(as),\quad
A^\dagger=-\partial_s+a\tanh(as).
\]

Direct multiplication gives the **exact linear-operator identities**

\[
H=A^\dagger A+m_0^2+a^2,\qquad
AA^\dagger=-\partial_s^2+a^2.
\]

The equation `Af0=0` integrates to

\[
\boxed{f_0(s)=\sqrt{a/2}\operatorname{sech}(as),\quad
\mu_0^2=m_0^2+a^2,\quad
\omega^2=|k|^2+\mu_0^2.}
\]

Normalization follows from `∫ds sech²(as)=2/a`; the reduced probe kinetic term is therefore canonical. There is no second bound state: a positive discrete eigenvalue of `A†A` below `a²` would map by `A` to a square-integrable eigenfunction of the free operator `AA†`, which has none. Negative eigenvalues are excluded by its quadratic form. For real transverse momentum `p`, applying `A†` to `e^(ips)` constructs continuum modes with

\[
\boxed{\mu^2=m_0^2+2a^2+p^2,\qquad
\Delta\mu^2=a^2.}
\]

The threshold solution is not square-integrable. These statements are exact for the linearized operator, not for interacting quantum excitations.

### 4. Meaning of the region

For a chosen invariant mass-squared budget `q` satisfying
`m0²<q<m0²+2a²`, a classical turning-point definition gives

\[
\mathcal R_q:\quad |s|<a^{-1}\operatorname{artanh}
\sqrt{\frac{q-m_0^2}{2a^2}}.
\]

This follows from `M²(s)<q`; it is an energy-dependent diagnostic, not a quantum boundary. For the ground state its half-width is `a^−1 artanh(1/sqrt(2))`, but the wavefunction is nonzero at every finite `s`, decaying as `e^(−a|s|)`. Under the exact boost this diagnostic layer moves with the wall and is Lorentz-contracted in laboratory coordinates.

Below the continuum threshold, this probe's on-shell wall sector has only a `3+1` dispersion; above it, bulk transverse propagation is available. A finite-width wavefunction is not itself an extra continuous low-energy direction. Thus this is a dynamical localization layer, **not** an extra-dimensional bubble surrounded by a physical four-dimensional exterior. Even perfect spectral binding does not remove the bulk.

## Approximations

The background and its constant-velocity boost are exact classical solutions. The probe spectrum retains order `epsilon` in its equation, discarding order `epsilon³` corrections from induced medium response. This is a small-amplitude expansion over times for which backreaction remains small, not a uniform infinite-time claim. The bending equation retains quadratic slope terms and leading derivatives; require small slopes and variation scale `L` with `aL≫1`. No quantum-loop corrections are included.

## Validity regime

Use positive `a`, the tuned coupling, infinite transverse line, and rest-frame stationary spectral boundary conditions. Frequencies and inverse widths must lie below the EFT cutoff. No coordinate is quotiented and no gauge freedom removes transverse states. The background spontaneously breaks transverse translations and boosts; ordinary wall Poincaré symmetry gives the displayed dispersion. Overall five-dimensional translation invariance conserves full-system stress-energy; probe energy exchange with a moving or perturbed wall cannot generally be ignored beyond linear response.

## Dimensional check

In mass units, `[x]=[y]=−1`, `[phi]=[chi]=[v]=3/2`, `[lambda]=[g]=−1`, and `[a]=[m0]=1`. Every Lagrangian term has dimension five; both medium-equation terms have dimension `7/2`. `[H]=[mu²]=2`, `[f0]=1/2`, and the reduced four-dimensional field has dimension one. `[T]=4` and `[Z]=−1`, consistent with the effective wall action. Potential and gradient energies are nonnegative; `mu0²>0`, so the demonstrated probe sector has neither a tachyon nor a wrong-sign kinetic term.

## Limiting-case checks

As `a→0` at fixed nonnegative `m0²`, binding gap and localization disappear: the normalized mode spreads without a normalizable limiting eigenfunction. One must not interchange this limit with normalization on the infinite line. At `m0=0` the localized mode remains massive, `mu0=a`; a massless wall particle has not been produced. Reflection `s→−s` combined with `phi→−phi` leaves the probe operator invariant. A decoupled species with `g=0` sees a free fifth direction, explicitly falsifying probe universality. Bulk states survive for every finite positive coupling in this model.

## Unresolved concerns

The central blocker is geometric reach: this is a global planar layer, not a finite localized region in ordinary three-space. The medium is also a physical bulk species. Species-dependent potentials, exponentially noncompact tails, unrestricted sufficiently energetic bulk states, and a permanent fifth coordinate defeat the strong intrinsic target. The special solvable coupling has no protection established here. Probe interactions and wall radiation can invalidate a fixed-background spectrum; classical positivity does not establish quantum consistency.
