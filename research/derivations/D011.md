# D011: Symplectic quotient and stabilizer classification

## Question addressed

Within the fixed-interface planar basic persistent `SO(1,3)` sector of
`D005`/`D006`, what is the minimum complete bulk-plus-interface Lorentz
generator and boundary polarization needed to decide whether the C016
Stueckelberg rank-ten block is first-class gauge, physical edge structure, or
a second-class remnant?

## Branch charter

Represent the shell boundary data as a product of core, exterior, matching,
and Stueckelberg Hamiltonian `SO(1,3)` spaces. Use local symplectic reduction
and stabilizer analysis to determine which diagonal or relative Lorentz action
could remove the rank-ten block, what moment-map level is required, and the
resulting dimension bounds. State explicitly which bulk moment maps must
exist; do not assume regularity or freeness.

## Distinction from sibling branches

This branch uses a finite/local Hamiltonian-product model and clean/singular
symplectic-quotient dimension bounds. It does not derive a full field-theory
Gauss law, perform a Dirac-bracket calculation, or choose a corner
polarization. It therefore differs materially from routes based on direct bulk
canonical decomposition, constraint-matrix completion, or corner-charge
construction. No sibling derivation was inspected before this first pass.

## Target claim

The target is a **conditional classification theorem**, not a promotion of
`C016`:

1. identify the total local Lorentz moment maps which a complete cut theory
   must possess;
2. determine whether reduction of the shell block is possible at zero or at a
   nonzero moment-map level;
3. give necessary and locally sufficient conditions for all five
   presymplectic pairs to be gauge;
4. bound the rank left when those conditions fail, without assuming a regular
   level or a free action.

All statements are exact in the frozen local regime. There is no perturbative
expansion.

## Derivation

### 1. Group actions and interface moment maps

Let independent persistent frame transformations be `(h_+,h_-) in G_+ x
G_-`. Exactly,

\[
 g\mapsto h_+gh_-^{-1},\qquad u\mapsto h_-u,
 \qquad v=gu\mapsto h_+v. \tag{8}
\]

Thus the **core action** moves both `g` and `v` on the left; the **exterior
action** moves `g` on the right and `u` on the left while leaving `v`
invariant. Contracting (6) gives

\[
 J^I_+=Q+\lambda=:\Pi,
 \qquad J^I_-=-\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\lambda. \tag{9}
\]

On the shell,

\[
 \boxed{J^I_+=0,\qquad J^I_-=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}Q\ne0.} \tag{10}
\]

The **matched diagonal action** has infinitesimal parameters obeying
`alpha_+=Ad_g alpha_-`, so `delta g=0`; it still changes `u` and therefore the
relative variable in (7). The complementary **relative action** changes the
matching frame `g`. Which linear combination is gauge is not fixed by the
interface action: it depends on the two bulk Gauss actions and their boundary
improvements.

There is also a possible **seed-frame right action**

\[
 u\mapsto u s^{-1},\qquad a_0\mapsto s a_0s^{-1}-ds\,s^{-1}. \tag{11}
\]

If `a_0` is frozen and not transformed, only its covariant stabilizer is a
symmetry of one phase space. If (11) is promoted to a Hamiltonian action, it
requires an additional seed moment map `J_0`; its interface contribution is,
up to the stated right-action sign convention,
`-Ad_{u^{-1}}Ad_{g^{-1}}Q`. Merely writing the Stueckelberg formula for `a`
does not supply `J_0`, a seed symplectic form, or a differentiable generator.

### 2. Bulk moment maps that must exist

For a complete canonical vote, there must be presymplectic forms
`Omega_+^bulk`, `Omega_-^bulk` and differentiable Hamiltonians for allowed
Lorentz parameters such that

\[
 \iota_{X_{\alpha_+}}\Omega_+^{\rm bulk}
   =\delta\langle J_+^{\rm bulk},\alpha_+\rangle,
 \qquad
 \iota_{X_{\alpha_-}}\Omega_-^{\rm bulk}
   =\delta\langle J_-^{\rm bulk},\alpha_-\rangle . \tag{12}
\]

Their total cut moment maps must be

\[
 \boxed{
 J_+^{\rm tot}=J_+^{\rm bulk}+\Pi,
 \qquad
 J_-^{\rm tot}=J_-^{\rm bulk}
        -\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\lambda .} \tag{13}
\]

Consequently, on (1), zero total Lorentz charge requires the **boundary
levels**

\[
 \boxed{J_+^{\rm bulk}=0,
 \qquad J_-^{\rm bulk}=-\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}Q
 =-P^- .} \tag{14}
\]

The last equality uses exact momentum matching. Its sign is the D005 incident
orientation sign. Equation (14) states the minimum required level; it does not
prove that the exterior Palatini generator realizes it. In particular:

1. a formal bulk Gauss density must exist;
2. its smeared generator must be differentiable under the chosen cut/corner
   boundary conditions;
3. its improvement must produce the boundary term in (14), with the same
   normalization and dressing;
4. the core degenerate-rank limit must admit the first equation in (14);
5. the two generators must close with the matching/seed action.

Failure at any one step prevents a gauge classification even though the
Lagrangian is Lorentz covariant.

### 3. Planar `Q` stabilizer and `rank(dQ)=5`

In the adapted planar frame, the cut has

\[
 Q=qT_{03}^*,\qquad q={b\over2}\,dx^1\wedge dx^2\ne0. \tag{15}
\]

The coadjoint stabilizer of this non-null simple bivector has Lie algebra

\[
 \mathfrak g_Q=\operatorname{span}\{T_{03},T_{12}\},
 \qquad \dim\mathfrak g_Q=2, \tag{16}
\]

because the boost in the `03` plane and rotation in its orthogonal `12` plane
commute with `T03`; the other four generators move its coadjoint orbit.
Therefore the coadjoint orbit has dimension four.

The image of `Q(e)` is locally larger than that orbit by one radial/area
direction. Exact planar linearization gives

\[
 \operatorname{rank}(dQ)=4_{\rm orbit}+1_{\rm area}=5. \tag{17}
\]

The annihilator of `im(dQ)` is only
`span{T12}`. The `T03` direction pairs with the radial variation of `q` even
though it stabilizes the fixed value of `Q`. This distinction explains both
facts in D006: the polarized block has five pairs, while a fixed nonzero
coadjoint value has a two-dimensional stabilizer.

### 4. Reduction at zero versus nonzero level

**Exact obstruction for interface-only zero reduction.** From (10) and
`Q ne 0`,

\[
 (J_-^I)^{-1}(0)=\varnothing \tag{18}
\]

on the rank-three branch. Thus quotienting the interface by the exterior
Lorentz group at zero either deletes the desired branch or, if one imposes
`Q=0`, collapses to the rank-at-most-one coframe limit. It cannot classify the
rank-ten block as gauge.

**Nonzero interface level.** Fixing `J_-^I=mu ne0` makes only `G_mu`
preserve that level. On the clean planar orbit type, four components of the
full Lorentz group move `mu` and therefore are not gauge transformations within
the fixed-level sector. The radial pair is also not supplied with a zero
constraint by fixing a coadjoint orbit alone. In the local polarized model,
fixing the full value of `Q` and quotienting its effective stabilizer leaves a
four-dimensional coadjoint-orbit sector (two pairs). This count is conditional
on the clean orbit type; at singular values it becomes a stratified bound, not
a manifold formula. In particular, a nonzero interface charge is not erased
by calling its stabilizer gauge.

**Zero total level.** The only candidate capable of gauging all five pairs is
the product reduction

\[
 ({\cal M}_-\times {\cal M}_{\rm match}\times {\cal M}_{\rm St})
 \sslash_{0}G_- ,\qquad
 J_-^{\rm bulk}+J_-^I=0, \tag{19}
\]

with the nonzero matching (14). Here the *total* moment map is zero while its
bulk and interface summands are separately nonzero. This is the Hamiltonian
meaning of cancellation by the exterior Gauss boundary momentum. A promoted
seed-frame reduction can play the same role only if its own phase space and
opposite level are supplied; a fixed seed cannot.

### 5. Dimension and rank bounds, including singular orbits

Let `r` be the rank of the admissible total-gauge orbit projected onto the five
relative configuration directions of (7), and let `c` be the number of
independent, differentiable total moment-map equations transverse to their
five momentum directions. Then

\[
 0\le r\le5,\qquad 0\le c\le5. \tag{20}
\]

Each usable first-class generator removes at most one configuration and one
momentum direction. Therefore, without regularity,

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{rank}\Omega_{\rm survivor}
 \ge 10-2\min(r,c),\qquad
 0\le\operatorname{rank}\Omega_{\rm survivor}\le10.} \tag{21}
\]

The lower bound is saturated only when the constraint and orbit directions
pair transversely and introduce no mixed bulk/interface survivor. If a clean
Hamiltonian action has `c=r`, the familiar local count is

\[
 \operatorname{rank}\Omega_{\rm survivor}=10-2r. \tag{22}
\]

The structural `T12` annihilator means a six-generator Lorentz action has at
most five effective directions on this block. Thus all ten directions can be
removed only at the maximal projected rank `r=c=5`. Stabilizer enhancement,
failure of differentiability, a corner restriction, or a singular moment-map
level lowers `r` or `c` and leaves at least two directions for every missing
effective generator. These are rank bounds, not assertions that the survivors
are automatically physical; they may mix with bulk constraints or become
second class.

The standard smooth formula

\[
 \dim(M\sslash_\mu G)=\dim M-2\dim G+2\dim G_\mu \tag{23}
\]

is quoted only for a regular free clean reduction of an ordinary symplectic
`M`. It must not be applied blindly to (7), which is a shell-pulled
presymplectic block with a structural null and field-dependent image.

### 6. Necessary and locally sufficient conditions for all five pairs to be gauge

The following are **necessary**:

1. `Omega_+^bulk` and `Omega_-^bulk` exist on the declared rank-changing cut,
   and (12) has differentiable generators for the same admissible parameters.
2. Their boundary improvements give exactly (13)--(14), including sign,
   dressing, `b=kappa_4^{-1}`, and no uncancelled corner term.
3. Five independent gauge vectors project onto the five relative
   Stueckelberg directions modulo the `T12` structural null: `r=5`.
4. Five independent total moment equations vary oppositely to all five
   components of `dQ`: `c=5`. The bulk moment map must therefore be able to
   absorb not only the four coadjoint-orbit variations but also the radial area
   variation.
5. The generators close as first class on the matching shell and preserve
   `Delta e=Delta omega=0`, `lambda=-Q`, `mu=0`, and `H(K)=0`.
6. The relevant exterior/diagonal or seed transformation belongs to one phase
   space rather than mapping between fixed-reference problems.
7. Allowed parameters have zero **total** corner charge. Compact support,
   fixed corner frame, or a justified improvement may achieve this, but these
   are different polarizations.

A useful **locally sufficient package** is: (i) a clean invariant shell near
the planar point; (ii) a Hamiltonian `G_-` action with total zero level (19);
(iii) projected orbit and transverse moment-map ranks both five; (iv) kernel of
the pulled total two-form equal exactly to the five generated gauge directions
plus already-known parametrization nulls; and (v) a zero-charge boundary
polarization. Under these assumptions, local coisotropic reduction removes
`2x5=10` ranks and no Stueckelberg pair survives. This is a conditional theorem,
not evidence that the required bulk structure exists.

### 7. Explicit counterexamples to an interface-only vote

**Counterexample A: zero-moment declaration.** Take precisely (7) with no
bulk factor. Its exterior moment map is (10). Zero reduction has no
rank-three point by (18). Calling the Lorentz action gauge forces the excluded
`Q=0` branch. Covariance has not supplied a gauge quotient.

**Counterexample B: spectator bulk.** Let the omitted bulk be any symplectic
space on which `G_-` acts trivially, so `J_-^bulk=0`. The interface form and its
rank remain exactly those of C016, but (14) fails and the nonzero charge remains.
Thus the interface matrix does not imply gauge removal.

**Counterexample C: cancelling cotangent bulk.** Locally append a cotangent
Hamiltonian `G_-` factor with momentum `p` and total moment map

\[
 J_-^{\rm tot}=p+\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}Q . \tag{24}
\]

The zero level `p=-Ad_{g^{-1}}Q` is nonempty and permits a diagonal reduction.
If the clean maximal-rank conditions hold, it removes the relative five-pair
block, transferring the matching to bulk momentum. Counterexamples B and C
have identical interface data and opposite canonical outcomes. Hence no
interface-only vote is logically sufficient.

**Counterexample D: transforming fixed seed.** A fixed `a_0` may transform
geometrically across a family of problems while not being a phase-space
variable. Then (11) is covariance, not a Hamiltonian gauge action, and no
seed moment map exists. The same local action formula therefore does not decide
whether a seed-frame quotient is allowed.

## Boundary and corner dependence

For parameters compactly supported away from every boundary component, local
corner integrals vanish, but the cut form (7) does not. If parameters are
nonzero on a temporal cut, the interface contribution is

\[
 {\cal Q}_I[\alpha_-]
 =\int_C\langle\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}Q,\alpha_-\rangle. \tag{25}
\]

It is generically nonzero. The total generator is gauge only if a
differentiable bulk improvement contributes its negative, or if the boundary
condition excludes that parameter. If `partial C` is present, lateral corner
terms and possible counterterms must also be specified. Fixing `u` at temporal
endpoints removes endpoint variations but does not by itself null the interior
rank-ten block. Changing the corner potential can change the Hamiltonian
realization and charge assignment, but cannot eliminate the requirement to
derive (13)--(14).

## Limiting-case checks

2. **Sign/normalization.** The plus interface term in `J_-^I` follows from
   `lambda=-Q` in (9); cancellation therefore requires the minus sign in (14).
   Reversing the cut or circle orientation reverses both momenta and charges,
   not the ranks.
3. **Lorentz symmetry.** Stabilizer dimensions and ranks are invariant under
   conjugation. The adapted `Q03` representative is a frame choice, not a time
   gauge or symmetry breaking.
4. **Degenerate limit.** `Q->0` makes zero interface reduction nonempty and
   destroys the rank-ten block, but it is the excluded rank-at-most-one branch.
5. **No-Stueckelberg limit.** Fixing/removing `u` deletes (7); it changes the
   phase space and is not a proof that the original block was gauge.
6. **`K=0` and `H(K)=0`.** Neither affects `Q`, (7), or the moment levels.
   The six-dimensional `H` kernel therefore cannot settle this classification.
7. **Conservation/closure.** A vanishing total moment map would express Lorentz
   momentum balance across the shell. Conservation is conditional on closure
   and differentiability of the undeclared bulk generators, not established
   here.

## Conclusion

**Exact local derived result.** The C016 rank-ten block cannot be removed by
interface-only reduction at zero moment map because its exterior interface
moment is `Ad_(g^-1)Q ne0`. Reduction at a fixed nonzero interface level retains
a stabilizer/orbit sector and is a charge-sector construction, not a proof of
gauge. The only candidate capable of removing all five pairs is a total
bulk-plus-interface zero-level reduction whose separately nonzero levels obey

\[
 \boxed{J_+^{\rm bulk}=0,\qquad
 J_-^{\rm bulk}=-\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}Q=-P^-.} \tag{26}
\]

All five pairs are gauge only if five differentiable first-class generators
project onto the five relative directions, five transverse moment equations
include the radial area direction, the matching shell is preserved, and the
total corner charge vanishes. With projected ranks `(r,c)`, the surviving
block obeys `rank >=10-2 min(r,c)`; complete removal requires `r=c=5`.
Regularity and freeness were not assumed.

**Minimum complete datum.** Derive the core and exterior cut presymplectic
potentials, differentiable Gauss generators, and their boundary improvements;
verify (13)--(14), first-class closure, orbit rank five, and the chosen
temporal/lateral corner polarization. For a seed-frame alternative, also
derive its Hamiltonian space and `J_0`. Until those data exist, classification
must stop: interface-only examples realize both survival and removal.
