# D021: Profile-independent endpoint curvature lemma

## Question addressed

Does a positive proper-normal circle radius attached to an open collapsed side
with `rho(0)=rho'_+(0)=0` necessarily have unbounded `rho''/rho` near the
endpoint, under the weakest classical regularity useful for the current F0
geometry, and what part of that statement is coordinate invariant?

## Branch charter

Prove or refute a sharp one-sided theorem for rho in C^1([0,epsilon)) intersect C^2((0,epsilon)), rho(0)=rho'_+(0)=0, rho(s)>0 on the punctured side: determine whether rho''/rho can be locally bounded above or in absolute value. State the minimal regularity actually used, give a direct differential-inequality or counterexample argument, and translate the result into the invariant orthonormal radial-circle sectional curvature for ds^2+rho(s)^2 dtheta^2 and for a regular lapse coordinate. Explicitly separate what follows (profile-independent unbounded curvature, if proved) from what does not (a limit/sign, stress boundedness, NEC, L1 integrability, matter realization, universal H003). Include dimensional, limiting, regularity, and singular-coordinate checks; expose only genuinely useful machine-check candidates; stop after the exact theorem/counterexample and scoped F0 implications.

## Target claim

Let `epsilon>0` and

\[
 \rho\in C^1([0,\epsilon))\cap C^2((0,\epsilon)),\qquad
 \rho(0)=\rho'_+(0)=0,\qquad \rho(s)>0\quad(0<s<\epsilon).       \tag{1}
\]

The target is to decide whether there can be `delta>0` and a finite constant
`M` such that either

\[
 {\rho''(s)\over\rho(s)}\le M                                  \tag{2}
\]

or the stronger `|rho''/rho|<=M` holds for every `0<s<delta`.
The sharp target also asks whether the answer implies a limit or eventual sign.

## Assumptions

1. Equation (1) is the entire analytic hypothesis. In particular, no equation
   of motion, monotonicity, convexity, analyticity, or infinite flatness is
   assumed.
2. `s` is outward proper distance on the opened side. The endpoint is `s=0`,
   and only `s>0` is used in ratios. A prime means `d/ds`.
3. The circle is spacelike and `theta` has period `2pi`. On the nondegenerate
   punctured side the radial-circle metric is
   `g_2=ds^2+rho(s)^2 dtheta^2`, with orthonormal coframe
   `E^3=ds`, `E^4=rho dtheta`.
4. Curvature follows `research/QUESTION.md` lines 72--81:
   `R^{AB}=d omega^{AB}+omega^A{}_C wedge omega^{CB}` and
   signature `(-,+,+,+,+)` in the five-dimensional application.
5. All conclusions are exact classical statements. There is no perturbative
   expansion, distributional endpoint equation, or assertion at the degenerate
   endpoint itself.

## Derivation

### 1. Comparison lemma

**Lemma (exact differential-inequality statement).** Let `u` satisfy the
endpoint regularity used in (1), with `u(0)=u'_+(0)=0` and `u>=0` on the
punctured interval. If, for some
`delta>0` and finite `M`,

\[
 u''(s)\le M u(s)\qquad(0<s<\delta),                            \tag{4}
\]

then `u(s)<=0` throughout `(0,delta)`.

**Proof.** Choose a constant

\[
 \lambda\ge0,\qquad \lambda^2\ge\max(M,0).                    \tag{5}
\]

When `u>=0`, (4) and (5) imply

\[
 u''-\lambda^2u\le (M-\lambda^2)u\le0.                        \tag{6}
\]

For the application below `u=rho>0`, so (6) holds on the entire punctured
interval. Differentiate the integrating-factor combination:

\[
 {d\over ds}\left[e^{-\lambda s}(u'+\lambda u)\right]
 =e^{-\lambda s}(u''-\lambda^2u)\le0.                          \tag{7}
\]

Because `u,u'` extend continuously to zero and both endpoint values vanish,

\[
 \lim_{s\downarrow0}e^{-\lambda s}(u'+\lambda u)=0.            \tag{8}
\]

Integrating (7) from `a>0` to `s` and then taking `a downarrow 0` gives

\[
 u'(s)+\lambda u(s)\le0.                                      \tag{9}
\]

A second exact integrating factor gives

\[
 {d\over ds}(e^{\lambda s}u)=e^{\lambda s}(u'+\lambda u)\le0. \tag{10}
\]

Taking the endpoint value in (10) yields
`e^{lambda s}u(s)<=u(0)=0`, hence `u(s)<=0`. This proves the
lemma in the nonnegative regime needed here. No uniqueness theorem, division by
`u`, or behavior at `s=0` of `u''` was used. \(\square\)

### 2. Profile-independent endpoint theorem

**Theorem (exact).** Under (1), `rho''/rho` is not bounded above on any
punctured endpoint neighborhood. Equivalently,

\[
 \boxed{\forall\delta\in(0,\epsilon),\quad
        \sup_{0<s<\delta}{\rho''(s)\over\rho(s)}=+\infty,}
 \qquad
 \boxed{\limsup_{s\downarrow0}{\rho''(s)\over\rho(s)}=+\infty.} \tag{11}
\]

Consequently `|rho''/rho|` cannot be bounded there either.

**Proof.** Suppose (2) held on `(0,delta)`. Positivity of `rho` converts it
without changing the inequality into

\[
 \rho''\le M\rho.                                               \tag{12}
\]

The lemma would force `rho<=0` on `(0,delta)`, contradicting (1). Thus no such
finite upper bound exists. Since this holds after every shrink of the endpoint
neighborhood, the extended-real `limsup` is `+infinity`. Absolute boundedness
would imply upper boundedness and is therefore also impossible. \(\square\)

This is stronger than merely saying that `r` is unbounded somewhere in the
whole interval: the positive excursions occur arbitrarily close to the
endpoint. It is weaker than `r(s)->+infinity`.

### 3. Sharpness: no limit and no eventual sign follow

To show that (11) is the sharp conclusion at this regularity, fix
`a=3/4` and positive length scales `ell,L`, and define for `s>0`

\[
 \rho_{\rm osc}(s):=\ell\exp\!\left[-(L/s)^2
                 +a\sin\!\big((L/s)^2\big)\right],\qquad
 \rho_{\rm osc}(0):=0.                                        \tag{13}
\]

The bounded oscillatory factor lies between `e^{-a}` and `e^a`. Every
derivative of (13) is the same exponentially decaying factor times a finite
sum of powers of `s^{-1}`, sines, and cosines. Hence its zero extension is in
fact `C^infinity` flat, so it obeys (1) with far more regularity than required.

Put

\[
 t:=(L/s)^2,
 \qquad \Phi:=\log(\rho_{\rm osc}/\ell)=-t+a\sin t.            \tag{14}
\]

Exact differentiation, using `t'=-2L^2s^{-3}`, gives

\[
 \Phi'=2L^2s^{-3}(1-a\cos t),                                  \tag{15}
\]

\[
 {\rho_{\rm osc}''\over\rho_{\rm osc}}
 =\Phi''+(\Phi')^2
 =4L^4s^{-6}\big[(1-a\cos t)^2-a\sin t\big]
  -6L^2s^{-4}(1-a\cos t).                                     \tag{16}
\]

Choose `alpha` with `sin alpha=4/5`, `cos alpha=3/5`, and let
`s_n=L(alpha+2pi n)^(-1/2)`. The dimensionless coefficient in square
brackets is

\[
 (1-9/20)^2-(3/4)(4/5)=121/400-240/400=-119/400<0.             \tag{17}
\]

Along this sequence, the leading term is
`4L^4s_n^(-6)(-119/400)`, while the second term is only of order
`L^2s_n^(-4)`; their magnitude ratio grows as `L^2/s_n^2`. Hence (16)
tends to `-infinity`. Along
`\tilde s_n=L(3pi/2+2pi n)^(-1/2)`, one has
`(sin t,cos t)=(-1,0)`, so the square bracket is
`1+a=7/4>0`; the same leading-versus-subleading comparison makes (16) tend to
`+infinity`. Therefore

\[
 \liminf_{s\downarrow0}{\rho_{\rm osc}''\over\rho_{\rm osc}}
 =-\infty,
 \qquad
 \limsup_{s\downarrow0}{\rho_{\rm osc}''\over\rho_{\rm osc}}
 =+\infty.                                                     \tag{18}
\]

Thus even `C^infinity` flatness does not imply an actual positive-infinity
limit, eventual positivity of `rho''/rho`, or eventual convexity. Those
stronger properties of the two D002 profiles come from their special rates.

### 4. Exact geometric translation in proper-normal gauge

On `s>0`, the Cartan calculation quoted from D019 is

\[
 \omega^{43}={\rho'\over\rho}E^4=\rho'd\theta,
 \qquad
 R^{34}=-{\rho''\over\rho}E^{34}=-\rho''ds\wedge d\theta.      \tag{19}
\]

Therefore the orthonormal radial-circle sectional component is exactly

\[
 \boxed{K_{s\theta}=R^{34}{}_{34}=-{\rho''\over\rho}.}         \tag{20}
\]

By (11), `K_{s theta}` is unbounded below on every punctured endpoint
neighborhood and `|K_{s theta}|` is unbounded:

\[
 \inf_{0<s<\delta}K_{s\theta}=-\infty,
 \qquad \sup_{0<s<\delta}|K_{s\theta}|=+\infty.                \tag{21}
\]

This is the direct profile-independent extension of the D019
`R^{34}=-r E^{34}` mechanism. Equation (21) concerns an orthonormal curvature
component on the nondegenerate side, not the potentially flat coordinate-form
coefficient `-rho''` and not a curvature tensor defined at `s=0`.

### 5. Regular lapse coordinates and invariance

Let `x in [0,eta)` be a regular radial coordinate with `x=0` at the endpoint,

\[
 g_2=N(x)^2dx^2+\rho(x)^2d\theta^2,
 \quad N\in C^1([0,\eta)),\quad N(0)>0,\quad N(x)>0.            \tag{22}
\]

After shrinking `eta`, continuity gives positive finite lower and upper bounds
on `N`. Define

\[
 s(x)=\int_0^xN(y)dy.                                          \tag{23}
\]

Then `s` is a `C^2`, orientation-preserving endpoint diffeomorphism and
`ds=Ndx`. On the punctured side the chain rule gives the exact identities

\[
 \rho_s={\rho_x\over N},\qquad
 \rho_{ss}={1\over N}{d\over dx}\left({\rho_x\over N}\right)
 ={1\over N^2}\left[\rho_{xx}-{N_x\over N}\rho_x\right].      \tag{24}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 {\rho_{ss}\over\rho}
 ={\rho_{xx}-(N_x/N)\rho_x\over N^2\rho},
 \qquad
 K_{s\theta}=-{\rho_{xx}-(N_x/N)\rho_x\over N^2\rho}.}       \tag{25}
\]

Sufficient coordinate regularity for transporting the theorem is precisely a
regular nondegenerate endpoint chart such as (22), together with
`rho in C^1` up to the endpoint and `C^2` inside in either coordinate. The
boundary data transform as

\[
 \rho_s(0+)={\rho_x(0+)\over N(0)},                             \tag{26}
\]

so zero slope is preserved when `0<N(0)<infinity`.

The raw ratio `rho_xx/rho` is not invariant: rearranging (24),

\[
 {\rho_{xx}\over\rho}
 =N^2{\rho_{ss}\over\rho}+{N_x\over N}{\rho_x\over\rho}.      \tag{27}
\]

It contains both a lapse scaling and an inhomogeneous connection term. Only
the combination (25) is the radial Hessian divided by `rho` and hence the
sectional curvature. A change with `N->0`, `N->infinity`, no finite nonzero
endpoint Jacobian, or insufficient differentiability can hide or manufacture
behavior in the raw coordinate ratio, but it is a singular reparametrization:
it is not an admissible regular endpoint chart, need not preserve (1), and
changes the regularity problem rather than evading (21).

### 6. Proper-normal uniqueness and orientation

Suppose `s` and `\tilde s` are both proper-normal coordinates on the same
connected radial interval. Equality of the radial line elements gives

\[
 ds^2=d\tilde s^2,
 \qquad {ds\over d\tilde s}=\pm1.                              \tag{28}
\]

Continuity makes the sign constant. Fixing the endpoint removes the additive
constant. With the same outward orientation, `\tilde s=s`; with reversed
orientation, `\tilde s=-s`. Thus proper-normal gauge is unique up to endpoint
translation and orientation reversal. Under reversal,
`rho_{\tilde s}=-rho_s` but `rho_{\tilde s\tilde s}=rho_{ss}`;
the zero-slope datum, `rho''/rho`, and `K_{s theta}` are unchanged. Reversing
the circle orientation `theta->-theta` flips both `E^4` and its coordinate
representatives but not the sectional curvature. There is no residual regular
radial gauge capable of removing (21).

## Minimal regularity actually used

For the stated **pointwise classical theorem**, the proof uses only:

\[
 \rho,\rho'\text{ continuous at }0;\quad
 \rho(0)=\rho'(0)=0;\quad
 \rho>0;\quad
 \rho''\text{ exists and }\rho''\le M\rho\text{ on }(0,\delta). \tag{29}
\]

Thus the supplied class `C^1([0,epsilon)) intersect C^2((0,epsilon))` is
sufficient and no higher endpoint derivative is used. Continuity of `rho''`
is not used except insofar as `C^2` makes the ratio a classical continuous
function on the punctured interval.

A strictly weaker standard formulation also works: `rho in C^1` up to the
endpoint, `rho' in AC_loc((0,epsilon))`, and
`rho''<=M rho` almost everywhere. Then (7)--(10) hold by absolute-continuity
integration on `[a,s]`, followed by `a downarrow0`. This weak extension is
not needed for (1), but identifies the actual analytic regularity consumed.

Both zero endpoint data are material. Positivity is used to pass from (4)--(5)
to (6); without it the comparison conclusion relevant to a radius is absent.

## Validity regime

The theorem is local to a one-sided positive circle radius in proper distance,
or to a regularly equivalent lapse chart. It applies to every D002
`C^infinity`-flat profile because those profiles satisfy much more than (1),
but flatness is unnecessary. It does not apply to an ordinary polar axis with
nonzero endpoint slope, to a nonpositive signed function, to a singular radial
coordinate treated as a new regularity category, or to a distributional
endpoint equation.

## Dimensional check

Let `mathsf L` denote the physical length dimension. With
`[s]=[x]=[rho]=[ell]=[L]=mathsf L`, dimensionless `theta`, and dimensionless
lapse when `x` has length dimension, both `(L/s)^2` and the full exponent in
(13) are dimensionless, while `rho_osc` has the required radius dimension.
Moreover,

\[
 [\rho_s]=1,\quad [\rho_{ss}]=\mathsf L^{-1},\quad
 [\rho_{ss}/\rho]=[M]=[\lambda^2]=\mathsf L^{-2},\quad
 [\lambda]=\mathsf L^{-1}.                                    \tag{30}
\]

The combinations `lambda s`, `e^{+-lambda s}`, and
`rho''-lambda^2 rho` are dimensionally consistent. In (25), `N_x/N` has
dimension `mathsf L^-1`, so both numerator terms have dimension
`mathsf L^-1`; division by `N^2 rho` gives `mathsf L^-2`, as required for
sectional curvature. In the repaired witness (16), both
`L^4s^-6` and `L^2s^-4` have dimension `mathsf L^-2`; the subsequences
`s_n` and `tilde s_n` have dimension `mathsf L`. If `x` were dimensionless,
`[N]=mathsf L`; the same invariant expression still has dimension
`mathsf L^-2`.

3. **Oscillatory flat profile.** Equations (13)--(18) obey every endpoint
   hypothesis while producing both signs without bound. This falsifies any
   attempted promotion of `limsup=+infinity` to a limit or eventual sign.
4. **Constant-ratio soluble equation.** If `rho''=M rho` with zero position and
   slope, elementary uniqueness gives `rho=0`; the integrating-factor proof
   recovers that conclusion without assuming equality or the sign of `M`.
5. **Endpoint data.** If only `rho(0)=0` is retained, the polar affine branch
   survives, matching D019's endpoint-rank warning. If positivity is removed,
   the theorem is no longer a circle-radius statement.
6. **Signs and normalization.** For `rho=s`, (19) gives `R^{34}=0`. For either
   D002 profile, positive divergent `rho''/rho` gives negative divergent
   `K_{s theta}`, exactly matching D019/D020. No factor of two appears because
   the coefficient of `E^{34}` in the curvature two-form equals
   `R^{34}{}_{34}` under the stated `1/2 R E wedge E` convention.
7. **Symmetry and coordinate check.** Proper-normal orientation reversal and
   circle-orientation reversal leave (20) invariant. Equation (25) transforms
   the coordinate Hessian into the same scalar sectional component.
8. **Conservation laws.** None is invoked. The endpoint theorem is kinematical;
   no effective tensor or field equation has been assumed whose divergence
   could be tested.

## Conclusion

**Exact derived result.** For every
`rho in C^1([0,epsilon)) intersect C^2((0,epsilon))` with
`rho(0)=rho'_+(0)=0` and `rho>0` on the punctured side,

\[
 \boxed{\limsup_{s\downarrow0}{\rho''(s)\over\rho(s)}=+\infty;}
\]

indeed the ratio is unbounded above on every endpoint neighborhood, so it
cannot be bounded in absolute value. The proof uses the exact inequality
`rho''<=M rho` and two integrating factors. The assumption can be weakened to
`C^1` endpoint data plus locally absolutely continuous first derivative and an
a.e. inequality.

Geometrically, in `ds^2+rho^2dtheta^2`,
`K_{s theta}=-rho''/rho` is therefore unbounded below in every punctured
endpoint neighborhood. In a regular lapse coordinate this same invariant is
the negative of
`[rho_xx-(N_x/N)rho_x]/(N^2 rho)`; raw `rho_xx/rho` is not invariant. Proper
distance is unique up to orientation, which leaves the result unchanged.

The dimensionally consistent oscillatory `C^infinity`-flat profile (13), with
positive radius scale `ell` and exponent scale `L`, has `liminf=-infinity` and
`limsup=+infinity`, proving that no actual `+infinity` limit or eventual sign
follows. Dropping the zero-slope datum admits the flat polar counterexample
`rho=s`.
