# D009: Vanishing regular five-dimensional Lorentz flux at circle collapse

flux at exact circle collapse that could cancel the C016 Stueckelberg moment
map?

## Derivation

## Five-dimensional Palatini potential and Lorentz gauge contraction

### Exact relations

Define the five-dimensional coframe momentum

\[
 P^{(5)}_{AB}:={1\over12\kappa_5}
 \epsilon_{ABCDE}E^C\wedge E^D\wedge E^E. \tag{7}
\]

The bare Palatini potential is exactly

\[
 \boldsymbol\Theta_5(\delta)
 =\delta\omega^{AB}\wedge P^{(5)}_{AB}. \tag{8}
\]

Its Lorentz contraction is therefore

\[
 \boldsymbol\Theta_5(\delta_\alpha)
 =-D\alpha^{AB}\wedge P^{(5)}_{AB}. \tag{9}
\]

Using invariance of the adjoint pairing gives the exact identity

\[
 -D\alpha^{AB}\wedge P^{(5)}_{AB}
 =-d\!\left(\alpha^{AB}P^{(5)}_{AB}\right)
   +\alpha^{AB}DP^{(5)}_{AB}. \tag{10}
\]

The connection equation is the Lorentz Gauss form

\[
 DP^{(5)}_{AB}=0. \tag{11}
\]

No inverse coframe or torsionless division is used in (7)--(11).

On a spatial cut, for field-independent `alpha`, contraction of the cut
presymplectic form gives, with `Omega=delta Theta`,

\[
 \Omega_{Y_+}(\delta,\delta_\alpha)
 =\delta\!\left[
   \int_{Y_+}\alpha^{AB}DP^{(5)}_{AB}
   -\int_{\partial Y_+}\alpha^{AB}P^{(5)}_{AB}
 \right]. \tag{12}
\]

Thus one compatible differentiable-generator convention is

\[
 G_5[\alpha]
 =\int_{Y_+}\alpha^{AB}DP^{(5)}_{AB}
  -\int_{\partial Y_+}\alpha^{AB}P^{(5)}_{AB}. \tag{13}
\]

Changing the convention for `Omega` reverses both terms. The invariant content
needed here is that the boundary polarization is the pullback of
`alpha P^(5)` and that the bulk term is proportional to `DP^(5)`.

### Persistent-sector momentum

For `alpha^{4a}=0`, the exact internal split of (7) is

\[
 \boxed{
 P^{(5)}_{ab}={1\over4\kappa_5}
 \epsilon_{abcd}E^c\wedge E^d\wedge E^4.} \tag{14}
\]

This is the generator analogue of the first channel in D004's Palatini
potential. Substituting (2), pulling to a positive-radius collar, and applying
fiber pushforward—which selects the vertical `dtheta` coefficient and
annihilates the horizontal `A` contribution—gives

\[
 p_!j_s^*P^{(5)}_{ab}
 ={2\pi\rho(s)\over4\kappa_5}
 \epsilon_{abcd}e_+^c\wedge e_+^d, \tag{15}
\]

\[
 p_!^Hj_s^*P^{(5)}_{ab}
 ={\rho(s)\over4\kappa_5}
 \epsilon_{abcd}e_+^c\wedge e_+^d. \tag{16}
\]

Equations (15)--(16) are exact at each `s>0`; they are not small-radius
expansions.

## Boundary pullback and circle pushforward

### 1. Bare regular core flux

At the actual collapsed boundary, `j_+^*E^4=0`. Hence (14) gives

\[
 \boxed{j_+^*P^{(5)}_{ab}=0,\qquad
 p_!j_+^*P^{(5)}_{ab}=p_!^Hj_+^*P^{(5)}_{ab}=0.} \tag{17}
\]

This is also an immediate instance of D003's regular equalizer theorem: the
three-form `P^(5)_ab` needs a vertical leg on `C x S1`, and its regular
vertical coefficient is proportional to `rho`, hence zero and flat.

Consequently the persistent core boundary term in (13) is exactly

\[
 \boxed{G_{5,\partial}[\alpha^{ab}]=0} \tag{18}
\]

for every regular basic persistent gauge parameter in the frozen class.
Normalized Haar integration removes a dimensionless `2pi`; it cannot turn the
zero in (17) into a finite nonzero trace.

### 2. Profile-renormalized trace

Fixing a particular defining profile and a length `ell_*`, one can instead
*define* on a restricted one-sided domain

\[
 \widehat P^{+}_{ab}
 :=\ell_*\lim_{s\downarrow0}{1\over\rho(s)}
 p_!j_s^*P^{(5)}_{ab}. \tag{19}
\]

Equation (15) then yields the finite conditional value

\[
 \boxed{\widehat P^{+}_{ab}
 ={2\pi\ell_*\over4\kappa_5}
 \epsilon_{abcd}e_+^c\wedge e_+^d.} \tag{20}
\]

For normalized Haar, the `2pi` is absent. This is not the ordinary or
normalized trace of the regular equalizer: it adds (i) a selected flat
generator `rho`, (ii) divisibility/domain conditions, and (iii) the length
`ell_*`. The D002 profiles obey `rho_2/rho_1 -> 0`, so changing the selected
profile can change the domain and value. Therefore (20), if adopted, is a
**new generalized bulk-trace datum**. It is not already contained in the
frozen regular bulk phase space.

### 3. Retained boundary-circle incidence current

D005 takes a different route. It retains `eta` and the marked circle as
independent incidence data and includes the interface primitive

\[
 B_a={\gamma\over4}\int_{\widetilde\Sigma}
 \epsilon_{abcd}K^{ab}\wedge e_+^c\wedge e_+^d\wedge\eta. \tag{21}
\]

Its connection momentum is

\[
 \boxed{Q_{ab}={b\over4}\epsilon_{abcd}e_+^c\wedge e_+^d,
 \qquad b=2\pi\gamma.} \tag{22}
\]

This current contains no `rho` and is finite because the unscaled boundary
circle form `eta` is retained rather than obtained as the trace of
`E^4=rho eta`. It is therefore **already D005 interface data**, not a new bare
bulk trace. It lies outside the D002 regular equalizer because `eta` does not
descend.

The formulas (20) and (22) become algebraically equal only after the additional
identification

\[
 \gamma={\ell_*\over\kappa_5},\qquad
 b={2\pi\ell_*\over\kappa_5}. \tag{23}
\]

That equality does not make their constructions equivalent: (20) uses a
profile-divided bulk trace, while (22) uses profile-independent enriched
boundary-circle data. Including both as independent contributions would in
general double count rather than provide a cancellation mechanism.

## Limiting-case checks

On the spatial planar cut `C`, (15) has only the component

\[
 \left(p_!P^{(5)}_{03}\right)_C
 ={\pi\rho(s)\over\kappa_5}
 dx^1\wedge dx^2, \tag{24}
\]

whereas D006 has

\[
 Q_{03,C}={b\over2}dx^1\wedge dx^2. \tag{25}
\]

The following controls are exact.

1. **Collapse limit.** For bounded regular fields,
   `p_!P^(5)_03=O(rho)` and tends to zero. For the D002 flat profiles it tends
   to zero faster than every power of the normal coordinate. At `s=0` it is
   exactly zero, not merely asymptotically small.
2. **Normalized circle.** Equation (24) becomes
   `rho/(2 kappa_5) dx1 wedge dx2`; it still vanishes.
3. **Fixed radius.** If `rho=R>0` on an ordinary product-circle boundary,

   \[
   p_!P^{(5)}_{ab}={2\pi R\over4\kappa_5}
   \epsilon_{abcd}e^c\wedge e^d. \tag{26}
   \]

   With `kappa_4=kappa_5/(2pi R)`, this equals
   `P^-_{ab}`. This reproduces standard Kaluza--Klein normalization and checks
   the coefficient and sign relative to the chosen incidence orientation.
4. **Renormalized control.** Dividing (24) by `rho` and multiplying by
   `ell_*` gives `pi ell_*/kappa_5` times the area form, matching (25) only
   under (23).
5. **Degenerate-area control.** If the induced area bivector vanishes, both
   (20) and `Q` vanish. This is the rank-at-most-one branch excluded by C016's
   planar rank-three target.
6. **`H(K)=0` control.** The core momentum (14) contains no `K`. Hence the
   six-dimensional kernel of `H` does not alter (17), just as it does not
   alter D006's rank-ten block.

## Can a core Gauss term cancel the D006 edge moment map?

The answer is exact and negative **within the frozen regular class**.

First, the volume part of (13) is the constraint
`int alpha DP^(5)`. On the core Gauss shell it vanishes. Off shell it is a
bulk constraint density, not a boundary current proportional to `Q`. Its
integration by parts produces only the boundary polarization (14), whose
regular collapsed trace is zero by (17).

Second, the D006 residual Stueckelberg moment map is

\[
 J_-^B=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}Q_C \tag{27}
\]

on `lambda=-Q`. It belongs to the exterior/seed-frame transformation channel.
The core persistent edge moment map is already zero on the same shell. A zero
core flux cannot cancel the nonzero (27), and independent core/exterior gauge
parameters cannot be paired by fiat.

Third, the shell relation `Q=g\cdot P^-` identifies (27) with the dressed
**exterior** Palatini momentum. Thus the adjacent candidate cancellation is an
exterior bulk Gauss boundary term with the correct declared polarization, as
D006 anticipated. Establishing its sign, differentiability, and first-class
status requires the exterior bulk cut potential and total generator; this
branch does not derive that system. The regular five-dimensional core supplies
no missing term.

A cancellation attributed to the core is possible only after leaving the
frozen class by adopting either the new generalized trace (19) or an enriched
boundary-circle current such as (21). In the latter case the datum is already
the D005 interface current that produces `Q`; it must not be relabeled as a
previously omitted bare core Gauss flux.
