# D004: Palatini boundary mismatch at smooth dimensional collapse

## Target claim

This artifact performs the fixed-interface first variation that was left open
by `D002` and `D003`. It proposes two sharply restricted claims for director
allocation; `C012` and `C013` are not added to the ledger here.

**Proposition P (bare Palatini potential mismatch; proposed C012).** On the
resolved closures

\[
M_+=U\times[0,\epsilon)\times S^1,
\qquad M_-=U\times(-\epsilon,0], \tag{1}
\]

equip five-dimensional and four-dimensional Einstein--Cartan/Palatini gravity
with the explicit actions and orientations below. Restrict the five-dimensional
fields and tangent variations to the exact `C^infty` quotient-equalizer class
of `D002`, with an adapted coframe
`E^4=rho(dtheta+A)` and horizontal persistent coframes `E^a`. Then the ordinary
and normalized-circle pushforwards of the bare five-dimensional Palatini
boundary potential vanish at `Sigma`. The two possible vertical-leg channels
in the Einstein--Cartan potential vanish separately: the `E^4` channel has a
factor `rho(0)=0`, and the mixed-connection channel requires a vertical
coefficient of `delta omega^{4a}`, which the equalizer makes zero (indeed flat)
at the interface. The four-dimensional Palatini boundary potential is a
horizontal three-form and is generically nonzero. Thus the bare sum has an
uncancelled exterior potential under free regular variations.

The same zero-core-trace result extends to a polynomial Lovelock bulk term only
when its complete potential, including every curvature factor, is a smooth
descended four-form. A Lovelock curvature supplies an additional possible
vertical leg, also killed by that regularity. The result does not automatically
extend to non-descending connections/curvatures, transgression or boundary
terms, singular traces, or independent blow-up fields.

**Proposition Q (bounded completion classification; proposed C013).** For the
same fixed interface:

1. fixing the relevant boundary variations (or changing Palatini polarization
   with a boundary term and then fixing the coframe) makes the variation
   differentiable but supplies no transition equation;
2. a regular horizontal interface Lagrangian supplies ordinary surface Euler
   equations and can absorb the exterior potential, but it receives no regular
   disappearing-circle momentum. A pure multiplier that only equates the two
   persistent coframes and connections forces the nonzero exterior Palatini
   momentum to vanish and is incompatible with a rank-three induced coframe;
3. a `rho`-renormalized trace or retained boundary circle can supply a finite
   core momentum and a momentum-matching equation, but only after adding a
   dimensionful incidence normalization and extra domain/boundary data. Such a
   completion is profile-dependent in the `D002` flat ideal, or introduces
   boundary-circle/edge variables; unscaled `eta=E^4/rho` also has a singular
   nondegenerate-core norm.

These are exact propositions for a fixed `Sigma`, fixed regularity and the
specified action representatives. They are not a moving-interface theory, a
proof of field-space integrability for a renormalized trace, a constraint
analysis, or evidence for C002 or C003.

## Assumptions

## Derivation

### 1. Bare five- and four-dimensional actions

With the ordered-index convention above, the two actions are

\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
S_+[E,\omega]
={}&\int_{M_+}\left[
\frac1{12\kappa_5}\epsilon_{ABCDE}
R^{AB}\wedge E^C\wedge E^D\wedge E^E\\
&\hspace{32mm}
-\frac{\Lambda_5}{120\kappa_5}\epsilon_{ABCDE}
E^A\wedge E^B\wedge E^C\wedge E^D\wedge E^E
\right],\\[1mm]
S_-[e,\varpi]
={}&\int_{M_-}\left[
\frac1{4\kappa_4}\epsilon_{abcd}
{\cal R}^{ab}\wedge e^c\wedge e^d
-\frac{\Lambda_4}{24\kappa_4}\epsilon_{abcd}
e^a\wedge e^b\wedge e^c\wedge e^d
\right].
\end{aligned}} \tag{15}
\]

Equivalently, in either dimension,

\[
S_D=\frac1{2\kappa_D}\int_{M_D}\left[
\frac1{(D-2)!}\epsilon_{I_1\ldots I_D}
R^{I_1I_2}\wedge E^{I_3}\wedge\cdots\wedge E^{I_D}
-2\Lambda_D V_D\right]. \tag{16}
\]

Equation (15) is polynomial at coframe rank loss and contains no inverse
coframe. The cosmological terms contain no differentiated field and therefore
will not affect the boundary potentials.

### 2. Exact first variation in general dimension

Set

\[
\Sigma^{(D)}_{IJ}:=\frac1{(D-2)!}
\epsilon_{IJ K_1\ldots K_{D-2}}E^{K_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge E^{K_{D-2}},
\qquad P^{(D)}_{IJ}=\frac1{2\kappa_D}\Sigma^{(D)}_{IJ}. \tag{17}
\]

The exact identities needed for the variation are

\[
\delta R^{IJ}=D\delta\omega^{IJ}, \tag{18}
\]

\[
\delta\Sigma^{(D)}_{IJ}
=\frac1{(D-3)!}\epsilon_{IJ K L_1\ldots L_{D-3}}
\delta E^K\wedge E^{L_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge E^{L_{D-3}}, \tag{19}
\]

\[
D\Sigma^{(D)}_{IJ}
=\frac1{(D-3)!}\epsilon_{IJ K L_1\ldots L_{D-3}}
T^K\wedge E^{L_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge E^{L_{D-3}}, \tag{20}
\]

and

\[
D\delta\omega^{IJ}\wedge\Sigma^{(D)}_{IJ}
=d\!\left(\delta\omega^{IJ}\wedge\Sigma^{(D)}_{IJ}\right)
+\delta\omega^{IJ}\wedge D\Sigma^{(D)}_{IJ}. \tag{21}
\]

The plus sign in the last bulk term follows because
`D(delta omega wedge Sigma)=D delta omega wedge Sigma-delta omega wedge D Sigma`.
Substitution into (16) gives the exact bulk equations

\[
\boxed{\frac1{(D-3)!}\epsilon_{IJKL_1\ldots L_{D-3}}
R^{JK}\wedge E^{L_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge E^{L_{D-3}}
-\frac{2\Lambda_D}{(D-1)!}\epsilon_{I J_1\ldots J_{D-1}}
E^{J_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge E^{J_{D-1}}=0,} \tag{22}
\]

\[
\boxed{D\Sigma^{(D)}_{IJ}=0,} \tag{23}
\]

and the presymplectic potential current

\[
\boxed{\boldsymbol\Theta_D(\delta)
=\delta\omega^{IJ}\wedge P^{(D)}_{IJ}
=\frac1{2\kappa_D(D-2)!}\epsilon_{IJ K_1\ldots K_{D-2}}
\delta\omega^{IJ}\wedge E^{K_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge E^{K_{D-2}}.} \tag{24}
\]

No integration by parts of `delta E` occurs, so the bare potential has no
`delta E` term.

### 3. Explicit bulk equations and potentials

For `D=5`, equations (22)--(24) become

\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
{\cal G}^{(+)}_A
&=\frac1{4\kappa_5}\epsilon_{ABCDE}
R^{BC}\wedge E^D\wedge E^E
-\frac{\Lambda_5}{24\kappa_5}\epsilon_{ABCDE}
E^B\wedge E^C\wedge E^D\wedge E^E=0,\\
{\cal C}^{(+)}_{AB}
&=\frac1{4\kappa_5}\epsilon_{ABCDE}
T^C\wedge E^D\wedge E^E=0,\\
\boldsymbol\Theta_5(\delta)
&=\frac1{12\kappa_5}\epsilon_{ABCDE}
\delta\omega^{AB}\wedge E^C\wedge E^D\wedge E^E.
\end{aligned}} \tag{25}
\]

For `D=4`, they are

\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
{\cal G}^{(-)}_a
&=\frac1{2\kappa_4}\epsilon_{abcd}{\cal R}^{bc}\wedge e^d
-\frac{\Lambda_4}{6\kappa_4}\epsilon_{abcd}
e^b\wedge e^c\wedge e^d=0,\\
{\cal C}^{(-)}_{ab}
&=\frac1{2\kappa_4}\epsilon_{abcd}t^c\wedge e^d=0,\\
\boldsymbol\Theta_4(\delta)
&=\frac1{4\kappa_4}\epsilon_{abcd}
\delta\varpi^{ab}\wedge e^c\wedge e^d
=\delta\varpi^{ab}\wedge P^-_{ab}.
\end{aligned}} \tag{26}
\]

On a nondegenerate coframe, the connection equations imply zero torsion, and
the coframe equations are equivalent to the Einstein equations with the stated
cosmological constants. In particular, a constant-curvature solution has
`R^{AB}=(Lambda_5/6)E^A wedge E^B` in five dimensions and
`R^{ab}=(Lambda_4/3)e^a wedge e^b` in four dimensions. This is a check on the
normalizations; invertibility is not used at the rank-changing interface.

For two commuting variations, define

\[
\boldsymbol\omega_D(\delta_1,\delta_2)
:=\delta_1\boldsymbol\Theta_D(\delta_2)
-\delta_2\boldsymbol\Theta_D(\delta_1). \tag{27}
\]

Then

\[
\boldsymbol\omega_D
=\delta_2\omega^{IJ}\wedge\delta_1P^{(D)}_{IJ}
-\delta_1\omega^{IJ}\wedge\delta_2P^{(D)}_{IJ}. \tag{28}
\]

On shell and for linearized solutions, `d boldsymbol omega_D=0` in either
ordinary stratum. This local identity does not supply an incidence law between
the two different-dimensional potentials.

### 4. Exact interface sign in the summed variation

Outer supports being absent, Stokes' theorem and (7) give

\[
\left.\delta(S_++S_-)\right|_{\rm boundary}
=\int_{\widetilde\Sigma,o_\Sigma\wedge d\theta}j_+^*\boldsymbol\Theta_5
-\int_{\Sigma,o_\Sigma}j_-^*\boldsymbol\Theta_4. \tag{29}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{\left.\delta(S_++S_-)\right|_{\rm boundary}
=\int_{\Sigma,o_\Sigma}\left(
p_!j_+^*\boldsymbol\Theta_5-j_-^*\boldsymbol\Theta_4\right).} \tag{30}
\]

If the entire five-dimensional action is normalized by `1/(2pi)`, then `p_!`
in (30) is replaced by `p_!^H`. That is a different relative action
normalization, not an identity that removes a factor from only the boundary.

### 5. Internal split of the five-dimensional Einstein--Cartan potential

Using `epsilon_abcd=epsilon_abcd4`, summing both orders of each antisymmetric
pair, and collecting the three placements of internal index `4` among the
coframes gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{\boldsymbol\Theta_5
=\frac1{4\kappa_5}\epsilon_{abcd}
\delta\omega^{ab}\wedge E^c\wedge E^d\wedge E^4
+\frac1{6\kappa_5}\epsilon_{abcd}
\delta\omega^{4a}\wedge E^b\wedge E^c\wedge E^d.} \tag{31}
\]

There are precisely two internal channels:

1. if the varied connection has only persistent indices `ab`, the internal
   epsilon forces one coframe to be `E^4`;
2. if the coframes have only persistent indices, the varied connection is
   mixed, `delta omega^{4a}`.

Since `E^a` is horizontal, a nonzero four-form on
`Sigma x S1` must contain one vertical spacetime leg. In the first channel it
can come from `E^4`; in the second it must come from the connection variation.
This exhausts the Einstein--Cartan term.

### 6. Equalizer test of both vertical-leg channels

#### 6.1 Vertical leg through `E^4=rho eta`

At fixed `s>0`, retain horizontal, theta-independent persistent variations for
the displayed coefficient. Pullback of the first term in (31) gives

\[
\frac{\rho(s)}{4\kappa_5}\epsilon_{abcd}
\delta\omega_H^{ab}\wedge e_+^c\wedge e_+^d\wedge(d\theta+A). \tag{32}
\]

The `A` part is a horizontal four-form on the three-dimensional `Sigma` and is
zero. Therefore ordinary fiber integration is exactly

\[
p_!\boldsymbol\Theta_{5,E^4}(s)
=\frac{2\pi\rho(s)}{4\kappa_5}\epsilon_{abcd}
\delta\omega_H^{ab}\wedge e_+^c\wedge e_+^d. \tag{33}
\]

At the actual interface this is not merely small:

\[
j_+^*E^4=\rho(0)j_+^*\eta=0,
\qquad
\boxed{p_!j_+^*\boldsymbol\Theta_{5,E^4}=0.} \tag{34}
\]

The conclusion also holds when `delta omega^{ab}` has an allowed vertical
component: every resulting term either contains the same factor `rho`, contains
two vertical one-forms and vanishes, or has a vertical coefficient that itself
vanishes by the equalizer.

#### 6.2 Vertical leg through a connection variation

For the displayed theta-independent mixed mode, write the variation near the
boundary as

\[
\delta\omega^{4a}=\delta w_H^{4a}+\delta\chi^{4a}d\theta. \tag{35}
\]

The horizontal part wedged with three horizontal coframes is a horizontal
four-form on `Sigma` and vanishes. Moving `dtheta` through three coframes to the
right gives one minus sign, so the exact fiber coefficient is

\[
p_!\boldsymbol\Theta_{5,\chi}(s)
=-\frac{2\pi}{6\kappa_5}\epsilon_{abcd}
\delta\chi^{4a}(s) e_+^b\wedge e_+^c\wedge e_+^d. \tag{36}
\]

The one-form equalizer requires `delta chi^{4a}=0` on the complete open region
`s<0`. Smoothness through `s=0` consequently gives

\[
\partial_s^n\partial_u^I\partial_\theta^m
\delta\chi^{4a}\big|_{s=0}=0
\quad\hbox{for all }n,I,m. \tag{37}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{p_!j_+^*\boldsymbol\Theta_{5,\chi}=0.} \tag{38}
\]

For a theta-dependent core mode, the left-hand side of (36) contains
`int_0^(2pi) delta chi(u,s,theta)dtheta` rather than `2pi delta chi`; every
Fourier coefficient is still flat at the interface, so (38) is unchanged.

Equations (34) and (38) prove directly that

\[
\boxed{p_!j_+^*\boldsymbol\Theta_5=0,
\qquad p_!^Hj_+^*\boldsymbol\Theta_5=0.} \tag{39}
\]

This agrees with, but is more component-explicit than, the general descended
four-form result C010. Because (39) is an identity on the declared field space,
its field-space exterior derivative also vanishes:

\[
p_!j_+^*\boldsymbol\omega_5=0 \tag{40}
\]

for tangent variations preserving the same equalizer class.

#### 6.3 The four-dimensional potential need not vanish

No vertical leg is needed for (26): it is already a three-form on `Sigma`.
For the planar coframe

\[
j_-^*(e^0,e^1,e^2,e^3)=(du^0,du^1,du^2,0) \tag{41}
\]

and the allowed variation `delta varpi^{03}=f(u)du^0`, direct summation over
`03,30` and `12,21` gives

\[
\boxed{j_-^*\boldsymbol\Theta_4
=\frac{f(u)}{\kappa_4}o_\Sigma,} \tag{42}
\]

which is nonzero for nonzero `f`. Combining (30), (39), and (42),

\[
\boxed{\left.\delta(S_++S_-)\right|_{\Sigma}
=-\int_{\Sigma}j_-^*\boldsymbol\Theta_4} \tag{43}
\]

for regular equalizer variations. Thus identifying persistent traces does not
make the bare potentials cancel. If `delta varpi^{ab}` is left arbitrary with
no interface action, stationarity would require

\[
j_-^*P^-_{ab}=0. \tag{44}
\]

For a rank-three induced coframe this is impossible for all pairs; for example,
`P^-_{03}` is proportional to `e^1 wedge e^2` and is nonzero in (41).

### 7. Exact scope of the extension to polynomial Lovelock terms

Use the explicit convention

\[
S_D^{\rm LL}=\frac1{2\kappa_D}
\sum_{p=0}^{\lfloor D/2\rfloor}
\frac{\alpha_{D,p}}{(D-2p)!}
\int\epsilon_{I_1\ldots I_D}
R^{I_1I_2}\wedge\cdots\wedge R^{I_{2p-1}I_{2p}}
\wedge E^{I_{2p+1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge E^{I_D}, \tag{45}
\]

where `alpha_(D,1)=1` and `alpha_(D,0)=-2 Lambda_D`. Varying any one of the
`p` curvature factors, using their even form degree and the Bianchi identity,
gives the exact bare potential

\[
\boxed{\boldsymbol\Theta_{D,p}^{\rm LL}
=\frac{p\alpha_{D,p}}{2\kappa_D(D-2p)!}
\epsilon_{I_1\ldots I_D}\delta\omega^{I_1I_2}
\wedge R^{I_3I_4}\wedge\cdots\wedge R^{I_{2p-1}I_{2p}}
\wedge E^{I_{2p+1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge E^{I_D}.} \tag{46}
\]

The `p=0` cosmological term has no potential. In five dimensions the only new
bulk Lovelock term is `p=2`, with

\[
\boldsymbol\Theta_{5,2}^{\rm LL}
=\frac{\alpha_{5,2}}{\kappa_5}\epsilon_{ABCDE}
\delta\omega^{AB}\wedge R^{CD}\wedge E^E. \tag{47}
\]

Unlike the Einstein--Cartan term, (47) has three possible sources for its one
required vertical spacetime leg: `E`, `delta omega`, or `R`. Decompose a regular
adjoint curvature as

\[
R^{AB}=r_H^{AB}+q_H^{AB}\wedge d\theta. \tag{48}
\]

The degree-two equalizer requires `q_H^{AB}=0` for `s<=0`, hence at `s=0`.
The `E` and `delta omega` channels vanish by the same arguments as (34) and
(38); the curvature channel vanishes by (48). With all factors horizontal, a
four-form on the three horizontal boundary directions is zero. Therefore

\[
\boxed{p_!j_+^*\boldsymbol\Theta_{5,p}^{\rm LL}=0} \tag{49}
\]

for every polynomial five-dimensional Lovelock term whose factors obey the
declared smooth equalizer.

There is also a short invariant proof: wedge products and invariant internal
contractions preserve the equalizer, so (46) is a smooth descended four-form;
C010 then forces its boundary trace to vanish. The component proof is retained
because it exposes the additional curvature channel.

Equation (49) has four important limits:

1. it does not say that a connection with non-descending vertical boundary data
   is excluded from every enriched theory;
2. it does not apply if `R` has an edge, distributional, or polyhomogeneous
   vertical coefficient;
3. Lovelock boundary, Chern--Simons, transgression, and polarization terms alter
   the potential and must be varied separately;
4. on the four-dimensional side the Euler (`p=2`) potential is proportional to
   `epsilon_abcd delta varpi^{ab} wedge R^{cd}` and can be a nonzero three-form,
   even though its four-dimensional bulk Euler equations are topological.

Thus polynomiality and absence of an inverse coframe are sufficient for
off-shell definability at rank loss, but not for cancellation of mixed-dimensional
boundary potentials.

### 8. Completion A: fixed variations or a fixed polarization

#### 8.1 Connection-Dirichlet (or fully fixed) data

Impose

\[
j_-^*\delta\varpi^{ab}=0 \tag{50}
\]

and retain the regular equalizer conditions on the core. Then (43) vanishes.
Fixing all persistent traces is stronger and has the same effect. The resulting
equations are exactly the bulk equations (25)--(26); there is **no** Euler
equation on `Sigma`. Boundary values are external data.

Fixing only the coframe does not make the bare Palatini action differentiable,
because (26) contains `delta varpi`, not `delta e`.

#### 8.2 Coframe-Dirichlet after changing polarization

On an ordinary boundary, add

\[
B_D=-\int_{\partial M_D}\omega^{IJ}\wedge P^{(D)}_{IJ}. \tag{51}
\]

Its exact variation changes (24) to

\[
\boldsymbol\Theta_D+\delta B_D
=-\omega^{IJ}\wedge\delta P^{(D)}_{IJ}. \tag{52}
\]

Then `delta E=0`, hence `delta P=0`, is sufficient. The raw term (51) is not
invariant under unrestricted boundary Lorentz transformations because a
connection transforms inhomogeneously. A covariant version requires a
reference connection `bar omega` and `omega-bar omega` in (51), or boundary
normal/edge data. Those are additional structures, not consequences of the
quotient.

**Boundary equations supplied by A:** none. A chooses a variational
polarization and boundary data. It can define separate initial-boundary
problems, but it neither derives persistent-field matching nor transports data
needed for reopening a circle.

### 9. Completion B: a regular horizontal interface Lagrangian

Let `psi` denote any regular fields intrinsic to `Sigma`, and let

\[
S_\Sigma=\int_\Sigma L_\Sigma(e_+,\omega_+^{ab};e_-,\varpi_-^{ab};\psi) \tag{53}
\]

be a horizontal, local three-form. Identifying `omega_+^{ab}` with a
four-dimensional connection representation already requires an identification
of the persistent `SO(1,3)` subbundle on the two sides. After tangential
integrations by parts and with no boundary of the support, write its exact
variation as

\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta S_\Sigma=\int_\Sigma\big[&
\delta e_+^a\wedge J^+_{e,a}
+\delta\omega_+^{ab}\wedge J^+_{\omega,ab}
+\delta e_-^a\wedge J^-_{e,a}\\
&+\delta\varpi_-^{ab}\wedge J^-_{\omega,ab}
+\delta\psi^r\wedge {\cal E}_{\psi,r}\big].
\end{aligned} \tag{54}
\]

The `J` are interface Euler forms, not assumed currents from either bulk. Using
(39) and (43), free boundary variations give

\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
J^+_{\omega,ab}&=0,& J^-_{\omega,ab}&=j_-^*P^-_{ab},\\
J^+_{e,a}&=0,&J^-_{e,a}&=0,& {\cal E}_{\psi,r}&=0.
\end{aligned}} \tag{55}
\]

These are genuine fixed-interface boundary equations if (53) is part of the
action. A regular horizontal interface theory can therefore absorb the
four-dimensional momentum in its `J^-_omega` equation. It does not receive an
opposite regular momentum from the disappearing circle: its core equation is
`J^+_omega=0`.

If `L_Sigma` contains time/tangential derivatives of `psi`, it has its own
presymplectic potential and introduces interface phase-space degrees. If it is
algebraic, `psi` can be auxiliary, but its values, couplings, and constraint
equations are still extra transition data.

#### 9.1 Exact failure of the pure equality multiplier

Consider the most economical algebraic matching action

\[
S_{\rm match}=\int_\Sigma\left[
\lambda_a\wedge(e_+^a-e_-^a)
+\lambda_{ab}\wedge(\omega_+^{ab}-\varpi_-^{ab})\right], \tag{56}
\]

where `lambda_a` and antisymmetric `lambda_ab` are two-forms. The normalization
uses the same ordered antisymmetric-pair sum as the bulk actions. Variation of
the multipliers gives

\[
e_+^a=e_-^a,
\qquad \omega_+^{ab}=\varpi_-^{ab}. \tag{57}
\]

The coframe variations give `lambda_a=0`. The connection currents in (54) are

\[
J^+_{\omega,ab}=\lambda_{ab},
\qquad J^-_{\omega,ab}=-\lambda_{ab}. \tag{58}
\]

Equations (55) then imply

\[
\lambda_{ab}=0,
\qquad j_-^*P^-_{ab}=0. \tag{59}
\]

The second equation contradicts the intended rank-three induced coframe, as in
(44). Thus a pure equality multiplier does not repair the bare mismatch. A
richer horizontal action must have asymmetric surface response or independent
interface fields. A regular polarization term can also cancel the exterior
`delta varpi` term, but then it is completion A in another guise and does not
couple a core fiber flux.

**Boundary equations supplied by B:** equations (55), plus matching equations
such as (57) when multipliers are included. They can terminate, reflect, or
source the four-dimensional connection data, but regular horizontal fields do
not by themselves restore a nonzero core incidence momentum. No determinism or
constraint closure follows from these Lagrangian boundary equations alone.

### 10. Completion C: blow-up/renormalized or boundary-circle data

This section is conditional. It shows exactly what equations an enriched trace
would make available; it does not prove that the trace is the variation of a
complete, gauge-invariant action under moving or profile-changing variations.

#### 10.1 Renormalizing the `E^4` channel

Fix a defining profile `rho` and a length `ell_*`. On the domain where the
one-sided limit exists, define

\[
\widehat{\boldsymbol\Theta}_{+,E}
:=\ell_*\lim_{s\downarrow0}\frac1{\rho(s)}
p_!\boldsymbol\Theta_{5,E^4}(s). \tag{60}
\]

Equation (33) gives the finite result

\[
\boxed{\widehat{\boldsymbol\Theta}_{+,E}
=\frac{2\pi\ell_*}{4\kappa_5}\epsilon_{abcd}
\delta\omega_+^{ab}\wedge e_+^c\wedge e_+^d.} \tag{61}
\]

Equivalently, this replaces the vanishing `E^4` boundary factor by the
non-descending blow-up form `eta=E^4/rho` and integrates its unit circle leg.
The factor `ell_*` is required dimensionally.

Add the connection part of (56) and now use (61) as the declared core incidence
potential. Define

\[
\widehat P^+_{ab}
:=\frac{2\pi\ell_*}{4\kappa_5}\epsilon_{abcd}
e_+^c\wedge e_+^d. \tag{62}
\]

The independent connection variations give

\[
\widehat P^+_{ab}+\lambda_{ab}=0,
\qquad -P^-_{ab}-\lambda_{ab}=0, \tag{63}
\]

while multiplier variation still gives connection matching. Eliminating the
multiplier yields the nontrivial momentum equation

\[
\boxed{\widehat P^+_{ab}=P^-_{ab}.} \tag{64}
\]

If the coframes are also matched, (64) requires

\[
\boxed{\ell_*\frac{2\pi}{\kappa_5}=\frac1{\kappa_4}.} \tag{65}
\]

For normalized Haar the `2pi` is absent. Equation (65) displays rather than
hides the extra relative length normalization.

#### 10.2 Renormalizing a vertical mixed-connection variation

The equalizer only says that `delta chi^{4a}` in (35) is flat. It does not say
that it is smoothly divisible by a selected flat `rho`. Restrict further to
the domain

\[
\chi^{4a}=\rho\,\zeta^{4a} \tag{66}
\]

with fixed `rho` and finite one-sided `zeta`. Dividing (36) by `rho` gives

\[
\boxed{\widehat{\boldsymbol\Theta}_{+,\chi}
=-\frac{2\pi\ell_*}{6\kappa_5}\epsilon_{abcd}
\delta\zeta^{4a}e_+^b\wedge e_+^c\wedge e_+^d.} \tag{67}
\]

With no interface action and free `delta zeta`, stationarity would set the
rank-three coframe volume in (67) to zero. If an interface action has
`delta L_Sigma=delta zeta^{4a} wedge J_{zeta,4a}+...`, its boundary equation is

\[
\boxed{J_{\zeta,4a}
=\frac{2\pi\ell_*}{6\kappa_5}\epsilon_{abcd}
e_+^b\wedge e_+^c\wedge e_+^d.} \tag{68}
\]

Thus this channel introduces a boundary scalar/mixed-connection trace (or
requires it to be fixed); it does not directly cancel the four-dimensional
`delta varpi^{ab}` potential because it is conjugate to a different variable.

If `rho` varies, `delta(chi/rho)=delta chi/rho-chi delta rho/rho^2`; equations
(66)--(68) are then incomplete. They are exact only in the fixed-profile
domain declared here.

#### 10.3 Independent boundary-circle data

Instead of division by a flat function, retain `widetilde Sigma`, its marked
circle, and `eta` as independent incidence data. One explicit regular boundary
functional that can supply the first term in (61) is

\[
S_\eta=\frac{\gamma}{4}\int_{\widetilde\Sigma}
\epsilon_{abcd}K^{ab}\wedge e_+^c\wedge e_+^d\wedge\eta,
\qquad K^{ab}:=\omega_+^{ab}-\bar\omega^{ab}, \tag{69}
\]

where `bar omega` is a reference connection transforming with `omega`, and
`gamma` has dimension `L^-2`. For theta-independent fields and fixed unit
vertical component of `eta`, fiber integration gives

\[
S_\eta=\frac{2\pi\gamma}{4}\int_\Sigma
\epsilon_{abcd}K^{ab}\wedge e_+^c\wedge e_+^d. \tag{70}
\]

Its connection current and coframe current are

\[
J^+_{\omega,ab}=\frac{2\pi\gamma}{4}
\epsilon_{abcd}e_+^c\wedge e_+^d, \tag{71}
\]

\[
J^+_{e,c}=-\pi\gamma\,\epsilon_{ab c d}K^{ab}\wedge e_+^d. \tag{72}
\]

The sign in (72) follows by combining the two coframe variations and moving
`delta e^c` past the one-form `K`. With connection matching, (71) can balance
the exterior Palatini momentum if

\[
2\pi\gamma=\kappa_4^{-1}. \tag{73}
\]

But (72) is then an additional coframe boundary equation unless the coframe is
fixed or other interface terms cancel it. If the coefficient of the vertical
part of `eta` is varied, (69) also gives
`epsilon_abcd K^{ab} wedge e^c wedge e^d=0`; if `eta` is fixed, this equation is
replaced by external boundary-circle data. The reference connection can be
replaced by an edge/Stueckelberg field, but that replacement is itself new
field content.

This route avoids selecting `rho_1` versus `rho_2`, but it retains precisely the
circle data erased by the bare quotient. It is therefore an enriched state
space, not a construction internal to the final diffeology.

#### 10.4 Profile dependence and singular norms

The flat ideal has no preferred generator. For the two exact profiles,

\[
\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}\longrightarrow0,
\qquad \frac{\rho_1}{\rho_2}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{74}
\]

Consequently

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{\rho_1}(\rho_1)=1,
\quad \operatorname{Tr}_{\rho_2}(\rho_1)=\infty,
\quad \operatorname{Tr}_{\rho_1}(\rho_2)=0,
\quad \operatorname{Tr}_{\rho_2}(\rho_2)=1. \tag{75}
\]

Thus a renormalized trace has a profile-dependent domain and value unless the
defining scale is promoted to physical data with a compensating transformation
law.

On the nondegenerate core metric

\[
g_+=\eta_{ab}e_+^a e_+^b+\rho^2\eta^2, \tag{76}
\]

one has

\[
g_+^{-1}(\eta,\eta)=\rho^{-2}. \tag{77}
\]

Hence `eta=E^4/rho` has divergent one-form norm as `rho downarrow0`. A
quadratic Yang--Mills-like norm for a finite vertical mode `chi eta` scales
locally as

\[
\int ds\,\rho\,|\chi\eta|^2
\sim\int ds\,\frac{\chi^2}{\rho}, \tag{78}
\]

which diverges for a nonzero finite `chi` for the flat profiles. If
`chi=rho zeta`, the same estimate scales as `int ds rho zeta^2` and can be
finite. The Palatini action itself contains no inverse metric, so (77)--(78)
are warnings about an eventual kinetic/symplectic norm, not a proof that every
polynomial gravitational completion diverges.

**Boundary equations supplied by C:** under the declared enriched incidence,
(64) is a genuine core/exterior momentum-matching equation; (68) is the source
equation for a renormalized mixed mode; and (71)--(73) show the corresponding
equations from one explicit boundary-circle functional. These equations come
with a selected length/profile or with marked circle/reference/edge data. Their
field-space integrability, gauge charges, and dynamical health remain open.

## Limiting-case checks

1. **Fixed nonzero circle radius.** If `rho=R>0` on a regular product boundary
   and mixed vertical variations vanish, (33) gives the standard reduction

   \[
   p_!\boldsymbol\Theta_{5,E^4}
   =\frac{2\pi R}{4\kappa_5}\epsilon_{abcd}
   \delta\omega^{ab}\wedge e^c\wedge e^d. \tag{83}
   \]

   It equals the four-dimensional potential when
   `kappa_4=kappa_5/(2pi R)`, the expected product-circle normalization.
   Exact collapse sends this regular coefficient to zero.
2. **Pure four-dimensional boundary.** Example (42) explicitly confirms that
   the exterior term survives with the sign in (43).
3. **Cosmological terms.** Setting either `Lambda` to zero changes no boundary
   formula. Keeping it gives the constant-curvature factors `Lambda_5/6` and
   `Lambda_4/3`, checking all factorials.
4. **Torsionless nondegenerate limit.** Equations (25)--(26) reduce to ordinary
   Einstein gravity. At rank loss, the weaker polynomial connection equations
   are retained rather than divided by a coframe determinant.
5. **Orientation reversal.** Reversing the circle orientation flips both
   `o_(partial M+)` and `p_!`; reversing the `s` coorientation exchanges the
   incidence signs. The physical statement that the two incident boundary
   contributions have opposite signs is unchanged.
6. **Gauge covariance.** Under a regular field-independent Lorentz frame
   transformation, `delta omega` transforms homogeneously and (24) is
   invariant. The split terms in (31) mix if the internal normal is changed,
   but their summed zero trace does not. The raw polarization term (51) fails
   this test without reference/edge data, as stated.
7. **Coordinate covariance.** Equations (15), (24), and (30) are form
   identities. A change of the adapted coordinates preserving orientation and
   the marked circle leaves them unchanged; forgetting that marking removes
   the data needed to define `p_!`, in agreement with C011.
8. **Lovelock `p=2`.** A vertical curvature coefficient would produce a finite
   fiber term in (47), confirming why it had to be checked separately. The
   exact equalizer makes that coefficient zero, yielding (49).
9. **Profile comparison.** Equations (74)--(75) reproduce the strict
   `rho_2/rho_1 -> 0` ordering and show that equal interface jets do not make a
   divided trace resolution-independent.
10. **Presymplectic conservation.** Each bulk current is closed on shell, but
   (40) and a generic nonzero exterior current leave no bare cross-interface
   cancellation. A completion must impose boundary conditions or carry the
   missing current; conservation is not inferred from bulk closure alone.
11. **Nondegenerate induced triad.** Conditions (44) or (59) fail already on
    the planar coframe because `e^1 wedge e^2` is nonzero. The multiplier
    obstruction is therefore not an artifact of a curved example.

## Unresolved concerns

- The bare potential is representative-dependent. A complete classification
  of Lorentz-covariant local boundary/transgression terms has not been made.
- A connection is not itself a tensorial quotient form. The result imposes the
  equalizer on `delta omega` and, for Lovelock, on curvature. A principal-bundle
  formulation of descent across the jumping fiber remains to be built.
- The interface identification of the five-dimensional persistent subbundle

  transformations and edge charges are unknown.

  coframe/profile variations analogous to (72), which must not be discarded.

  the smooth flat ideal is not principal.

**Exact restricted derived result (proposed C012).** The explicitly normalized

\[

\left(p_!j_+^*\boldsymbol\Theta_5-j_-^*\boldsymbol\Theta_4\right). \tag{84}

The internal split (31) shows all possible Einstein--Cartan vertical legs. The

\[
\boxed{p_!j_+^*\boldsymbol\Theta_5=0
\quad\hbox{while}\quad
j_-^*\boldsymbol\Theta_4\ \hbox{is generically nonzero}.} \tag{85}

For polynomial Lovelock bulk terms, (85)'s core half remains true only under
the explicit descended-curvature hypothesis; higher curvature adds a vertical
curvature channel, and the equalizer kills it. Boundary/transgression and
singular terms are not covered.

**Exact bounded classification (proposed C013).** Completion A removes the
