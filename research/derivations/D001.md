# D001: Constraint-rank diagnostic for a collapsing fiber

## Target claim

This bounded derivation asks for the cheapest exact canonical diagnostic that
separates:

1. removal of a compact resolution coordinate by a regular first-class gauge
   reduction; from
2. disappearance of a physical canonical pair only because the Legendre map
   loses rank at zero fiber radius, possibly erasing a charge or data needed to
   reopen the fiber.

The target result is a diagnostic, not a proof about gravity: on a resolved
rotor, compute the Legendre rank, complete constraint surface
`\mathcal C_\rho`, pulled-back presymplectic form, its kernel, and the compact
shift moment map `J`; then test whether reduction commutes with `\rho\to0` and
whether evolution through an open `\rho=0` interval is a single-valued
symplectic map on reduced data.

This is a necessary-condition analysis relevant to C003. It is **not evidence
that C003 is true or false**. In particular, a `0+1`-dimensional rotor has no
spatial principal symbol, gravitational connection sector, or interface
field content.

## Assumptions

### Physical and mathematical assumptions

- Time is an interval with coordinate `t`. Except in the explicit
  free-boundary subsection, endpoint configurations are fixed in the
  variational principle.
- The resolved configuration variables are a radius/order parameter
  `\rho\geq0` and an angle `\theta\sim\theta+2\pi`. A radial kinetic term is
  included so that approach to and departure from collapse can be discussed.
- The exact circle moment of inertia is
  `I(\rho)=\mu\rho^2`, with `\mu>0`. Thus the nonzero-radius kinetic form is
  positive; the zero-radius eigenvalue is zero, not negative.
- `U(\rho)` is `C^2` and finite near `\rho=0`. An identically collapsed
  solution without a reaction multiplier additionally requires `U'(0)=0`.
- Velocities are finite when the exact image of the Legendre map at
  `\rho=0` is stated. Finite-energy trajectories are used when deciding
  whether a fixed nonzero momentum sector can reach `\rho=0`.
- Two theories are kept distinct:
  - **P (physical/global rotor):** `A_0=0` (or is fixed background data), so
    constant shifts of `\theta` are a global symmetry and `J` is a physical
    conserved charge.
  - **G (gauged rotor):** `A_0(t)` is varied and has no kinetic term. The
    compact shift is a gauge symmetry and its Gauss equation is part of the
    action.
- Gauge parameters vanish at fixed temporal endpoints when deriving the
  action symmetry. At a canonical time, transformations generated by
  zero-charge first-class constraints are quotiented. Global temporal
  holonomies are excluded by taking the time domain to be an interval; this
  restriction is revisited under unresolved concerns.
- No perturbative expansion, numerical input, inverse coframe, or
  gravitational field equation is used.

### Conventions and boundary conditions

- Natural units `c=\hbar=1` are used, with `\hbar` restored once when noting
  compact-momentum quantization.
- The canonical symplectic convention is
  `\Omega=dq^i\wedge dp_i`, so `{q^i,p_j}=\delta^i{}_j`.
- `\approx0` denotes equality on a Dirac constraint surface.
- “Legendre rank” means the rank of the velocity Hessian. “Constraint-matrix
  rank” means the rank of Poisson brackets among constraints. “Presymplectic
  rank” means the rank of the pullback of `\Omega`. These are not conflated.
- A strict open collapsed phase means `\rho(t)=0` on an interval. A single
  crossing event and a limit through `\rho>0` are treated separately.

## Notation

Define

\[
 v:=D_t\theta=\dot\theta+A_0,
 \qquad I(\rho):=\mu\rho^2,
\]

and the resolved Lagrangian

\[
 L=\frac{\mu}{2}\dot\rho^2-U(\rho)
   +\frac{I(\rho)}{2}v^2. \tag{1}
\]

The canonical momenta are `p_\rho`, `J=p_\theta`, and, when `A_0` is varied,
`\pi_0=p_{A_0}`. `J` is also the moment map for compact shifts of `\theta`.
`\ell` denotes a fixed classical value of `J`.

For a constraint surface `\iota:\mathcal C\hookrightarrow\mathcal P`, write

\[
 \omega_{\mathcal C}:=\iota^*\Omega,
 \qquad \mathcal K:=\ker\omega_{\mathcal C},
 \qquad \mathcal P_{\rm red}:=\mathcal C/\mathcal K,
\]

when the kernel is an integrable gauge distribution and the quotient is
well-defined.

## Derivation

### 1. Exact variation and Legendre map

Varying (1), integrating only the `\delta\dot\rho` and
`\delta\dot\theta` terms by parts, gives the exact identity

\[
 \begin{split}
 \delta S={}&\left[p_\rho\,\delta\rho+J\,\delta\theta\right]_{t_i}^{t_f}\\
 &+\int dt\left[
 \left(-\dot p_\rho-U'(\rho)+\frac{I'(\rho)}2v^2\right)\delta\rho
 -\dot J\,\delta\theta+J\,\delta A_0\right], \tag{2}
 \end{split}
\]

where the exact Legendre relations are

\[
 p_\rho=\mu\dot\rho,
 \qquad J=I(\rho)v=\mu\rho^2(\dot\theta+A_0),
 \qquad \pi_0=0. \tag{3}
\]

Thus the Euler--Lagrange equations are

\[
 \dot p_\rho=-U'(\rho)+\mu\rho v^2,
 \qquad \dot J=0, \tag{4}
\]

and, only in theory G where `A_0` is varied,

\[
 J=0. \tag{5}
\]

The velocity Hessian in coordinates `(\rho,\theta,A_0)` is

\[
 W=\operatorname{diag}(\mu,\mu\rho^2,0). \tag{6}
\]

Consequently,

\[
 \operatorname{rank}W=
 \begin{cases}
 2,&\rho>0,\\
 1,&\rho=0,
 \end{cases} \tag{7}
\]

or, for the fiber variable alone, the rank is `1` for `\rho>0` and `0` at
`\rho=0`. This rank change is present whether or not the eventual reduced
system is harmless.

More strongly, the exact image of the Legendre map is stratified:

\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \rho>0:& J\text{ is arbitrary and }\pi_0=0,\\
 \rho=0:& J=0\text{ and }\pi_0=0
 \end{array} \tag{8}
\]

for finite velocities. It is not a constant-rank primary constraint
submanifold near `\rho=0`. Allowing `v\sim\rho^{-2}` can keep `J` nonzero in a
sequence approaching zero, but then the velocity and energy diverge; it does
not alter the exact fiber image at `\rho=0`.

The canonical boundary one-form and symplectic form are, exactly,

\[
 \Theta=p_\rho\,d\rho+J\,d\theta+\pi_0\,dA_0,
 \qquad
 \Omega=d\rho\wedge dp_\rho+d\theta\wedge dJ
       +dA_0\wedge d\pi_0. \tag{9}
\]

### 2. Branch P: a physical rotor and a singular disappearance

Set `A_0=0` and do not vary it. For `\rho>0`, (3) is invertible and gives

\[
 \dot\rho=\frac{p_\rho}{\mu},
 \qquad \dot\theta=\frac{J}{\mu\rho^2}. \tag{10}
\]

There is no constraint. The exact Hamiltonian is

\[
 H_P=\frac{p_\rho^2}{2\mu}
     +\frac{J^2}{2\mu\rho^2}+U(\rho). \tag{11}
\]

The shift `\theta\mapsto\theta+\beta`, with constant `\beta`, is a global
symmetry, and (4) shows that its charge `J` is conserved. It is not an
off-shell local gauge symmetry for `\rho>0`.

At exact `\rho=0`, the fiber Lagrangian is identically zero and (3) imposes
the new primary constraint `J\approx0`. If `\rho=0` is frozen as a stratum,
the pullback of the angular symplectic form is

\[
 \left.(d\theta\wedge dJ)\right|_{J=0}=0, \tag{12}
\]

so `\partial_\theta` becomes a null direction. The action there admits the
accidental local shift `\delta\theta=\varepsilon(t)`. This gauge/null
direction exists only at collapse; it was a physical canonical direction at
every `\rho>0`. Therefore calling (12) “gauge removal” does not make the
reduction regular across `\rho=0`.

For the angular sector, the exact rank table is

| theory P fiber sector | constraint surface | presymplectic rank | kernel |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `\rho>0`, all `J` | `T^*S^1` | 2 | 0 |
| `\rho>0`, fixed `J=\ell` | `S^1` | 0 | `\operatorname{span}(\partial_\theta)` |
| exact `\rho=0` Legendre image | `J=0` | 0 | `\operatorname{span}(\partial_\theta)` |

The middle row is a deliberate fixed-charge symmetry reduction; the last row
is forced by a singular Legendre map. They have the same pulled-back form but
different physical origins.

#### Fixed-momentum sectors

On the level set `J=\ell`, the pullback of (9) is

\[
 \omega_\ell=d\rho\wedge dp_\rho, \tag{13}
\]

with `\partial_\theta` in its kernel. Quotienting the compact shift removes
`\theta`, but `\ell` survives as the selected moment-map value and hence as a
sector label. The reduced Hamiltonian is

\[
 H_\ell=\frac{p_\rho^2}{2\mu}
       +\frac{\ell^2}{2\mu\rho^2}+U(\rho). \tag{14}
\]

If the conserved energy is finite and `U` is finite near zero, then for every
`\ell\neq0`

\[
 \frac{\ell^2}{2\mu\rho^2}\longrightarrow+\infty
 \quad(\rho\to0), \tag{15}
\]

so that sector cannot reach collapse. Only `\ell=0` can do so at finite
energy. Compactness gives `\ell=n\hbar`, `n\in\mathbb Z`, in canonical quantum
mechanics, but no quantum conclusion is needed here.

Equation (15) supplies a sharp branch test:

- retaining `\ell\neq0` leaves a physical compact-fiber charge and obstructs
  exact collapse in this model;
- deleting `\ell\neq0` at collapse violates conservation unless the action
  supplies a charge sink or an interface carrier;
- selecting `\ell=0` avoids the centrifugal divergence, but it does not by
  itself convert the physical angle at `\rho>0` into gauge.

#### Open-set `\rho=0` and information transfer

Suppose an interval `[t_1,t_2]` has `\rho=0`. Its fiber action is zero. If
`\theta` is retained as a resolution variable, every interpolation
`\theta(t)` across that interval has the same action and satisfies the fiber
equation. If `\theta` is omitted in the collapsed quotient, no equation relates
the angle on a later reopened interval to the angle on the earlier resolved
interval. Thus:

- if `\theta` was physical on the resolved intervals, collapse followed by
  reopening is a many-valued evolution relation (or erases the incoming
  angle);
- if the same compact shift is already gauge on both resolved intervals, the
  missing angle carries no reduced information.

The second branch is harmless only because of a gauge reduction defined
before collapse, not because the kinetic coefficient happened to vanish.

### 3. Exactly soluble polar-resolution check

For `A_0=0`, define Cartesian variables

\[
 x=\rho\cos\theta,\qquad z=\rho\sin\theta. \tag{16}
\]

For `\rho>0`, direct differentiation gives the exact identity

\[
 \dot x^2+\dot z^2=\dot\rho^2+\rho^2\dot\theta^2. \tag{17}
\]

Thus (1) with a radial potential is an ordinary planar particle in polar
coordinates. In terms of Cartesian momenta,

\[
 p_\rho=p_x\cos\theta+p_z\sin\theta,
 \qquad
 J=\rho(-p_x\sin\theta+p_z\cos\theta). \tag{18}
\]

At the Cartesian origin, `J=0` for every finite `(p_x,p_z)`, but `p_\rho`
without `\theta` does not determine the direction of the Cartesian momentum.
A naive quotient that retains `p_\rho` while discarding `\theta` therefore
loses canonical data. Cartesian coordinates make the dynamics regular, but
they also show why this example is **not** exact dimension reduction: the
origin still has a two-dimensional tangent space and a full Cartesian
momentum pair.

This soluble case establishes two distinct lessons. A collapsing angular
coordinate can be only a coordinate singularity, and a rank loss in bad
coordinates need not be fatal. Conversely, quotienting the angle together
with its hidden tangent data is not justified merely by `\rho=0`.

### 4. Branch G: Gauss reduction before collapse

Now vary `A_0`. The action is invariant off shell under

\[
 \delta_\alpha\theta=-\alpha(t),\qquad
 \delta_\alpha A_0=\dot\alpha(t),\qquad
 \delta_\alpha\rho=0, \tag{19}
\]

because `\delta_\alpha v=0` exactly.

For `\rho>0`, the canonical Hamiltonian and total Hamiltonian are

\[
 H_C=\frac{p_\rho^2}{2\mu}+\frac{J^2}{2I(\rho)}+U(\rho)-A_0J,
 \qquad H_T=H_C+u\pi_0. \tag{20}
\]

The Dirac algorithm gives

\[
 \pi_0\approx0 \quad\hbox{(primary)}, \tag{21}
\]

\[
 \dot\pi_0=\{\pi_0,H_T\}=J\approx0
 \quad\hbox{(secondary)}, \tag{22}
\]

and `\dot J=0`, with no tertiary constraint. Since

\[
 \{\pi_0,J\}=0, \tag{23}
\]

both constraints are first class. A generator with the sign convention of
(19) is

\[
 G[\alpha]=-\alpha J+\dot\alpha\,\pi_0, \tag{24}
\]

which gives `\delta\theta=\{\theta,G\}=-\alpha` and
`\delta A_0=\{A_0,G\}=\dot\alpha`.

On the stabilized constraint surface

\[
 \mathcal C_G=\{J=0,\ \pi_0=0\}, \tag{25}
\]

the exact pulled-back form is

\[
 \omega_G=d\rho\wedge dp_\rho, \qquad
 \ker\omega_G=\operatorname{span}(\partial_\theta,\partial_{A_0}). \tag{26}
\]

The reduced variables are only `(\rho,p_\rho)`, and

\[
 H_{G,{\rm red}}=\frac{p_\rho^2}{2\mu}+U(\rho). \tag{27}
\]

There is no nonzero fixed-`J` sector in the source-free theory G. Introducing
a nonzero Gauss level would retain that value as charge data and reproduce the
divergence (15), unless additional charged/interface degrees of freedom alter
the constraint and energy.

#### The exact zero-radius branch is still Legendre-singular

At `\rho=0`, both `J=0` and `\pi_0=0` are primary Legendre constraints. The
fiber action is independent of both `\theta` and `A_0`, so it has the enlarged
accidental symmetry

\[
 \delta\theta=\varepsilon(t),\qquad
 \delta A_0=\eta(t) \tag{28}
\]

with two independent functions. At `\rho>0`, by contrast, only the linked
transformation (19) is a symmetry and `v=0` follows from (3) and (5).

The final reduced form (26) is the same on the two branches, but the Legendre
rank, primary/secondary split, and number of independent Lagrangian gauge
functions change. In particular, if one retains only the original linked
gauge group (19), then `v` is gauge invariant and is unconstrained on an open
`\rho=0` interval. It is an arbitrary zero-action function. It is removed only
if the enlarged shifts (28) are explicitly included in the collapsed
resolution equivalence, or if a matching condition such as `v=0` is imposed
from the `\rho>0` limit.

Therefore this decoupled toy has two sub-branches:

1. **Reduced-benign but singular:** declare (28) gauge on the collapsed
   branch. Then the reduced radial phase space and Hamiltonian agree with the
   `\rho\to0` limit, even though the unreduced Legendre map changes rank.
2. **Predictively incomplete:** retain only (19). Then the exact open
   `\rho=0` equations admit arbitrary gauge-invariant `v`, so the limiting
   equations and exact collapsed equations do not agree without extra data.

The first sub-branch proves that a Hessian rank change alone is not a no-go.
It does not make the rank-changing Legendre map regular, and it does not show
that an interacting field theory has a consistent enhanced gauge algebra.

### 5. A genuinely regular reference reduction

For comparison, consider the first-order action

\[
 S_{\rm reg}=\int dt\left[
 p_\rho\dot\rho+J(\dot\theta+A_0)
 -\frac{p_\rho^2}{2\mu}-U(\rho)\right]. \tag{29}
\]

Variation of `A_0` gives `J=0`, variation of `J` gives
`\dot\theta+A_0=0`, and (19) is a gauge symmetry. The constraint `J=0`, its
gauge orbit, and the reduced form `d\rho\wedge dp_\rho` are independent of
`\rho`. This is a regular removal of a pure-gauge canonical pair.

However, (29) is not equivalent to the physical rotor for `\rho>0`: it has no
nonzero-`J` sectors and no angular inertia. Trying to recover (1) by adding

\[
 -\frac{J^2}{2I(\rho)} \tag{30}
\]

and eliminating `J` reproduces `I v^2/2` for `\rho>0`, but (30) is undefined
off shell at `\rho=0`. Its vanishing on the Gauss surface does not supply a
differentiable first-order action there. Thus a multiplier can make a
constant-rank gauge theory, but only by being part of a different off-shell
theory; it cannot retroactively turn the singular physical rotor into a
regular reduction.

### 6. Collapse multiplier and free-boundary conditions

#### Holonomic collapse multiplier

Add `\lambda(t)\rho` to theory P. Variation of `\lambda` enforces
`\rho=0`. On an open interval this also gives `\dot\rho=\ddot\rho=0`, and the
radial equation from (2) becomes

\[
 0=-U'(0)+\lambda,\qquad\hbox{so}\qquad \lambda=U'(0). \tag{31}
\]

The multiplier supplies a radial reaction force when necessary. It does not
restore the fiber Legendre rank: `J=0` is still forced at `\rho=0`, and
`\theta(t)` remains arbitrary unless it is independently gauged. In the
Hamiltonian description the holonomic chain includes `\rho=0` and
`p_\rho=0`; the term `J^2/(2\mu\rho^2)` also forces selection of the finite
`J=0` branch. Hence this multiplier constrains the radius but neither carries
the missing compact charge nor determines a reopening angle.

#### Free transition time or free fiber endpoint

Let a resolved interval meet a collapsed interval at `t=\tau`, with no
interface action. The exact fiber boundary term from (2) is

\[
 (J_+-J_0)\,\delta\theta(\tau), \tag{32}
\]

when a common resolution angle is varied. Since `J_0=0` on the collapsed
side, a free interface angle gives

\[
 J_+=0. \tag{33}
\]

If the collapsed theory has no angle at all, the resolved endpoint variation
again gives (33). A movable `\tau` additionally gives the usual energy
transversality condition (equality of Hamiltonians in the absence of an
interface action), but it gives no equation relating an incoming angle to a
later outgoing angle.

Thus a free boundary naturally selects the zero-charge sector; it does not
turn a global shift into gauge and does not repair information transfer. An
interface action could store charge or relate endpoint data, but then its
variables, symplectic term, and variation must be included in the canonical
count rather than treated as harmless removal.

### 7. Cheapest exact diagnostic

The rotor calculation yields the following diagnostic for any candidate
collapse action. On one canonical slice, compute

\[
 \boxed{
 \left(
 \operatorname{rank}D\mathbb F L,\quad
 \mathcal C_\rho,\quad
 \ker\iota_\rho^*\Omega,\quad
 J_\rho,\quad
 (\mathcal P_{{\rm red},\rho},\omega_{{\rm red},\rho},H_{{\rm red},\rho})
 \right)
 }
 \tag{34}
\]

for `\rho=\epsilon>0`, the exact open `\rho=0` branch, and any transition
variables. Then compare “reduce first, then take `\epsilon\to0`” with “set
`\rho=0`, complete the constraints, then reduce.”

A **regular gauge removal** in this diagnostic requires all of the following:

1. the disappearing fiber directions lie in a smooth gauge kernel generated
   by regular first-class constraints already on a punctured neighborhood,
   rather than entering the kernel only when a kinetic coefficient vanishes;
2. the relevant moment map is constrained to zero, or every nonzero charge is
   explicitly carried by retained bulk/interface data;
3. gauge generators and their boundary charges have a finite limit and their
   algebra does not change rank;
4. the reduced symplectic form and Hamiltonian have a finite, constant-rank
   limit; and
5. evolution through the collapsed region is a single-valued canonical map
   on the complete reduced data (including transition data).

Failure of item 1 identifies a **singular rank-changing Legendre map**, not by
itself a fatal theory. It becomes fatal for deterministic canonical evolution
if items 2--5 cannot be repaired: a physical charge is deleted, a formerly
physical angle is identified without a gauge generator, arbitrary
gauge-invariant zero modes appear, or reopening requires data absent from the
reduced state.

### 8. Justified local coframe analogy, and no more

Let `y\sim y+2\pi` be a dimensionless compact fiber angle and define only the
kinematic coframe ansatz

\[
 E^4=\rho\,\eta,\qquad \eta:=dy+A. \tag{35}
\]

Under

\[
 y\mapsto y-\alpha(x),\qquad A\mapsto A+d\alpha, \tag{36}
\]

`\eta` and `E^4` are invariant. Pulling (35) back along a time curve gives
`E^4(\partial_t)=\rho(\dot y+A_0)`, exactly the combination in (1); a
quadratic kinetic norm therefore has `I\propto\rho^2`. This is sufficient to
justify the analogy, but not a gravitational dynamics.

The exact differential identity

\[
 dE^4=d\rho\wedge(dy+A)+\rho\,dA \tag{37}
\]

shows why the algebraic statement `E^4=0` is insufficient. On an open
`\rho=0` set, `d\rho=0` and both `E^4` and `dE^4` vanish. At a transition where
`d\rho` need not vanish, `dE^4=d\rho\wedge(dy+A)` can retain fiber/resolution
data. Moreover, in a first-order theory

\[
 T^4=dE^4+\omega^4{}_a\wedge E^a \tag{38}
\]

can retain independent connection components even when `E^4=0`. Neither
`A`, a fiber holonomy, nor `\omega^{4a}` is proved gauge-null by the coframe
rank alone. Their fate depends on the full action, constraints, boundary
terms, and presymplectic current.

Equations (35)--(38) are the only field/coframe extrapolation made here. No
Einstein--Cartan, Lovelock, interface, or other gravitational action is
postulated.

### 9. Minimal checklist for a candidate first-order action

A candidate action should not be called a harmless collapse until it passes
this minimal canonical checklist on a planar or regular-polar background:

1. **Legendre table:** give the full kinetic/Legendre rank for
   `\rho=\epsilon>0`, exact open `\rho=0`, and the transition, including
   coframe, connection, multipliers, and interface variables.
2. **Complete Dirac system:** list all primary, secondary, and interface
   constraints; their Poisson-bracket rank; regular first-class generators;
   and the physical degree count on every branch.
3. **Presymplectic kernel and charges:** derive the bulk-plus-boundary
   symplectic potential from the action, identify its kernel, and calculate
   fiber moment maps, holonomies, surface charges, and symplectic flux. A
   declared gauge direction must have zero charge under the admitted boundary
   conditions.
4. **Fixed-charge sectors:** show that nonzero fiber charge is excluded by a
   regular Gauss constraint, remains finite and stored, or cannot reach the
   transition. It may not simply disappear at `\rho=0`.
5. **Reduction/limit commutation:** compare reduction before the limit with
   exact collapsed-branch reduction, and exhibit a deterministic canonical
   transfer map including all transition data. Check explicitly for accidental
   open-set zero modes.
6. **Complete variation:** vary collapse multipliers, interface displacement,
   free boundaries, corners, and large gauge data; do not impose matching
   conditions after the fact.
7. **Only then, hyperbolicity and sign:** on the resulting reduced system,
   test the gauge-fixed principal symbol and quadratic kinetic eigenvalues.
   A zero eigenvalue is harmless only if steps 2--5 establish that it is gauge,
   not merely because it is nonnegative.

## Approximations

No approximation is used in the rotor calculation. Equations (1)--(38) are
exact for `I(\rho)=\mu\rho^2`. There is no perturbative order and no discarded
term. The coframe discussion is an exact kinematic analogy, not an
approximation to a specified gravitational action.

## Validity regime

- The derivation applies to one compact rotor mode with a positive quadratic
  inertia and a regular radial potential near `\rho=0`.
- The finite-energy obstruction applies to fixed `J\neq0`, `I\to0^+`, and a
  potential finite near collapse.
- The regular gauge conclusion applies only when the Gauss constraint and
  admitted gauge group are part of the off-shell variational principle and
  their boundary charges vanish.
- The open-set information-loss statement concerns collapse followed by
  possible reopening. Permanent removal can avoid a reopening ambiguity but
  still is not invertible canonical evolution unless missing data are gauge or
  retained elsewhere.
- The model does not test a spatial PDE, strong hyperbolicity, Lorentz or
  diffeomorphism covariance, local gravitational degrees of freedom,
  distributional curvature, or resolution independence.

## Dimensional check

With `[t]=[\rho]=L` and action dimensionless,

\[
 [\mu]=L^{-1},\quad [U]=L^{-1},\quad [\theta]=1,
 \quad[A_0]=L^{-1},\quad[I]=L. \tag{39}
\]

Hence `[v]=L^{-1}` and both `\mu\dot\rho^2/2` and `Iv^2/2` have dimension
`L^{-1}`, as a Lagrangian should. Also

\[
 [p_\rho]=L^{-1},\qquad [J]=1,
 \qquad\left[\frac{J^2}{I}\right]=L^{-1}. \tag{40}
\]

Because `[A_0]=L^{-1}`, its canonical momentum has `[\pi_0]=L`, making
`dA_0\wedge d\pi_0` dimensionless like the other canonical two-forms. For
`L+\lambda\rho`, `[\lambda]=L^{-2}`. In (35), `y` and `A=A_\mu dx^\mu` are
dimensionless, `[\rho]=L`, and `[E^4]=L`, matching the repository coframe
convention. The use of a dimensionless compact angle `y` is an explicit
coordinate-convention exception.

## Limiting-case checks

1. **Fixed nonzero radius:** for `\rho=R>0`, theory P is the standard positive
   rotor with `H=J^2/(2\mu R^2)`; theory G reduces exactly to `J=0`.
2. **Zero charge:** setting `J=0` before `\rho\to0` removes the centrifugal
   term and gives the smooth radial Hamiltonian (27).
3. **Nonzero charge:** (15) has the positive sign expected for a spacelike
   compact mode and blocks finite-energy collapse. A negative sign would be a
   ghost rather than a cure.
4. **Exact zero radius:** the angular kinetic eigenvalue vanishes and the
   Legendre rank drops. It does not become negative.
5. **Cartesian resolution:** (17)--(18) reproduce a regular planar particle
   and expose hidden momentum-direction data at the polar origin.
6. **Conservation:** `\dot J=0` follows from the compact shift; a free boundary
   gives `J=0` rather than violating this conservation law.
7. **Gauge normalization:** the `2\pi` period makes the finite shift group
   `U(1)` and gives quantum levels `J=n\hbar` without an extra factor of
   `2\pi`.

action has been specified.

## Unresolved concerns

- On a time circle or with nontrivial spatial topology, the standard linked
  gauge group can leave a Wilson line/holonomy. The interval assumption removes
  that global issue from the toy model; a field theory must calculate it.
- The accidental gauge enhancement (28) is benign only in this decoupled
  model. Couplings can turn its would-be null variables into constraints,
  strong-coupling modes, surface charges, or nonclosing generators.
- A singular but well-defined stratified symplectic reduction can exist; the
  constant-rank criterion identifies the cheap regular branch, not a theorem
  excluding every singular branch.
- Treating `\rho(t)` as a temporal order parameter does not reproduce a
  timelike gravitational interface, where rank changes primarily in a spatial
  normal direction and symplectic flux through the interface matters.
- The model contains one compact mode, whereas five-dimensional gravity has
  coframe, connection, lapse/shift, diffeomorphism, Lorentz, and possible
  boundary modes. Constraint closure and degree counts can differ radically.
- No strong-hyperbolicity test is possible in `0+1` dimensions. Positive
  rotor energy and a consistent toy quotient cannot establish the no-ghost or
  predictivity parts of C003.

## Conclusion

**Derived result (exact toy model; diagnostic/necessary-condition category):**
the cheapest canonical discriminator is the joint Legendre--presymplectic
rank and moment-map test (34), performed on `\rho>0`, exact `\rho=0`, and
transition branches, followed by the reduction/limit commutation test.

The branch structure is exact:

- A physical rotor has a nondegenerate `(\theta,J)` pair for `\rho>0`.
  Nonzero `J` survives symmetry quotienting as a charge label and cannot reach
  `\rho=0` at finite energy; at `J=0`, quotienting a previously physical angle
  can erase reopening data.
- A Gauss constraint imposed before collapse removes `J` and places the fiber
  directions in the presymplectic kernel, yielding a smooth reduced radial
  Hamiltonian. Nevertheless, the second-order rotor Legendre map still changes
  rank at exact collapse and its gauge symmetry enlarges there.
- A genuinely constant-rank reference gauge reduction can be written, but it
  is a different off-shell theory with no physical rotor sector. Radius
  multipliers and free-boundary conditions select `J=0` or supply radial
  reaction forces; they do not by themselves turn physical information into
  gauge or determine reopening data.

The toy model therefore neither establishes a gravitational no-go nor supplies
a viable gravitational action. It only identifies what the first candidate
action must calculate: whether every disappearing fiber/coframe/connection
direction is generated by a regular zero-charge first-class constraint and
whether the complete reduced symplectic evolution remains deterministic after
interface data are included. The evidence category returned to the director is
**derived toy-model diagnostic**, not support, contradiction, reproduction, or
independent verification of C003.
