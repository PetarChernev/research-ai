Map baseline theories for dynamical dimensionality: can a non-prescribed extra-spatial-direction region have autonomous dynamics and a calculable energy or probe response? Compare literal, emergent, and operational meanings without assuming the prior Palatini circle-collapse formulation.

## Distinction from sibling branches

The fixed circle below never changes circumference or disappears. No transverse
potential selects bound states. Instead a scalar changes the coefficient of a
spatial derivative, including its exact zero. This is neither radion collapse,
trapping, nor changing adjacency. Its cost is permanent exterior field sheets.

## Target claim

**Derived result sought:** an autonomous positive-energy classical model can
change a probe's spatial principal rank from three to four without degenerating
its time kinetic term. Calculate its dispersion and test what remains at rank
zero in the extra direction. This is a propagation-dimension toy, not intrinsic
total spacetime dimension change or a universal matter/gravity construction.

## Assumptions

* Fixed ontology: Minkowski base with coordinates `(t,x)` and three spatial
  coordinates `x`, plus physical labels `y` on a circle of fixed length `L`.
  Intrinsic total dimension is always `d=5`; base signature is `(-,+,+,+)`.
* A real selector `q(t,x)` is shared by all circle labels. A real probe
  `phi(t,x,y)` has independent initial data at every label. Sharing the selector
  is an assumption: the theory is local on the base but has a global internal
  average in its selector equation, not unrestricted local five-dimensional
  matter dynamics.
* Natural units, positive constants `f,L`, no gauge equivalence, no gravity.
  Fields are periodic in `y`; initial fields are smooth, with sufficiently
  rapid base-space decay for finite total energy. Compactly supported selector
  data are allowed. Variations vanish at temporal endpoints and spatial
  infinity. Homogeneous diagnostic backgrounds instead use energy density.

## Derivation

### 1. Autonomous action and complete equations

**Assumption (model):**

\[
 S=\int dt\,d^3x\left\{
 \tfrac12\dot q^2-\tfrac12|\nabla q|^2
 +\left\langle\tfrac12\dot\phi^2-\tfrac12|\nabla\phi|^2
 -\tfrac{q^2}{2f^2}(\partial_y\phi)^2\right\rangle\right\}.
\]

The normalized circle measure is fixed, not a dynamical volume. Integrating
the variations by parts gives the **exact equations**

\[
 Dq+\frac q{f^2}\langle(\partial_y\phi)^2\rangle=0,
 \qquad D\phi-\frac{q^2}{f^2}\partial_y^2\phi=0. \tag{1}
\]

There is no prescribed profile, interface action, or independent boundary
condition at `q=0`: it is an ordinary level set of a varied field. Momenta,
defined using the normalized measure, are `πq=qdot`, `πphi=phidot`.
Their velocity Hessian is the identity even at `q=0`; there are no primary
constraints eliminating sheet data.

**Exact energy check.** The Hamiltonian is

\[
 E=\int d^3x\,\left[\tfrac12\dot q^2+\tfrac12|\nabla q|^2+
 \left\langle\tfrac12\dot\phi^2+\tfrac12|\nabla\phi|^2+
 \tfrac{q^2}{2f^2}\phi_y^2\right\rangle\right]\geq0. \tag{2}
\]

Differentiation and integration by parts give
`dEq/dt=−∫d³x (q qdot/f²)⟨phi_y²⟩` and the opposite expression
for the probe energy, hence `dE/dt=0`. Positivity is non-strict in transverse
gradients at `q=0`, not a negative-energy instability. Base Poincaré symmetry,
circle translations, and `q→−q` are preserved; five-dimensional Lorentz symmetry
is not imposed.

### 2. One load-bearing witness: probe characteristics and rank crossing

Take the **exact background** `phi=0,q=q0`, with constant real `q0`.
At quadratic order selector and probe decouple. The probe principal symbol is

\[
 P(\omega,k,p)=-\omega^2+|k|^2+a_0^2p^2,
 \qquad \boxed{\omega_n^2=|k|^2+a_0^2(2\pi n/L)^2}. \tag{3}
\]

This follows by substituting `exp(−iωt+ik·x+ip_n y)` into (1).
The local spatial tensor is `diag(1,1,1,a0²)`: rank four for `q0≠0`,
three for `q0=0`. The full principal tensor has rank five or four respectively,
with precisely one negative time eigenvalue. Locally transverse signal speed
is `|a0|`; on the circle the momentum spectrum is discrete. For varying `q`
the same tensor is the frozen principal tensor, but (3) is then only a local
short-wavelength dispersion, not a global eigenfrequency.

At `q0=0` every `n` remains a massless base-space field, with its own
positive time kinetic energy. Thus an infinite degeneracy becomes inexpensive;
it does **not** become one field. Equivalently there is one uncoupled wave
equation on every exterior sheet `y`. Quotienting these data to a single
field would change the model and generally discard conserved canonical data.

For a time-dependent diagnostic use `q=vt,phi=0`, an exact homogeneous
solution, where constant `v` is initial velocity. Each linear probe mode obeys

\[
 \ddot u_n+\left[|k|^2+(vp_n/f)^2t^2\right]u_n=0. \tag{4}
\]

Its coefficients are smooth at zero: initial `u_n,udot_n` determine a unique
mode continuation. For example `uddot_n(0)=−|k|²u_n(0)`. A turning-off
spatial coefficient alone therefore does not force modewise indeterminism.
This is the same characteristic witness tested dynamically, not a proof of
uniform continuum evolution for arbitrary transverse data.

### 3. What is dynamically selected?

In the exact invariant sector `phi=0`, (1) is the ordinary base wave equation
`Dq=0`. Smooth compactly supported initial `q,qdot` remain supported inside
their base light-cone enlargement. Consequently an open exterior with `q=0`
and an interior nonzero set arise from initial data, not an inserted coupling.
Both the support and its zeros evolve autonomously. This establishes motion
of prepared regions, not spontaneous nucleation: exact vacuum initial data
remain vacuum. Finite-energy selector packets disperse; no static particle,
stable bubble, or timelike material wall is established. Probe backreaction
is explicitly included in (1), but is not solved here.

### 4. Singular normalization and strong coupling

There is no singular **temporal** canonical normalization in this model.
Nevertheless the apparently innocuous rescaling `Y=y/|a0|` at nonzero constant
`a0` makes the transverse derivative coefficient unity while sending the
period to `L/|a0|`. It is singular at zero and describes decompactification of
the *propagation* scale, not removal of sheets. In normalized circle measure
`dy/L=dY/(L/|a0|)`; dropping this factor would produce spurious normalization
claims.

Expansion about `q0` gives the exact interaction terms

\[
 -\frac{q_0\delta q}{f^2}\phi_y^2
 -\frac{(\delta q)^2}{2f^2}\phi_y^2. \tag{5}
\]

At a fixed transverse cutoff `|p|≤Λy`, vertices are polynomial, bounded in
momentum, and not singular in `q0`. But the fractional change of transverse
stiffness is `2δq/q0+(δq/q0)²`; expansion around a nonzero stiffness is
nonuniform near zero. At fixed base energy `ε`, modes as large as
`|p|∼ε/|a0|` become accessible. Their quartic coefficient scales like
`p²/f²`, so small `ε/f` alone does not ensure weak interactions as `a0→0`.
This identifies a continuum strong-coupling/species risk, not a computed
quantum cutoff or scattering theorem.

Replacing the time term by `Z(q)phidot²/2` would be a different model.
If `Z→0`, then `πphi=Z phidot` and Hamiltonian contribution
`πphi²/(2Z)` diverges for generic fixed momentum. Canonical rescaling by
`√Z` is singular and generically enhances interactions. Whether the zero
produces constraints, nonunique evolution, or a benign gauge reduction requires
that new action's constraint analysis; spatial rank loss here implies none of
these automatically.

## Approximations

Equations (1)–(2) and the background solutions are exact classical statements.
Equations (3)–(4) are linear probe dynamics, order `δphi`, with energy retained
through quadratic order. Backreaction on `q` first enters at order `δphi²`.
No adiabatic approximation is used in (4). A frozen-symbol interpretation on
general profiles requires wavelength short relative to variation scales away
from zeros and is not uniform at the interface. Equation (5) is an exact
polynomial expansion, not a controlled weak-coupling conclusion.

## Validity regime

Classical smooth fields on fixed base and circle; all rank statements refer
to the specified probe, in regular coordinate charts. Rank is invariant under
nonsingular coordinate changes. Finite Fourier truncation supplies a regular
semilinear base wave system and ordinary local smooth-data evolution, not a
continuum ultraviolet completion. Full nonlinear well-posedness in a specified
transverse Sobolev space has not been established: (2) loses control of
`phi_y` where `q=0`.

## Dimensional check

With mass dimension notation, `[x]=[t]=[y]=[L]=−1`, `[q]=[phi]=[f]=1`,
`[a]=0`, `[v]=2`, and `[ω]=[k]=[p]=1`. The normalized average is
dimensionless; each action density has dimension four. Both terms in each
field equation have dimension three; `E` has dimension one. Every term in
(3) and the bracket in (4) has dimension two. Time kinetic signs are positive;
the transverse gradient enters the action negatively and energy positively.

## Limiting-case checks

The `n=0` probe always obeys the base massless wave equation. At fixed `n`,
`q0→0` is smooth and retains two real initial-data functions per real mode.
At fixed energy the number of accessible modes diverges in that limit, so
interchanging zero stiffness with summing the tower is unjustified. Negative
`q0` gives identical propagation, consistent with the selector reflection
symmetry. The finite-energy compact packet and homogeneous crossing diagnostic
are different boundary sectors and must not be conflated.
