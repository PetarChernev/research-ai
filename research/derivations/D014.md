# D014: Adversarial rank and charge no-go test

## Question addressed

Within the fixed-interface planar basic persistent SO(1,3) sector of D005/D006, what is the minimum scientifically complete bulk-plus-interface Lorentz generator and boundary polarization needed to decide whether the C016 Stueckelberg rank-ten block is first-class gauge, physical edge structure, or a second-class remnant?

## Branch charter

Attempt the strongest bounded impossibility argument: under the frozen D005/D006 assumptions and any local differentiable Lorentz Gauss completion consistent with the displayed shell, try to prove that the rank-ten Stueckelberg block or its corner charge cannot be wholly gauge-null. Attack the argument with explicit counter-completions and moment-map cancellations. Return either a conditional no-go with exact hypotheses or a demonstrated reason no such no-go follows.

## Distinction from sibling branches

This is the adversarial branch of `W001-total-gauss`. It does not attempt to
construct the actual Palatini Gauss generator. Instead it asks whether the
interface rank and moment map alone imply a theorem against *every* local,
differentiable completion, and then tries to defeat that theorem with explicit
formal completions. This is materially different from a constructive canonical
split: the burden here is a universal no-go, and one admissible
counter-completion is enough to defeat it. No sibling derivation `D007`--`D013`
was inspected during this first pass. Distinct GPT sessions are not independent
evidence.

## Target claim

### Candidate no-go, with quantified assumptions

Let `E` denote the ten-dimensional symplectic quotient of the shell
Stueckelberg block at one planar spatial point, and let `p=Ad_(g^-1)Q=P^-`.
The strongest candidate statement is:

> **N (universal candidate).** For every local differentiable persistent
> `SO(1,3)` Lorentz Gauss completion whose Euler shell contains
> `Delta e=Delta omega=0`, `lambda=-Q`, `mu=0`, `Q=g.P^-`, and `H(K)=0`, the
> pullback of the total cut presymplectic form to `E` has nonzero reduced rank,
> or a Lorentz transformation nonzero at the temporal corner has a nonzero
> total charge. Hence the entire rank-ten block cannot be gauge-null.

The target is to prove N or exhibit why N does not follow. “Gauge-null” means
null after imposing the *total* moment-map constraints and quotienting by the
declared proper Lorentz gauge group; it does not mean merely that an interface
moment map is nonzero before the bulk contribution is added.

**Outcome preview.** N is **refuted as a universal statement** by a local
sign-opposite bulk boundary polarization compatible with the displayed shell.
An exact conditional no-go survives only after adding hypotheses that forbid
that cancellation or leave fewer than five effective first-class Lorentz
generators. Those hypotheses are not contained in D005/D006/C016. The actual
Palatini classification therefore remains **inconclusive**.

stabilizer generator does not count toward `r`.

## Derivation

### 1. Strongest rank argument

From (3)--(4), exact field-space differentiation gives

\[
 \Omega_E(\delta_1,\delta_2)=\int_C\left[
 \langle\delta_1p,\zeta_2\rangle-
 \langle\delta_2p,\zeta_1\rangle+
 \langle p,[\zeta_1,\zeta_2]\rangle\right]. \tag{6}
\]

On the planar rank-three branch, D006 proves that (6) has rank ten after its
four null directions are removed. Thus `E` is pointwise ten-dimensional and
symplectic.

A tempting no-go counts only six Lorentz parameters and concludes that they
cannot remove ten directions. That count is wrong. If `r` independent
first-class constraints act regularly on a `2n`-dimensional symplectic space,
then imposing their moment-map levels and quotienting their gauge orbits gives

\[
 \dim E_{\rm red}=2n-2r. \tag{7}
\]

Here `2n=10`. The planar `p` has a one-dimensional Lorentz stabilizer, so the
six-dimensional Lorentz algebra has exactly five effective directions on its
orbit. Therefore `r=5` is algebraically possible and (7) gives zero. The
rank-ten block has precisely the size that five regular Gauss generators can
remove. Rank alone supplies no no-go.

An exact **conditional rank no-go** does follow if all of the following are
added:

* the completion adds no pullback that cancels or mixes with (6);
* its constraint surface intersects `E` regularly;
* at most `r<=4` independent first-class generators act on `E`;
* no additional first-class constraints beyond the Lorentz Gauss system act on
  `E`.

Then (7) implies

\[
 \dim E_{\rm red}\ge 10-2(4)=2, \tag{8}
\]

so the block cannot be wholly gauge-null. None of the four added hypotheses is
fixed by C016.

### 2. Strongest moment-map argument

Equation (5) is nonzero because a rank-three triad has nonzero area momentum.
If transformations nonzero at `C` are declared improper, (5) is a genuine
interface charge witness. If they are proper, differentiability of the *total*
Gauss generator requires the total moment map

\[
 J_{\rm tot}=J_E+J_B \tag{9}
\]

rather than `J_E` alone. The shell relation `p=P^-` is exactly the relation
needed for a bulk term proportional to `P^-` to cancel (5).

Consequently, a conditional charge no-go is available only under the explicit
hypothesis

\[
 J_B\big|_{(1)}\ne-p
 \quad\hbox{for every admitted local differentiable bulk improvement}. \tag{10}
\]

For example, if `J_B|_(1)=c p` with fixed `c != -1`, then

\[
 J_{\rm tot}|_{(1)}=(1+c)p\ne0. \tag{11}
\]

Either the transformation is improper and charged, or the proposed zero-level
proper-Gauss constraint is incompatible with the rank-three shell. But (10) is
not a consequence of locality, differentiability, or Lorentz covariance.
Declaring every nonzero *partial* moment map physical would simply assume the
desired conclusion and is not used.

### 3. Explicit favorable cancellation completion F1

Consider the following local family of omitted bulk boundary polarizations:

\[
 \Theta_B(c)=c\int_C\langle P^-,\zeta\rangle . \tag{12}
\]

It is point-local on `C`, differentiable, and Lorentz covariant because it
pairs coadjoint momentum with the adjoint Maurer--Cartan form. It introduces no
inverse coframe. It can arise formally by dressing the exterior bulk Lorentz
frame with `u`; for this adversarial purpose no claim is made that the actual
Palatini split has already been shown to produce (12).

On tangent variations to (1), `P^-=p` and `delta P^-=delta p`. Hence, exactly,

\[
 \iota_{(1)}^*\Theta_{\rm tot}=(1+c)\Theta_E,
 \qquad
 \iota_{(1)}^*\Omega_{\rm tot}=(1+c)\Omega_E,
 \qquad
 J_{\rm tot}=(1+c)p . \tag{13}
\]

The orientation-compatible choice

\[
 \boxed{c=-1} \tag{14}
\]

cancels the edge potential, its rank-ten two-form, and its corner moment map on
the displayed shell. This completion is consistent with the shell rather than
forcing `Q=0`: cancellation uses the accepted matching equation `Q=g.P^-`.
The minus sign is not dimensionally or symmetrically forbidden; indeed D005's
exterior incident variation carries the opposite sign to the interface
momentum. Establishing that the actual canonical Palatini boundary term gives
exactly (12) with `c=-1` still requires the missing split.

This is already a formal counterexample to universal N under the deliberately
broad quantified class “any local differentiable completion.” It does not prove
that F1 is the physically selected completion.

### 4. Explicit favorable first-class completion F2

A less degenerate favorable possibility retains a nonzero total two-form off
the Gauss surface. Let the bulk provide five regular boundary Gauss constraints

\[
 \mathcal G_A=J_{E,A}+J_{B,A}\simeq0,
 \qquad A=1,\ldots,5, \tag{15}
\]

with one stabilizer/reducibility identity, and let their Hamiltonian vectors
span a Lagrangian five-plane in `E`. If their brackets close into
`mathcal G_A` and the allowed parameters are proper at `C`, then (7) gives a
zero-dimensional reduction of `E`. Local moment-map cancellation
`J_B=-p` makes (15) compatible with (1). This is the standard finite-dimensional
symplectic-reduction mechanism, stated here as a formal completion rather than
as a result about the omitted Palatini brackets.

F2 shows that exact cancellation of the two-form itself is not necessary: five
effective first-class constraints can gauge the rank-ten block. Its unresolved
requirements are regularity, closure, and the actual bulk Hamiltonian vector
fields.

### 5. Explicit obstructed completion O

For contrast, take a completion whose boundary polarization is independent of
`u` and has no mixed contraction with the edge block, so `c=0` in (12), and
declare Lorentz parameters nonzero at `C` to be improper. Then

\[
 \Omega_{\rm tot}|_E=\Omega_E,\qquad
 \mathcal Q[\alpha]=\int_C\langle p,\alpha\rangle . \tag{16}
\]

The planar witness with the component paired to nonzero `Q_03` makes (16)
nonzero. Since improper transformations are not quotiented, the block survives
as charged edge structure. Alternatively, even for proper parameters, a
completion with no cancellation and only `r<=4` effective generators is
obstructed by (8).

The assumptions distinguishing O from F1/F2 are therefore not locality or
Lorentz symmetry. They are: the coefficient and mixed structure of the bulk
boundary polarization; the number and closure of effective Gauss generators;
and whether corner-nonzero transformations are proper.

### 6. Three decisive falsification attacks on the no-go

1. **Generator-count attack — successful.** Six Lorentz components with one
   stabilizer give five effective generators, exactly enough to reduce a
   ten-dimensional symplectic block to zero. The naive `6<10` argument fails.
2. **Moment-map cancellation attack — successful formally.** Equations
   (12)--(14) give `J_B=-P^-=-p` on shell and cancel both charge and two-form.
   Locality, differentiability, dimensions, and Lorentz covariance do not
   forbid this sign.
3. **Corner-status attack — successful.** The same nonzero interface witness
   is physical when corner transformations are improper, but can be a summand
   in a proper total Gauss constraint when they are admitted. C016 intentionally
   does not choose between these boundary conditions.

After these attacks, no stronger no-go follows without computing or postulating
the omitted total polarization and generator.

### 7. Minimum discriminator

The minimum scientifically complete datum is not the full bulk Hamiltonian. It
is the following cut-local packet:

1. the pullback of both bulk Lorentz symplectic potentials to `C`, including
   every `delta u u^-1` dressing term and incident sign;
2. the differentiable improved core and exterior Lorentz Gauss generators,
   including their boundary moment maps;
3. the allowed seed-frame action and whether parameters nonzero at temporal or
   lateral corners are proper;
4. the Jacobian rank and bracket closure of the total Gauss constraints on
   (1).

These data distinguish `c=-1,r=5` from `c!= -1` or `r<=4`. Anything less can
reproduce both favorable and obstructed completions above.

## Dimensional check

With `[e]=L`, `[b]=L^-2`, and dimensionless connections and group fields,

\[
 [Q]=[P^-]=[p]=1,
 \qquad [\zeta]=1. \tag{17}
\]

Thus `Theta_E`, `Theta_B`, all moment maps integrated over `C`, and their sum
are dimensionless in the conventions of D005/D006. The coefficient `c` is
dimensionless. The cancellation `p-P^-=0` is dimensionally normalized only
because the frozen branch fixes `b=kappa_4^{-1}`. No hidden `2pi` remains: it
is already included in `b=2pi gamma`.

## Limiting-case checks

1. **Degenerate area:** `p=0` makes (4)--(6) and every charge vanish, but this is
   the excluded rank-at-most-one branch and cannot establish the target result.
2. **Exact cancellation:** `c=-1` gives zero in all three relations (13), while
   `c=0` recovers C016 exactly. This checks normalization and sign sensitivity.
3. **Orientation reversal:** reversing the cut or marked-circle orientation
   reverses both matched incident terms. The cancellation condition remains a
   relative minus sign and ranks are unchanged.
4. **Stabilizer:** the `T_12` stabilizer reduces six Lorentz components to five
   effective orbit directions, matching half the rank ten. At a background
   with a larger stabilizer, F2 can fail for lack of five generators; no generic
   extrapolation is made.
5. **`K=0` or nonzero `H(K)=0` kernel:** neither changes `p`, (6), or the
   cancellation. This confirms that the connection-difference sector is not
   silently used.
6. **Symmetry and conservation:** (12) pairs adjoint and coadjoint fields, so it
   is Lorentz scalar. On a proper-gauge F1/F2 completion the total moment map
   vanishes; on O, its nonzero value is conserved only subject to the omitted
   total evolution and is therefore not promoted to a global conservation
   theorem.

## Unresolved concerns

### Ranking of failure modes

1. **Highest: unknown incident bulk polarization sign and dressing.** The
   actual coefficient corresponding to `c` is conclusion-critical. F1 is a
   formal counter-completion, not its derivation.
2. **High: undeclared proper/improper corner group.** Identical local formulas
   describe either a charge or a summand in a constraint depending on this
   choice.
3. **High: unknown closure and regularity of five effective Gauss generators.**
   The dimension count shows possibility, not first-class closure.
4. **Medium: extra mixed bulk-edge terms.** They can alter rank without being
   representable by the one-parameter family (12); the minimum packet in
   section 7 must retain them.
5. **Lower within the frozen regime: special stabilizers.** They can lower the
   effective orbit rank, but the accepted planar witness already fixes the
   stated one-dimensional stabilizer.

The sign of the actual Palatini cut term has not been recomputed here. Nor has a
local spacetime action realizing F1 while reproducing every bulk equation been
constructed; only the cut completion required to falsify the overbroad
universal claim is exhibited. These limitations prevent promotion of F1 to the
classification of the physical theory.

## Conclusion

locality, differentiability, Lorentz covariance, the displayed shell, rank ten,
and a nonzero *interface* moment map do not prohibit the formal sign-opposite
completion (12)--(14), and five effective Lorentz generators are exactly enough
to reduce ten symplectic dimensions. The stronger claim that the *actual*
Palatini completion gauges the block is **inconclusive**.

The conditional no-go **survives** only if one separately establishes either
`J_B|_(1)!=-p` together with noncancelling total polarization, or at most four
regular effective first-class generators on `E`, or that corner-nonzero
transformations are improper. Those are distinguishing assumptions, not
consequences of C016.
