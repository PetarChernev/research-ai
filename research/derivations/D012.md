# D012: Boundary polarization and counterterm portfolio

## Question addressed

Within the fixed-interface planar basic persistent `SO(1,3)` sector of
`D005`/`D006`, what is the minimum total Lorentz generator and boundary
polarization needed to decide whether C016's Stueckelberg rank-ten block is
first-class gauge, physical edge structure, or a second-class remnant?

## Branch charter

Classify the smallest local Lorentz-covariant boundary/corner polarizations
compatible with the D005 variational equations: fixed connection/coframe,
momentum, mixed, edge-extended, and exact corner shifts. For each, determine
whether reference-changing transformations are admitted, Hamiltonian, and
zero-charge, and whether the rank-ten block is removed, relocated, or retained.
Choices that obtain the answer only by forbidding the disputed variations are
rejected as classifications.

## Distinction from sibling branches

This branch is a **polarization portfolio and differentiability test**. It does
not reconstruct a full bulk Dirac algebra or propose a new edge action. It
holds the D005 equations fixed as the baseline and asks which boundary choices
are genuine canonical re-expressions, which alter the variational equations,
and what minimal total generator would discriminate among them. No sibling
artifact was inspected before completing this first pass. Distinct GPT
sessions are not independent evidence.

## Target claim

The target is a necessary-and-sufficient **declaration package**, not a final
classification of C016:

> On the D005 shell, an exact field-space corner shift cannot change the
> rank-ten two-form. A fixed-coframe or fixed-momentum polarization reduces its
> variable-momentum rank ten to a rank-four Lorentz coadjoint-orbit block, but
> changes the admissible variations and generally the D005 boundary equations.
> Fixing the Stueckelberg frame removes the block only by excluding its
> variations. The smallest polarization that preserves both the free-coframe
> D005 equations and reference-changing transformations is therefore the
> existing edge-extended polarization, completed by the exterior bulk Lorentz
> flux and a declared corner gauge group. Its total exterior cut moment map is
> forced, with the D005/D006 signs, to contain
> `Ad_(g^-1)Q-P^-`. It vanishes on momentum matching, but only the pullback of
> the **total** symplectic form can decide whether the corresponding vectors are
> null and quotient out the rank-ten block.

All statements are exact in the frozen local sector. There is no perturbative
claim beyond restricting to small Lorentz transformations.

## Assumptions

### Physical regime and boundary conditions

1. The D005 action is unmodified. The interface is fixed and timelike,
   `Sigma=I_t x C`, with oriented spatial cut `C`; variations vanish on the
   initial and final cuts when deriving Euler equations. Spatially compact
   support and transformations nonzero at a cut are distinguished.
2. Only basic, `theta`-independent persistent `SO(1,3)` data are retained. The
   marked-circle period and orientation are fixed. Moving `Sigma`, full
   `SO(1,4)`, nonbasic modes, large gauge transformations, hyperbolicity, and
   Hopf/global claims are excluded.
3. The evaluated shell is exactly

   \[
   \Delta e=\Delta\omega=0,\qquad \lambda=-Q,\qquad \mu=0,
   \qquad Q=g\!\cdot P^-,\qquad H(K)=0,                 \tag{1}
   \]

   with `b=2 pi gamma=kappa_4^{-1}` and a planar rank-three coframe. No inverse
   coframe, torsion equation, or time gauge is used.
4. A **polarization change** means a change of boundary variational data or a
   canonical re-expression. It is not assumed to leave the Euler equations
   unchanged. Only field-space-exact endpoint shifts leave the symplectic
   two-form unchanged automatically.
5. “Reference-changing” means a small exterior Lorentz transformation whose
   action changes the local representative of the reference connection or its
   orbit frame. Such a transformation is tangent to a fixed-background phase
   space only if the reference is edge-extended or an equivalence of background
   problems is explicitly declared.

### Conventions

6. `epsilon_0123=+1`, internal signature is `(-+++)`, antisymmetric pairs use
   the ordered-pair convention of D005, and

   \[
   Q_{ab}={b\over4}\epsilon_{abcd}e_+^c\wedge e_+^d,
   \qquad P^-_{ab}={1\over4\kappa_4}\epsilon_{abcd}
                     e_-^c\wedge e_-^d.                \tag{2}
   \]

7. `delta` is the field-space exterior derivative and
   `Omega_C=delta Theta_C`. Reversing this convention reverses all displayed
   symplectic forms and Hamiltonians together but not ranks or null spaces.
8. The analysis is pointwise on `C` when ranks are quoted. Functional zero
   modes and global stabilizers are outside scope.

## Derivation

### 1. Admissible variation and polarization definitions

Let `F_C` be the cut field space before imposing (1). The following local
classes are distinct.

1. **Connection Dirichlet:** the pullback of a chosen connection representative
   is fixed, e.g. `delta a_C=0` or `delta omega_C=0`. Gauge parameters must
   preserve that datum (`D_a alpha=0`) unless a compensating edge variable is
   included. Fixing `omega_+` also deletes its boundary Euler equation; it is
   not the free-connection D005 problem.
2. **Coframe Dirichlet:** `delta e_C=0`, hence `delta Q_C=0`. This deletes the
   free-coframe boundary equation that produced `H(K)+mu=0`; imposing `H=0`
   afterward is an additional restriction, not a consequence of the changed
   variational principle.
3. **Momentum:** `delta Q_C=0`, or, for the matched exterior, `delta P^-_C=0`.
   Locally at rank three this fixes the area bivector but not every coframe
   component. It has the same residual orbit calculation as coframe Dirichlet
   but a larger null set.
4. **Mixed:** fix a Lorentz-covariant subset of `(e,Q)` and its complementary
   connection datum. This is admissible only after identifying a covariant
   subbundle (or a transforming normal/reference). A componentwise split such
   as “fix `Q_03`” is frame dependent and is not admitted as a covariant
   polarization.
5. **Edge-extended:** vary `u` on its gauge orbit and retain the D005 equations,
   with reference changes represented by `u -> h_-u`. This is the smallest
   listed class that keeps both free coframe variation and reference-changing
   transformations.
6. **Exact corner shift:** add an endpoint functional `F_C` so
   `Theta_C -> Theta_C+delta F_C`. It changes the representative potential,
   not `Omega_C`.

Endpoint fixing means tangent variations vanish only at `C_i` and `C_f`. It
does not eliminate an interior cut degeneracy or rank. Conversely, fixing `u`
on every cut is a phase-space restriction, not merely endpoint fixing.

### 2. Baseline cut potential

The exact interface cut potentials from D006 are

\[
 \Theta_C^A=\int_C\langle\Pi_C,\xi\rangle,              \tag{5}
\]

for a fixed transforming reference, and

\[
 \Theta_C^B=\int_C\{\langle\Pi_C,\xi\rangle
                     +\langle Q_C,Z\rangle\}.           \tag{6}
\]

Pullback to (1) gives

\[
 \iota^*\Theta_C^A=0,\qquad
 \boxed{\iota^*\Theta_C^B=\int_C\langle Q_C,Z\rangle}. \tag{7}
\]

Writing `v=gu`, the second term is the canonical group/momentum potential
`<Q,delta v v^-1>` after the cancelling `(g,lambda)` block is removed. Since
`rank(dQ_C)=5`, its exact two-form has rank ten on the planar free-coframe
shell. Neither `K` nor `H(K)=0` enters (7).

### 4. Field-space exact versus non-exact changes

For any local corner functional `F_C`, nilpotence gives the exact identity

\[
 \Omega'_C=\delta(\Theta_C+\delta F_C)
           =\Omega_C+\delta^2F_C=\Omega_C.              \tag{8}
\]

Therefore no exact corner shift changes rank, kernel, or the interior cut
two-form. It may change endpoint polarization language and shift a Hamiltonian
by a field-independent constant.

Could one choose `F_C` with `delta F_C=-<Q,Z>`? No on the free shell, because

\[
 \delta\!\int_C\langle Q,Z\rangle=\Omega_{(v,Q)},
 \qquad \operatorname{rank}\Omega_{(v,Q)}=10\ne0.       \tag{9}
\]

If such an `F_C` existed locally, applying `delta` would set the left side of
(9) to zero, a contradiction. A counterterm that cancels (7) must therefore be
field-space non-exact, restrict variations, or add fields/constraints; each
changes more than the potential representative.

### 5. Explicit nontrivial candidate: fixed momentum

Take the planar cut

\[
 e_C^1=dx^1,\qquad e_C^2=dx^2,
 \qquad Q_C=q\,T_{03}^*\,dx^1\wedge dx^2,
 \quad q={b\over2}\ne0,                                \tag{10}
\]

and impose `delta Q_C=0` while allowing `v` to vary. Equation (7) becomes a
coadjoint-orbit potential. At the identity, its two-form on algebra variations
`chi_i=delta_i v v^-1` is exactly

\[
 \Omega_Q(\chi_1,\chi_2)
   =\int_C\langle Q,[\chi_1,\chi_2]\rangle.             \tag{11}
\]

The centralizer of the simple bivector `T_03` in `so(1,3)` is

\[
 \mathfrak z(T_{03})=\operatorname{span}\{T_{03},T_{12}\}. \tag{12}
\]

Indeed, `T_03` commutes with itself and with the rotation in the orthogonal
`12` plane; each of `T_01,T_02,T_13,T_23` has a nonzero commutator pairing in
(11). Hence the kernel dimension is two in a six-dimensional orbit-variable
space and

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{rank}\Omega_Q=6-2=4.}             \tag{13}
\]

Thus fixed momentum does not make the edge frame symplectically null. It
removes the five variable-momentum canonical pairs and leaves the four-rank
Kirillov block (two pairs). The result is Lorentz covariant when stated as the
orbit of a simple nonzero bivector; naming `03` is only an adapted-frame
calculation. If instead “fixed” means that `Q` does not transform, the admitted
Lorentz group is reduced to the stabilizer (12), on which (11) vanishes because
the non-stabilizer transformations are no longer tangent. Those are different
boundary problems.

### 6. Adversarial boundary-condition example

Impose

\[
 u|_C=u_0,\qquad \delta u|_C=0,
 \qquad \alpha_-|_C=0.                                 \tag{14}
\]

Then `Z=0`, so (7), its charge, and its rank-ten block vanish on that cut. This
does **not** show that the block was gauge: (14) removes both the disputed
coordinate and the transformation that might have generated gauge. If imposed
only at `C_i,C_f`, it makes the action differentiable at endpoints but leaves
the rank-ten form on every interior cut. If imposed on all cuts, it selects a
fixed reference and returns to case A. Therefore (14) is rejected as an answer
to C016, although it is a legitimate separate boundary value problem.

### 7. Differentiability and total corner Hamiltonians

For B, D006 gives interface moment maps

\[
 J^I_+=\Pi_C,\qquad J^I_-=-\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\lambda_C,
 \qquad \iota^*J^I_-=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}Q_C.     \tag{15}
\]

The exterior Palatini variation has incident momentum `-P^-` in D005. Thus the
smallest total exterior corner coefficient compatible with differentiability
and matching is

\[
 \boxed{J_-^{\rm tot}=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}Q_C-P^-_C} \tag{16}
\]

up to one simultaneous overall sign set by the convention for `G`. Equation
(1) makes (16) zero. The corresponding minimum schematic generator is

\[
 \boxed{G_{\rm tot}[\alpha_+,\alpha_-]
 =G_5[\alpha_+]+G_4[\alpha_-]
 +\int_C\!\left(\langle\Pi_C,\alpha_+\rangle
 +\langle\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}Q_C-P^-_C,
          \alpha_-\rangle\right),}                     \tag{17}
\]

where `G_5,G_4` must be derived from the pulled-back bulk symplectic potentials,
not guessed from (17). In the D005 regular interface class the core incident
boundary potential is zero; `Pi=0` is accordingly the displayed core corner
coefficient. Formula (17) fixes the **minimum terms and relative sign** that a
complete calculation must reproduce; it is not a claim that the omitted bulk
generators already have a first-class algebra.

A reference-changing vector `X_alpha` is admitted and Hamiltonian precisely if

\[
 \iota_{X_\alpha}\Omega_{\rm tot}=\delta G_{\rm tot}[\alpha]
 \quad\text{with no uncancelled lateral or temporal-corner variation}. \tag{18}
\]

It is zero-charge on the constraint surface if (17) vanishes there, after any
allowed constant is fixed. Equations (16) and (1) establish the necessary
zero-moment-map cancellation. They do not establish the stronger condition

\[
 \iota^*\iota_{X_\alpha}\Omega_{\rm tot}=0,             \tag{19}
\]

the regularity/closure of all six Gauss directions, or their quotient. Those
checks require the bulk terms in (18). A vanishing charge alone does not change
the rank-ten interface form; it can remove it only through symplectic reduction
of the total constrained system.

For a fixed reference A, arbitrary reference changes fail before (18): they
are not tangent vectors to one phase space. Only the reference stabilizer is
admitted. Calling the transforming family “gauge” would require an explicit
equivalence relation or promotion to B.

### 8. Effect on shell rank/nulls and gauge group

The exact consequences are:

1. Free `e,u` with the D005 shell retains rank ten before total Gauss reduction.
2. Exact endpoint shifts retain the same rank and nulls.
3. Fixed nonzero simple `Q` leaves rank four under full orbit variation; if the
   gauge group is reduced to its two-dimensional stabilizer, the excluded four
   directions cannot be counted as null gauge directions.
4. Fixing `u` removes the block by reducing the tangent space.
5. Adding an independent compensator merely relocates symplectic rank unless a
   new constraint and quotient are supplied.
6. The existing exterior bulk flux is the unique already-required candidate
   for cancellation of (15): matching makes (16) vanish without imposing
   `Q=0`. Whether its bulk Gauss vector removes all five pairs is undecided.

No local six-dimensional Lorentz group can be declared to remove “ten rank
directions” by dimension counting alone: moment-map constraints, stabilizers,
and quotient directions must be counted together in the total constrained
space.

### 9. Rejection criteria

A proposed polarization is rejected as a classification of the unchanged D005
sector if any of the following occurs:

- it sets `Q=0` or lowers the coframe below rank three;
- it sets `delta u=0` or `alpha_-=0` and then calls the absent transformation
  gauge;
- it fixes `e` or `omega` but continues to quote boundary equations that their
  free variations originally derived;
- it claims an exact `delta F_C` changes `Omega_C` or its rank;
- it cancels (7) with a non-exact term while calling the dynamics unchanged;
- it declares a transforming fixed background a canonical gauge variable;
- it infers first-class status from covariance or a vanishing on-shell charge
  without checking (18)--(19) and closure;
- it uses a frame-component mixed condition without a transforming normal or
  covariant subbundle.

## Dimensional check

With `[e]=L`, `[b]=[kappa_4^{-1}]=L^{-2}`, and connections and group variables
dimensionless,

\[
 [Q]=[P^-]=[\lambda]=[\Pi]=1,
 \qquad [\mu]=L^{-1}.                                  \tag{20}
\]

Therefore (5)--(7), (11), and the corner terms in (15)--(17) are dimensionless
after integration on `C`, as are the action and Lorentz generators in natural
units. The marked-circle factor is already in `b=2 pi gamma`; none is added in
(16). Orientation reversal changes `Q`, `P^-`, `Theta`, and `G` consistently
and leaves ranks unchanged.

## Limiting-case checks

1. **Degenerate area:** `Q_C=0` makes both rank ten and rank four vanish, but is
   the excluded rank-at-most-one branch.
2. **Fixed reference:** deleting `u` gives (5), whose pullback is zero. This
   agrees with D006 but admits only the reference stabilizer in one phase space.
3. **Fixed momentum:** (13) is even and bounded by the four-dimensional
   coadjoint orbit of a nonzero simple Lorentz bivector; it cannot equal ten.
4. **Free momentum:** restoring the five-dimensional image of `dQ` adds five
   canonical pairs and recovers rank ten, as in D006.
5. **`K=0` or nonzero `H(K)=0` kernel:** neither changes (7), confirming that
   connection polarization and the rank-ten block are distinct.
6. **Endpoint fixing:** `delta u=0` at initial/final cuts kills endpoint
   variations only; the interior `Omega_C` remains (9).
7. **Exact shift:** (8) directly checks that a corner Legendre re-expression
   cannot change rank.
8. **Momentum matching:** `Q=g.P^-` gives (16)=0 with the D005 incident sign.
   The opposite relative sign would double rather than cancel the nonzero
   planar area flux.
9. **Lorentz covariance:** the adapted `T_03` result is invariant under
   conjugation because centralizer dimension and two-form rank are conjugacy
   invariants.

## Minimal declaration needed to proceed

The smallest scientifically complete next calculation must state and compute
all of the following, without a full bulk Hamiltonian analysis:

1. **Cut phase space:** whether `a` is fixed (A) or represented by `u` (B), and
   whether `e,Q,u,g` vary on an interior cut.
2. **Admitted gauge group:** the stabilizer for A, or the left exterior action
   and any seed-frame right action for B; parameters at temporal and lateral
   corners must be specified.
3. **Bulk pullbacks:** the core and exterior Lorentz parts of the bulk
   symplectic potential on a spatial slice, restricted to the interface `C`.
4. **Differentiable generator:** derive `G_5+G_4` and verify that its corner
   completion is (17), including orientation and sign.
5. **Reduction test:** impose all total Gauss moment maps, compute their rank
   and stabilizers, test (18), closure, and (19), then quotient. Only the
   resulting rank can classify C016.

For preserving the unmodified free-coframe D005 dynamics **and** testing
reference changes, the minimum polarization choice is B with the existing
edge field; fixed-`Q`, fixed-`e`, and fixed-reference choices answer different
questions. No new independent edge pair is minimally required before the bulk
flux already present in D005 is included.

## Bounded C016/H002/H003 implications

- **C016:** The rank-ten block is robust under exact corner shifts and under
  connection fixing with free `(e,u)`. It can be reduced to rank four by fixing
  nonzero simple momentum or removed by fixing `u`, but those alter the
  variational domain. Its status in the unchanged D005 sector remains pending
  the total reduction (17)--(19).
- **H002:** Momentum matching supplies the necessary zero total exterior moment
  map (16), so a regular zero-charge first-class completion remains possible.
  This branch does not establish nullness, closure, or regular quotient and
  therefore does not support H002 as a derived result.
- **H003:** The inability of exact shifts to erase the block and the residual
  rank-four fixed-momentum orbit are warnings that edge structure is not a
  potential convention. But bulk Gauss reduction may still remove it, so no
  unavoidable physical or second-class remnant is derived.

No implication is drawn for moving interfaces, full `SO(1,4)`, nonbasic modes,
hyperbolicity, Hopf structure, or large-gauge/global sectors.

## Unresolved concerns

  derived here; (17) states the minimum differentiable completion they must be
  tested against, not their full algebra.
- Special backgrounds can enlarge the stabilizer and lower (13); the stated

## Conclusion

**Exact restricted derived result.** In the frozen D005/D006 sector, the shell
edge potential is (7). Field-space-exact corner shifts cannot change its
rank-ten two-form. Fixing a nonzero simple momentum relocates it to a rank-four
coadjoint-orbit block; fixing the Stueckelberg frame removes it only by
forbidding the disputed variations. Fixed coframe, momentum, connection, and
reference polarizations must not be presented as unchanged D005 dynamics when
they delete equations or gauge directions.

The minimum faithful discriminator is the existing edge-extended polarization
with free coframe, together with the bulk Lorentz pullbacks, declared corner
gauge group, and differentiable total generator (17). Momentum matching forces
the exterior total corner moment map `Ad_(g^-1)Q-P^-` to vanish, but only a
calculation of the total constrained two-form can show whether the rank-ten
block is removed by a regular first-class quotient, retained as physical edge
structure, or leaves a second-class remnant.
