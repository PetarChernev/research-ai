# D026: Boundary-trace connection descent at the polar trace

## Question addressed

Can one replace D022's stipulated finite mixed trace class by one concrete,
lower-regularity but resolution-presentation-independent **connection descent
category**, and does that category admit the constant polar tangent

\[
 \delta\omega^{43}=\delta\chi^{43}\,d\theta?                 \tag{1}
\]

The answer is sharp for the frozen **tangential boundary trace**. A connection
trace on `Y=Sigma x S1` descends along `p:Y->Sigma` precisely when it carries
explicit kernel-pair descent isomorphisms. Locally, this is equivalent to
trivial fiber holonomy together with vanishing vertical curvature intrinsic to
`Y`. For
`a=cT_{43}dtheta`, the curvature condition is automatic and descent is
equivalent to

\[
 \exp(2\pi cT_{43})=1.                                        \tag{2}
\]

Thus the generic-`c` boundary trace is excluded. At a discrete value satisfying
(2), the boundary trace is admitted, but the constant variation (1) changes holonomy at
first order and is excluded from the tangent to the descended sector. It is
neither a physical tangent nor an infinitesimal periodic gauge/presentation
tangent. The discrete trace itself can be a nontrivial *upstairs
presentation* of the trivial connection on `Sigma`. The finite map is an
isomorphism of boundary descent presentations, while only maps satisfying the
fixed-cocycle condition are automorphisms of that fixed presentation. An
adapted `SO(1,3)_V` transformation cannot perform it.

All claims below are exact except where explicitly labelled **linearized**.
The linearization keeps first order in a formal parameter `varepsilon` and
discards `O(varepsilon^2)`. No action, equation of motion, canonical claim, or
PDE statement is added.

## Distinction from sibling branches

There is no sibling branch in this bounded cycle. Unlike D022, this derivation
does not declare every finite one-sided connection trace admissible. Unlike
D024--D025, it does not repair or classify actions. Its materially different
route is categorical: it treats a connection by affine principal-bundle
descent along the ordinary projection `p:Y->Sigma`, rather than applying
D002's tensorial-form equalizer to a local connection potential. The decisive
restriction comes from boundary fiber holonomy and kernel-pair coherence, not
from an Euler equation. It makes no claim that arbitrary collar-normal data
define a connection on the singular quotient collar.

## Target claim

Construct a local category `TrConn_lr(p)` such that:

1. its objects are finite tangential traces `a=i_Y^*A` with connection descent
   data along `p:Y=Sigma x S1->Sigma`;
2. its morphisms identify changes of boundary resolution presentation without
   identifying arbitrary different descended `Sigma` connections;
3. its object and tangent criteria are necessary and sufficient for descent of
   `a` to a connection on `Sigma`, under the stated local triviality and
   regularity assumptions;
4. it decides separately generic `c`, discrete/trivial-holonomy `c`, and the
   standard `c=1` polar frame; and
5. it states exactly how the decision changes the scope, but not the truth, of
   C025/C027/C028.

The target concerns the **tangential core boundary trace only**. It does not
assert that a full `SO(1,4)` bundle, reduction field, or arbitrary one-sided
collar connection descends to the singular quotient `X_+` or onto the open
four-dimensional exterior.

## Assumptions

1. **Frozen local geometry.** As in D022,
   \[
   M_+=U\times[0,\epsilon)\times S^1,
   \quad Y=\partial M_+=\Sigma\times S^1,
   \quad \rho=c s\ (s>0),\quad \rho=0\ (s\leq0),              \tag{3}
   \]
   with `c>0`, `theta~theta+2pi`, flat punctured core and exterior, fixed
   `Sigma`, and unchanged persistent D005 data.
2. **Geometric context and theorem map.** The core-closure quotient context is
   \[
   q_+:M_+\longrightarrow X_+:=M_+/\!\sim,
   \tag{4}
   \]
   where `q_+` is one-to-one for `s>0` and collapses each boundary circle
   `(u,0,S1)` to `u in Sigma`. This is only geometric context. The map used in
   the theorem is the ordinary smooth projection
   \[
   p:Y=\Sigma\times S^1\longrightarrow\Sigma.                 \tag{4a}
   \]
   No connection sheaf or effectivity theorem on `X_+` is assumed.
3. **Group convention.** `G=SO_0(1,4)` unless a different global form is
   explicitly stated, with `eta=diag(-,+,+,+,+)`. The local generator
   `T_{43}` is a compact rotation in the spacelike `3-4` plane. The standard
   vector normalization is
   \[
   \exp(2\pi T_{43})=1.                                       \tag{5}
   \]
   A changed generator or a spin lift changes the lattice described below.
4. **Connection convention.** In a local trivialization a connection is a
   Lie-algebra-valued one-form `A`. Under a frame/presentation change
   `h:M_+->G`,
   \[
   A^h=hAh^{-1}-dh\,h^{-1},\qquad
   F^h=hFh^{-1}.                                               \tag{6}
   \]
   This affine law, not tensorial equalizer descent, is used throughout.
5. **Lower regularity and trace boundary.** A representative core connection
   `A` may be smooth for `s>0` and need only possess a finite one-sided
   tangential trace `a:=i_Y^*A`, with `a in C^1(Y)`. Tangents have the same
   trace regularity. `TrConn_lr(p)` contains `a`, not the unrestricted normal
   coefficients of `A`. In particular, `A_s(u,theta,0)ds` and collar curvature
   with a normal leg are not constrained by the theorem. Presentation changes
   on `Y` are `C^2`; they may be traces of collar frame changes, but no collar
   extension theorem is needed. This includes `a=cT_{43}dtheta` and is weaker
   than smooth extension through D002's open collapsed side.
6. **Local triviality.** `U` and the working patch in `Sigma` are contractible;
   the bundles on `Y` and `Sigma` are locally trivial. Global bundle sectors
   over `Sigma` are not classified. Ordinary principal-connection descent
   along the smooth surjective submersion `p` is the only descent statement.
7. **No reduction-field descent premise.** The category below descends only the
   tangential core connection trace. A fixed adapted normal/reduction field
   `V=e_4` is used only to distinguish the subgroup `SO(1,3)_V`. Requiring the
   boundary pair `(a,V|_Y)` to descend is a stronger category and is treated as
   a limitation, not silently imposed.
8. **Boundary support.** Variations are compactly supported in `U`. No outer
   face, corner, or displacement variation is introduced.

## Notation

### 1. Kernel pair

Let the boundary kernel pair be

\[
 R_p:=Y\times_\Sigma Y,
 \qquad p_1,p_2:R_p\rightrightarrows Y.                       \tag{7}
\]

\[
 R_p=\{(u,\theta_1;u,\theta_2)\}\cong\Sigma\times S^1\times S^1.
                                                                     \tag{8}
\]

On the triple fiber product write `gamma_ij` for pullback along the indicated
pair.

### 2. Objects and descent data

An object of `TrConn_lr(p)` in the chosen local trivialization is a pair
`(a,gamma)`, where `a\in\Omega^1_{C^1}(Y,\mathfrak g)` and
`gamma:R_p->G` is a `C^1` descent isomorphism satisfying

\[
 \gamma_{11}=1,
 \qquad \gamma_{12}\gamma_{23}=\gamma_{13},                  \tag{9}
\]

and the **affine connection descent equation**

\[
 \boxed{
 p_1^*a=\gamma\,p_2^*a\,\gamma^{-1}-d\gamma\,\gamma^{-1}.}
                                                                    \tag{10}
\]

Equation (10) is an equality on `R_p`. In bundle language,
`gamma:p_2^*P_Y->p_1^*P_Y` is a connection-preserving isomorphism. Under the
stated local assumptions, such data are equivalent to a bundle with connection
on `Sigma`. This sentence concerns `p`, not the rank-jumping map `q_+`.

This is not D002's form equalizer `p_1^*A=p_2^*A`: a local potential is affine.
Curvature is tensorial and obeys the consequence

\[
 p_1^*F_a=\gamma\,p_2^*F_a\,\gamma^{-1}.                      \tag{11}
\]

### 3. Presentation morphisms and fixed-cocycle automorphisms

A full boundary `G` **presentation change** is any admitted `h:Y->G`, acting by

\[
 (a,\gamma)\longmapsto
 (a^h,\gamma^h),\qquad
 \gamma^h=(p_1^*h)\gamma(p_2^*h)^{-1}.                        \tag{12}
\]

Equations (9)--(10) are invariant under (12). Objects related by (12) are
isomorphic boundary descent presentations. Under ordinary descent along `p`,
they correspond to isomorphic bundle-with-connection objects on `Sigma`.

A **gauge automorphism preserving the fixed descent presentation** is more
restrictive. Its representative `k:Y->G` must preserve `gamma`,

\[
 (p_1^*k)\gamma=\gamma(p_2^*k),                               \tag{13}
\]

so it is the pullback of a gauge transformation of the corresponding fixed
`Sigma` object. A fiber-dependent `h` violating (13) can still be a legitimate
isomorphism of boundary descent presentations through (12), but it is not an
automorphism preserving the original cocycle. This does not intrinsically deny
all gauge-isomorphism language for the corresponding `Sigma` objects.

Finally, after choosing `V=e_4`, adapted transformations satisfy `hV=V` and
form `SO(1,3)_V`. Since `T_{43}` does not stabilize `e_4`, the winding
transformations used below are full `SO(1,4)` presentation changes, not adapted
`SO(1,3)_V` gauge transformations.

### 4. Tangents

A tangent to an object is `(delta a,xi)`, where
`xi:=delta gamma gamma^{-1}` obeys the linearized cocycle

\[
 \xi_{13}=\xi_{12}+\operatorname{Ad}_{\gamma_{12}}\xi_{23},   \tag{14}
\]

and the linearization of (10),

\[
 \boxed{p_1^*\delta a
 =\operatorname{Ad}_{\gamma}(p_2^*\delta a)-D_{p_1^*a}\xi.}  \tag{15}
\]

An infinitesimal presentation change with parameter `epsilon` has

\[
 \delta_\epsilon a=-D_a\epsilon,
 \qquad
 \xi_\epsilon=p_1^*\epsilon-
       \operatorname{Ad}_{\gamma}(p_2^*\epsilon).             \tag{16}
\]

It is an automorphism tangent preserving the fixed cocycle precisely when
`xi_epsilon=0`, the linearization of (13). A tangent not of (16) can change the
descended `Sigma` connection and is merely an **admitted trace-connection
tangent**; this derivation does not call it physically propagating without a
presymplectic analysis.

## Lemmas

### Lemma 1: necessary-and-sufficient boundary-trace criterion

Let `v=partial_theta` on `Y=Sigma x S1`. Under Assumptions 5--6, a boundary
connection trace `a` is presentation-equivalent to the pullback of a local
connection on `Sigma` if and only if

\[
 \boxed{H_u[a]=1\quad\text{and}\quad \iota_vF_a=0
 \quad\text{for every }u\in\Sigma,}                           \tag{17}
\]

where

\[
 H_u[a]={\cal P}\exp\oint_{\{u\}\times S^1}a                 \tag{18}
\]

uses the fixed positive circle orientation.

**Status of criterion.** Equation (17) is both necessary and sufficient for
descent of the tangential boundary trace along `p:Y->Sigma` in
`TrConn_lr(p)`. It is not sufficient for an arbitrary collar connection to
define a connection on `X_+`, and it is not claimed for a singular trace or a
category that retains isotropy on the collapsed fiber.

**Proof — necessity (exact).** If `(a,gamma)` descends along `p`, choose any
local section of `p`. Equation (10) identifies the boundary restriction with
the pullback of one `Sigma` connection under connection-preserving fiber
isomorphisms. Parallel transport around a loop entirely inside one `p` fiber
must therefore be the identity, giving
`H_u=1`. Equation (11) says curvature is pulled back from `Sigma`; contraction
with the vertical vector vanishes, giving `i_vF=0`.

**Proof — sufficiency (exact).** Fix a base angle `theta_0`. Trivial holonomy
makes parallel transport `g(u,theta)` from `theta_0` to `theta` single-valued
and periodic. Apply the corresponding presentation change to set
`a_theta=0`. In that presentation,

\[
 (\iota_vF)_\mu=F_{\theta\mu}=\partial_\theta a_\mu.          \tag{19}
\]

The second condition in (17) therefore makes every horizontal coefficient
theta-independent. Hence `a=pi_Y^*\bar a` for a unique local connection
`bar a` on `Sigma`. Taking `gamma=1` gives (9)--(10); undoing the presentation
change gives coherent `gamma` by (12). This proves sufficiency. The proof uses
only circle ODE uniqueness and the declared `C1` trace regularity.

### Lemma 2: linearized criterion

For an admitted trace satisfying (17), a tangent `delta a` remains in
the same boundary-trace descent category if and only if

\[
 \boxed{\delta H_u[a]=0,
 \qquad \iota_vD_a\delta a=0.}                                \tag{20}
\]

The first relation is the linearization of trivial holonomy; the second uses
`delta F=D_a delta a`. If `U(theta)` denotes trace-background parallel transport,
then, up to the harmless left/right convention fixed by (18),

\[
 H^{-1}\delta H
 =\int_0^{2\pi}\operatorname{Ad}_{U(\theta)^{-1}}
       \delta a_\theta(\theta)\,d\theta.                     \tag{21}
\]

Equation (21), not an unweighted component average, is the non-Abelian
condition. Tangents satisfying (20) split locally into an infinitesimal
presentation part plus the pullback of a horizontal `Sigma`-connection tangent.
This follows by linearizing the gauge used in Lemma 1.

## Derivation

### 1. Constant polar trace and explicit descent data

Freeze the D022 trace

\[
 a=cT_{43}\,d\theta,\qquad F_a=0.                             \tag{22}
\]

Its fiber holonomy is exactly

\[
 H[a]=\exp(2\pi cT_{43}).                                     \tag{23}
\]

Therefore Lemma 1 gives the exact object criterion (2). When it holds, an
explicit kernel-pair cocycle on (8) is

\[
 \boxed{\gamma_c(\theta_1,\theta_2)
 =\exp[cT_{43}(\theta_2-\theta_1)].}                           \tag{24}
\]

It is single-valued precisely when (2) holds. Directly,

\[
 \gamma_{12}\gamma_{23}=\gamma_{13},                         \tag{25}
\]

and, since the subgroup is Abelian,

\[
 \gamma_c(cT_{43}d\theta_2)\gamma_c^{-1}
 -d\gamma_c\gamma_c^{-1}=cT_{43}d\theta_1,                  \tag{26}
\]

which verifies the affine descent equation rather than stipulating it.

For the standard normalization (5), (2) means

\[
 c\in\mathbb Z.                                               \tag{27}
\]

Because the frozen branch has `c>0`, its first allowed value is `c=1`.
More invariantly, the allowed set is the cocharacter lattice along the chosen
compact one-parameter subgroup:

\[
 cT_{43}\in\ker\!\left(\exp(2\pi\,\cdot):\mathfrak t\to G\right).
                                                                    \tag{28}
\]

Rescaling `T_{43}` inversely rescales the numerical lattice. For the canonical
lift `\widetilde T_{43}` of the vector-normalized planar generator, the
convention-exact relation is `exp(2pi \widetilde T_{43})=-1`; identity holonomy
in `Spin(1,4)` therefore requires the doubled/even lattice. Quotient groups,
nontrivial bundle lifts, or allowing a central isotropy arrow can alter this
statement. Those are different global categories; (28) is the
normalization-independent content here.

### 2. Generic `c`

If `exp(2pi cT43) != 1`, no single-valued `gamma` satisfying (9)--(10) exists.
The failure already occurs on one boundary `p` fiber and is invariant under all
presentation changes because holonomy transforms by conjugation.

Hence, in `TrConn_lr(p)`, the generic polar trace is **not an object**. The
variation (1) is consequently not an admitted physical or gauge tangent at
that trace; it is excluded before linearized dynamics:

\[
 \boxed{\text{generic }c:\quad a=cT_{43}d\theta\text{ fails descent, and
 (1) is category-excluded.}}                                  \tag{29}
\]

This is stronger than imposing `delta chi43=0` at an otherwise admitted
generic trace: the trace itself is absent from `TrConn_lr(p)`. No conclusion
about a different full-collar category follows.

### 3. Trivial-holonomy/discrete `c`

Let `c=c_*` satisfy (2), and consider the constant tangent

\[
 \delta a=\delta\chi\,T_{43}d\theta.                          \tag{30}
\]

The linearized curvature condition in (20) is satisfied, because
`D_a delta a=0`. The holonomy condition is not:

\[
 H^{-1}\delta H=2\pi\delta\chi\,T_{43}.                      \tag{31}
\]

Since a nonzero Lie-algebra element cannot be an infinitesimal lattice jump,
`delta H=0` requires

\[
 \boxed{\delta\chi=0.}                                       \tag{32}
\]

Thus a constant change of `c` is not tangent to the discrete descent locus.
Allowing `delta gamma` does not evade (31): changing coherent boundary descent
data changes the presentation, but every trace pulled back from a connection
on `Sigma` still has identity holonomy around a `p` fiber.

It is also not a periodic infinitesimal presentation transformation. In the
commuting `T43` direction, (16) would require

\[
 -\partial_\theta\epsilon^{43}=\delta\chi.                    \tag{33}
\]

Integrating around the circle gives `0=2pi delta chi`; no periodic `epsilon`
exists when `delta chi != 0`. A fortiori it is not an automorphism tangent
preserving the fixed cocycle. Therefore

\[
 \boxed{\text{discrete }c_*:\quad
 \delta\chi\,T_{43}d\theta\ (\delta\chi\ne0)
 \text{ is excluded by linearized descent, not gauge and not physical.}} 
                                                                    \tag{34}
\]

Here “not physical” means “not an admitted field-space tangent in this
category,” not “proved presymplectically null.”

### 4. The discrete trace is a presentation, not a continuous gauge mode

For `c_*` satisfying (2), the finite map

\[
 h_{c_*}(\theta)=\exp(c_*\theta T_{43})                        \tag{35}
\]

is single-valued and gives

\[
 a^{h_{c_*}}=0,
 \qquad \gamma_{c_*}^{h_{c_*}}=1.                            \tag{36}
\]

Thus every allowed constant polar trace is isomorphic, in the boundary descent
presentation category, to the zero boundary connection. This is a **finite
large presentation change**. It need not be connected to the identity; for
`SO_0(1,4)` its winding can detect the `Z2` fundamental-group class. The
category (12) admits all such smooth presentation changes, so this topology
does not create a continuous tangent between lattice points.

The same `h_c` fails condition (13) in the original nontrivial presentation:
it varies along a `p` fiber. It therefore is not an automorphism preserving the
original cocycle. In the trivial presentation (36), automorphisms preserving
that cocycle are fiber-constant and represent gauge transformations of the
fixed `Sigma` object. Calling (35) simply “gauge,” without distinguishing an
isomorphism of boundary descent presentations from a fixed-cocycle
automorphism, would be ambiguous; no broader intrinsic denial of
gauge-isomorphism language is intended.

Moreover, `h_c` is not in `SO(1,3)_V` for `V=e4`. If a reduction field is
transformed with the full frame, `V^h=h_cV` becomes theta-dependent. If instead
the adapted `V=e4` presentation is frozen absolutely, (35) is forbidden and
the discrete connection cannot be removed within the adapted subgroup. If the
pair `(A,V)` is required to descend, one must also impose covariant constancy
along the collapsed fiber; for the frozen adapted pair,

\[
 D_\theta V=cT_{43}V\ne0\quad(c\ne0),                         \tag{37}
\]

so that stronger pair-descent category excludes every positive `c`, including
`c=1`. Equation (37) explains why boundary connection-trace descent alone must
not be promoted silently to a D024 reduction-field result.

### 5. `c=1` polar frame

With standard vector normalization, `c=1` satisfies (2). The core half-cap
alone can be written in smooth Cartesian frames, and (35) is the familiar
one-turn polar-frame presentation change. In the present connection-only
category:

- the boundary connection is admitted and presentation-equivalent to zero;
- the nonzero constant tangent `delta c` is excluded by (31)--(32);
- the finite polar-frame change is full `SO(1,4)`, not adapted
  `SO(1,3)_V`, and is not an automorphism preserving the original cocycle; and
- none of this identifies the D002 quotient with the smooth half-plane cap,
  because the D002 object retains the separate open collapsed ray.

Thus the soluble `c=1` geometry checks the discrete conclusion but does not
erase the category distinction.

### 6. Nonconstant vertical tangents

The exclusion is specific to the constant holonomy-changing mode, not to every
vertical-looking variation. In the Abelian `T43` direction let

\[
 \delta a=f(u,\theta)T_{43}d\theta.                            \tag{38}
\]

Ignoring horizontal dependence momentarily, (21) requires

\[
 \int_0^{2\pi}f(u,\theta)d\theta=0.                           \tag{39}
\]

Then a periodic `epsilon` with `partial_theta epsilon=-fT43` exists, so this
vertical tangent is an infinitesimal **presentation** tangent. It is a
fixed-cocycle automorphism tangent only if its parameter also obeys (13); a
nonconstant fiber parameter does not. If `f` depends on `u`, the second equation in (20)
requires the companion horizontal components generated by the same
`-D_a epsilon`; retaining only (38) generally fails vertical-curvature descent.

This adversarial example shows that “contains `dtheta`” is not itself the
criterion. Holonomy, mixed curvature, and the affine companion terms decide.

### 7. Collar-normal counterexample and exact scope boundary

Let a one-sided collar representative be

\[
 A=f(\theta)\,ds,                                               \tag{39a}
\]

with smooth nonconstant periodic `f`. Its tangential pullback is exactly
`a=i_Y^*A=0`; hence `H[a]=1`, `F_a=0`, and `(a,gamma=1)` is an object of
`TrConn_lr(p)`. Nevertheless the collar curvature is

\[
 F_A=f'(\theta)d\theta\wedge ds,                               \tag{39b}
\]

and the angle-dependent normal coefficient need not extend as an ordinary
continuous connection through the smooth `c=1` Cartesian cap. Equations
(9)--(20) do not see `A_s ds`, `F_{s\theta}`, or any other curvature component
with a collar-normal leg.

This is not a counterexample to Lemma 1: the lemma classifies only the boundary
trace `a` along `p`. It is a counterexample to the stale stronger claim that
the same boundary equations suffice for arbitrary full-collar connection
descent on `X_+`. No such stronger claim is retained. Since the polar
classification uses only `a=cT_{43}dtheta` and its tangential variation, the
counterexample does not alter equations (23)--(34).

## Consequence for the D022 variational obstruction

D022's exact broader-class result is

\[
 p_!\Theta_{5,\chi^{43}}
 ={2\pi\over\kappa_5}\delta\chi^{43}o_\Sigma.                \tag{40}
\]

Nothing in the present derivation cancels (40), changes its sign, or removes it
from the variation on D022's field space. Instead:

1. for generic `c`, the candidate boundary trace is excluded by the object
   condition (2);
2. for discrete trivial-holonomy `c`, the constant variation multiplying (40)
   is absent because (32) fixes the tangent to the descended sector.

Therefore D022's obstruction is **merely avoided by a category/boundary-sector
restriction**. It is not dynamically removed, gauge-canceled, or shown to be a
null direction. This category behaves like a principled fixed-descent leaf,
with the added fact that only trivial-holonomy lattice presentations are
objects. It supplies no Euler equation selecting a lattice point.

## Approximations

Equations (3)--(13), (17)--(19), (22)--(29), (35)--(37), (39a)--(39b), and
(40) are exact under the stated category. Equations (14)--(16), (20)--(21),
(30)--(34), and the tangent tests (38)--(39) are first-order linearizations in
`varepsilon`; all `O(varepsilon^2)` terms are discarded.
Because the descent locus is discrete in the constant polar family, the
first-order conclusion `delta chi=0` is exact as a tangent-space statement and
has no small-`c` approximation. No semiclassical, weak-curvature, or numerical
expansion is used.

## Validity regime

The result is local to ordinary principal-connection descent along the smooth
projection `p:Y->Sigma`, `C1` tangential one-sided traces, fixed circle period
and orientation, the constant `T43` polar ansatz, fixed interface, flat
punctured bulks, and frozen persistent D005 sector. It is independent of the
numerical resolution profile except for retaining the frozen `rho=c s_+`; no
division by `rho` or embedding smooth structure occurs.

It does not control collar-normal coefficients, mixed normal-angular curvature,
or extension to a connection sheaf on `X_+`. It also does not cover singular or
distributional traces, quotient stacks retaining fiber isotropy, nontrivial
global bundles over `Sigma`, a mandatory descended reduction field, spin
structures beyond the stated canonical lift, altered global gauge groups,
