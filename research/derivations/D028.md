### Wall energy and autonomous initial contraction

For a planar static wall `φ=q(z)`, the exact equation is `q″=V′(q)`. Multiplying by `q′`, with `q′→0` and `V→0` at both ends, yields `q′²/2=V(q)`. The descending solution is

`q(z)=−v tanh(z/δ)`.

Consequently its **exact tension** is

`σ=∫dz[q′²/2+V(q)]=∫_{−v}^{v}dφ √(2V)`

` =√(λ/2)∫_{−v}^{v}(v²−φ²)dφ=2√(2λ)v³/3`.

The planar wall has infinite total energy in infinite three-space; tension alone is not a particle mass. Instead choose smooth spherical data at rest which equal `q(r−L)` near `r=L`, are regular and almost `+v` deep inside, and approach `−v` outside. Smoothly flattening the exponentially small kink tail near `r=0` ensures regularity. The resulting energy is finite since deviations and gradients decay exponentially. For `L≫δ`,

`Eφ(0)=4πσL²[1+O(δ²/L²)+exponentially small patch terms]`.

To see the power counting, put `z=r−L`: the wall integral contains `(L+z)²q′²`. Its odd term vanishes because `q′²` is even; the second moment is of order `σδ²`. There is no volume term because both vacuum energies are zero.

Let `ℓ(t)` be the nearby zero of the evolving field, with `ℓ(0)=L`. Differentiating `φ(t,ℓ(t))=0` twice, using initially zero velocity, gives `φ̈+φr ℓ̈=0` at the wall. The spherical field equation at the initial wall gives, **exactly for the locally kink-matched data**,

`φ̈=q″+2q′/L−V′(q)=2q′/L`.

Thus **`ℓ̈(0)=−2/L`**. This is local initial acceleration, not an all-time trajectory. It agrees with the leading thin-wall action `−4πσ∫dt ℓ²√(1−ℓ̇²)`, whose equation is `ℓ̈=−2(1−ℓ̇²)/ℓ`. That effective action requires a thin, slowly deformed wall and fails near collapse.

This establishes evolution of an initially supplied domain. Exact homogeneous vacuum data remain homogeneous by the autonomous equation: no spontaneous classical nucleation was shown. Moreover a nonconstant static finite-energy scalar bubble cannot be stabilized here. Under `φa(x)=φ(x/a)`, its gradient and potential energies scale as `aT+a³U`; stationarity would require `T+3U=0`. Since both are nonnegative, only vacuum survives this scaling test. Stable homogeneous phases therefore do not imply stable domains. Collapse can convert wall energy to scalar waves without violating total-energy conservation.
