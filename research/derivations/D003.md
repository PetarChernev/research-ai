# D003: Integration and Stokes obstruction on the circle-collapse quotient

## Target claim

This artifact asks whether the exact local quotient and quotient-diffeological
form complex of `D002` determine a resolution-independent integration and
Stokes calculus adequate to underlie an action. It analyzes three candidates
without assuming any of them:

1. **(A) resolved metric/coframe pushforward:** use the volume form of either
   flat-profile coframe and push its integration current to the common
   quotient;
2. **(B) normalized topological Haar pushforward:** integrate the opened
   `S^1` fiber with total Haar mass one, independently of its metric radius;
3. **(C) stratified incidence integration:** independently integrate top
   degrees on `X_5`, `X_4`, and `Sigma`, with an explicitly supplied incidence
   map between their boundary traces.

The exact restricted propositions proposed as the content of C010 and C011
are:

**Proposition I (equalizer/Stokes obstruction; proposed C010).** For every
smooth quotient-diffeological four-form in the exact `D002` equalizer, the
trace on the resolved boundary of the five-dimensional core vanishes. In
particular, its ordinary or normalized-Haar fiber pushforward to `Sigma`
vanishes. With the orientation conventions below, a general *resolution*
four-form would contribute with the opposite incidence sign to the exterior
four-dimensional boundary trace, but regular descended forms supply no
nonzero five-dimensional term to match. Thus the bare equalizer has no
nontrivial core-to-exterior Stokes flux.

**Proposition II (classification of the three candidates; proposed C011).**
The two coframe volumes push forward to densities proportional to `rho_1` and
`rho_2` and therefore differ exactly on `X_5`; this is legitimate dependence
on physical core geometry if the coframe is retained, but forbidden
resolution dependence if the two coframes are declared representations of one
bare quotient state. Normalized Haar integration removes the metric
circumference from the *fiber-integration operation* but neither supplies a
base/interface density nor evades Proposition I. A stratified incidence
functional can be defined after adding orientations, relative weights,
fiber/incidence data, and suitable interface or generalized trace data, but
none of those choices follows canonically from the final diffeology alone.

These are exact local propositions for the stated smooth equalizer, profiles,
orientations, and compact-support conditions. They are an obstruction to a
**canonical nontrivially coupled** calculus derived from the bare final
diffeology. They are not a no-go theorem for enriched stratified actions and
do not propose gravitational dynamics.

## Assumptions

   \[

   \qquad \theta\sim\theta+2\pi,
   \qquad X=M/\!\sim_q, \tag{1}
   \]

   where every circle is retained for `s>0` and each complete circle is one
   quotient point for `s<=0`.

   `(u^0,u^1,u^2)`. The `u^0` direction is timelike and `s` is spacelike.
   Signature does not enter Stokes' theorem, but it fixes the physical
   interpretation of `Sigma` as timelike.

   in `D002` with the kernel-pair equalizer. Their pullbacks to `M` are
   `C^infty`; no distributional, polyhomogeneous, edge, `b`-, or meromorphic
   forms are silently included.
4. All test forms have compact support in `U` and away from
   `s=+-epsilon`. Hence the only boundary contribution considered is the
   interface at `s=0`. There is no boundary of the support inside `Sigma`.
5. The two exact profiles are

   \[
   \rho _1(s)=\ell e^{-(L/s)^2},\qquad
   \rho _2(s)=\ell e^{-(L/s)^4}\qquad(s>0), \tag{2}
   \]

   and both are zero for `s<=0`, with `ell,L>0`. Their zero extensions and

   \[
   E^0=du^0,\quad E^1=du^1,\quad E^2=du^2,
   \quad E^3=ds,\quad E_i^4=\rho_i\eta,
   \quad \eta=d\theta+A, \tag{3}
   \]

7. Candidate (B) is conditional on a *marked*, oriented `U(1)` fiber and the

8. Candidate (C) permits independent top-degree forms on the three strata.
   This is a polydegree state choice, not the assertion that one ordinary
   homogeneous diffeological form has three different degrees.
9. No action, equations of motion, matching condition, interface stress
   tensor, symplectic current, or moving-interface law is assumed. A boundary
   compatibility relation is labeled as extra data unless it follows from the

### Conventions and boundary conditions

- Define the common orientation forms

  \[
  o_\Sigma:=du^0\wedge du^1\wedge du^2,
  \quad o_4:=o_\Sigma\wedge ds,
  \quad o_5:=o_\Sigma\wedge ds\wedge d\theta . \tag{4}
  \]

  The positive fiber orientation has `int_{S^1}dtheta=2pi`.
- Boundary orientation is outward-normal first, implemented by
  `o_boundary=i_{n_out}o_bulk`. At `s=0`, the outward normal of the core
  `s>0` is `-partial_s`, while that of the exterior `s<0` is `+partial_s`.
- Fiber forms are written with `dtheta` on the right. This fixes the sign of
  the pushforward without relying on an unstated contraction convention.
- `p_!` denotes ordinary oriented circle integration and `p_!^H` normalized
  Haar integration:

  \[
  p_!^H:=\frac1{2\pi}p_!. \tag{5}
  \]

- Coordinates and coframes have length dimension, while `theta`, `dtheta`,
  and integrated angles are dimensionless. Natural units are used.
- “Canonical from the final diffeology” means natural under diffeomorphisms of
  the unmarked diffeological object; a chosen coframe, orientation, fibration,
  density, or fundamental current is extra data.

## Derivation

### 1. Exact closure orientations

From (4), contraction with the two outward normals gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
o_{\partial M_+}
 &=\iota_{-\partial_s}
   (o_\Sigma\wedge ds\wedge d\theta)
   =+o_\Sigma\wedge d\theta,\\
o_{\partial M_-}
 &=\iota_{+\partial_s}(o_\Sigma\wedge ds)
   =-o_\Sigma .
\end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]

The signs follow because `deg(o_Sigma)=3`. Thus, after the core boundary is
pushed along its positively oriented circle, it has the **positive** common
`Sigma` orientation, whereas the exterior boundary has the **negative**
common `Sigma` orientation. This is the incidence sign needed below.

The metric signature does not modify (9). Reversing the chosen orientation of
`s` or of the circle reverses the corresponding displayed conventions, but
all later formulas transform consistently.

### 2. Equalizer lemma in arbitrary degree

Every ordinary `k`-form on `M` has a unique decomposition

\[
\alpha_k=a_k+b_{k-1}\wedge d\theta, \tag{10}
\]

where `a_k` and `b_(k-1)` are horizontal. On the collapsed branch of the
kernel pair, the independent coordinates are `(theta_1,theta_2)`, and

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_1^*\alpha_k
 &=a_k(u,s,\theta_1)+b_{k-1}(u,s,\theta_1)\wedge d\theta_1,\\
p_2^*\alpha_k
 &=a_k(u,s,\theta_2)+b_{k-1}(u,s,\theta_2)\wedge d\theta_2 .
\end{aligned} \tag{11}
\]

Because `dtheta_1` and `dtheta_2` are independent cotangent directions there,
the exact equality `p_1^*alpha=p_2^*alpha` is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
a_k\text{ is theta-independent and }b_{k-1}=0
\quad\text{for }s\leq0 .} \tag{12}
\]

The diagonal branch at `s>0` imposes no additional condition. Conversely,
(12) makes the two pullbacks agree on both branches, so it is sufficient.

Since `b_(k-1)` is `C^infty` and is zero on the open set `s<0`, every
coefficient obeys

\[
\partial_s^n\partial_u^I\partial_\theta^m b_{k-1}\big|_{s=0}=0
\qquad\text{for all }n,m,I. \tag{13}
\]

The theta-dependent part of `a_k` is flat for the same reason. Equation (13)
is not an asymptotic assumption; it follows exactly from smooth extension
through an open zero region.

### 3. Stokes formulas on the two closures

Let `alpha_4 in Omega_c^4(M_+)` and
`beta_3 in Omega_c^3(M_-)`. Ordinary Stokes' theorem and (9) give

\[
\int_{M_+}d\alpha_4=\int_{\Sigma\times S^1}j_+^*\alpha_4, \tag{14}
\]

and

\[
\int_{M_-}d\beta_3
=\int_{\partial M_-}j_-^*\beta_3
=-\int_{\Sigma,o_\Sigma}j_-^*\beta_3 . \tag{15}
\]

For `gamma_2 in Omega_c^2(Sigma)`, compact support gives

\[
\int_\Sigma d\gamma_2=0. \tag{16}
\]

Equations (14)--(16) are exact and make no quotient-smoothness assumption.

### 4. Explicit core-boundary fiber pushforward, sign, and `2pi`

Decompose the core test form as in (10):

\[
\alpha_4=a_4+b_3\wedge d\theta. \tag{17}
\]

Every horizontal four-form is proportional to
`o_Sigma wedge ds`, so `j_+^*a_4=0`. On the boundary, only the component of
`b_3` tangent to `Sigma` survives. Write it as

\[
j_+^*b_3=f(u,\theta)o_\Sigma . \tag{18}
\]

Therefore

\[
j_+^*\alpha_4=f(u,\theta)o_\Sigma\wedge d\theta . \tag{19}
\]

With vertical form written on the right, ordinary and normalized pushforward
are, by definition and direct integration,

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
p_!j_+^*\alpha_4
 &=\left(\int_0^{2\pi}f(u,\theta)d\theta\right)o_\Sigma
   =2\pi\langle f\rangle_\theta o_\Sigma,\\
p_!^Hj_+^*\alpha_4
 &=\langle f\rangle_\theta o_\Sigma,
\qquad
\langle f\rangle_\theta:=\frac1{2\pi}
\int_0^{2\pi}f(u,\theta)d\theta .
\end{aligned}} \tag{20}
\]

There is no hidden minus sign in (20): the boundary orientation is precisely
`o_Sigma wedge dtheta` by (9). Consequently (14) can be written either as

\[
\int_{M_+}d\alpha_4
=\int_\Sigma p_!j_+^*\alpha_4, \tag{21}
\]

or, after normalizing the *entire core integral* by the Haar mass,

\[
\frac1{2\pi}\int_{M_+}d\alpha_4
=\int_\Sigma p_!^Hj_+^*\alpha_4. \tag{22}
\]

Now write `j_-^*beta_3=g(u)o_Sigma`. Equations (15) and (22) align as

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{2\pi}\int_{M_+}d\alpha_4+
\int_{M_-}d\beta_3
=\int_{\Sigma,o_\Sigma}
\left(p_!^Hj_+^*\alpha_4-j_-^*\beta_3\right).} \tag{23}
\]

Thus normalized incidence cancellation would require

\[
p_!^Hj_+^*\alpha_4=j_-^*\beta_3. \tag{24}
\]

With ordinary, unnormalized core integration, (24) is replaced by
`p_!j_+^*alpha_4=j_-^*beta_3`; for a theta-independent coefficient this
contains the explicit factor `2pi`. Relative action couplings can absorb this
dimensionless normalization, but it may not be silently dropped.

### 5. The actual quotient equalizer forces zero core flux

Suppose now that `alpha_4` is the pullback of an actual smooth
quotient-diffeological four-form. Equation (12) with `k=4` says that the
vertical coefficient `b_3` vanishes for `s<=0`, and (13) says it is flat at
`s=0`. Hence (18) has `f=0`. The horizontal term already has zero boundary
pullback. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
j_+^*\alpha_4=0,
\qquad p_!j_+^*\alpha_4=0,
\qquad p_!^Hj_+^*\alpha_4=0.} \tag{25}
\]

For compact support, core Stokes consequently reduces to

\[
\boxed{\int_{M_+}d\alpha_4=0} \tag{26}
\]

for every regular descended four-form. This statement is independent of
`rho_1`, `rho_2`, a metric, or a choice between ordinary and normalized fiber
integration.

By contrast, a descended horizontal three-form can have a nonzero exterior
trace. For example, with a compactly supported smooth function `h(u,s)` that
is nonzero at `s=0`,

\[
\beta_3=h(u,s)o_\Sigma \tag{27}
\]

is theta-independent and passes the equalizer, but
`j_-^*beta_3=h(u,0)o_Sigma` need not vanish. Thus (23) is not automatically
closed by quotient descent: its first incidence term is forced to zero while
its second is not.

There is also a degree obstruction to pretending otherwise. Core Stokes acts
on a four-form and exterior Stokes acts on a three-form. A homogeneous
ordinary quotient form cannot simultaneously be both. A coupled calculus
must use shifted/polydegree data and an explicit incidence map, as candidate
(C) does.

### 6. Candidate (A): resolved metric/coframe pushforward

#### 6.1 Exact coframe volume

For either profile, the oriented coframe volume is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{vol}_{5,i}
&:=E^0\wedge E^1\wedge E^2\wedge E^3\wedge E_i^4\\
&=\rho_i\,du^0\wedge du^1\wedge du^2\wedge ds
  \wedge(d\theta+A)\\
&=\rho_i\,o_\Sigma\wedge ds\wedge d\theta .
\end{aligned} \tag{28}
\]

The last equality is exact: `A` is horizontal on a four-dimensional base, so
wedging it with all four base one-forms gives zero. No sign or connection term
is missing.

Ordinary and normalized fiber pushforward give

\[
\boxed{
p_!\operatorname{vol}_{5,i}=2\pi\rho_i o_4,
\qquad
p_!^H\operatorname{vol}_{5,i}=\rho_i o_4.} \tag{29}
\]

Thus normalized Haar removes only the circumference factor `2pi` from the
operation; it does not remove the metric radius already present in the
coframe volume.

For a theta-independent nonnegative compactly supported scalar test function
`phi` whose support lies in `0<s<min(epsilon,L)`, the two standard metric
volume functionals differ by

\[
\begin{aligned}
I_{A,1}[\phi]-I_{A,2}[\phi]
&:=\int_{M_+}\phi
 (\operatorname{vol}_{5,1}-\operatorname{vol}_{5,2})\\
&=2\pi\ell\int_{U\times(0,\epsilon)}
\phi\left[e^{-(L/s)^2}-e^{-(L/s)^4}\right]o_4>0
\end{aligned} \tag{30}
\]

whenever `phi` is nonzero. The strict sign holds on the chosen collar because
`(L/s)^4>(L/s)^2`. More generally,

\[
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_{5,2}}
     {\operatorname{vol}_{5,1}}
=\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}
=e^{-(L/s)^4+(L/s)^2}\longrightarrow0
\qquad(s\downarrow0). \tag{31}
\]

No single constant rescaling of a coupling identifies the two local volume
currents.

#### 6.2 Physical profile dependence versus forbidden resolution dependence

Equation (30) has two distinct interpretations that must not be conflated.

1. If `(E_i^A,g_i)` and the circumference `2pi rho_i` are designated physical
   fields/observables, then the two profiles are different core geometries.
   Different metric volume and action values are expected **physical core
   geometry dependence**, not a failure of covariance.
2. If the two horns are declared equivalent resolutions of one state after
   forgetting `rho_i` and the coframe metric, then using their metric volumes
   to define the supposedly intrinsic integral produces **forbidden
   resolution dependence** by (30). The bare quotient does not select one.

This distinction also explains why pushing forward the degenerate
five-volume does not create the exterior four-volume. Since `rho_i=0` on
`s<=0`, the pushforward of (28) is zero there. A nonzero exterior measure such
as `o_4` must be supplied independently; it is not the regular pushforward of
`vol_(5,i)`.

If instead one uses the Euclidean metric induced from the horn embedding in
`U x I x R^2`, its radial factor contains `sqrt(1+(rho_i')^2)` in addition to
`rho_i`. That is a different ambient-metric choice and is at least as
profile-dependent. It is not used in (28)--(31).

#### 6.3 Metric flux also vanishes for regular fields

For a smooth vector field `V`, consider the standard flux four-form
`alpha_(V,i)=i_V vol_(5,i)`. Pulling it to the core boundary leaves only its
normal component:

\[
j_+^*\alpha_{V,i}
=-\rho_i(0)V^s(u,0,\theta)o_\Sigma\wedge d\theta=0. \tag{32}
\]

The minus sign is the outward flux component `V dot (-partial_s)` in the
coordinate metric convention. Equation (32) agrees with the general
equalizer result (25). A finite nonzero limit would require, for example,
`V^s sim 1/rho_i`; such a vector is singular in the resolved coframe and its
flux form has a nonzero vertical boundary coefficient, so it is outside the
regular descended calculus.

Candidate (A) therefore supplies a valid metric integration current for each
chosen physical coframe, but not a common bare-quotient measure and not a
nonzero regular fiber-boundary flux.

### 7. Candidate (B): normalized topological Haar fiber integration

#### 7.1 Conditional, profile-independent operation

Given the marked oriented circle fibration on `X_5`, define for a compactly
supported core top form whose pullback extends smoothly to `M_+`

\[
\mathcal I_5^H(\omega_5)
:=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{M_+}q^*\omega_5
=\int_{U\times(0,\epsilon)}p_!^Hq^*\omega_5. \tag{33}
\]

This operation uses neither `rho_1` nor `rho_2`; forms identified through the
common quotient map have the same value. It is invariant under the ordinary
orientation-preserving vertical gauge transformation

\[
\theta\mapsto\theta-\alpha(u,s),
\qquad A\mapsto A+d\alpha, \tag{34}
\]

because Haar measure is translation-invariant and `eta=dtheta+A` is gauge
invariant. The decomposition into horizontal and vertical pieces changes, but
the fiber integral of the total form does not.

This is a genuine exact positive result: after the fiber marking and
orientation are added, normalized topological integration is independent of
the flat profile. It must not be overstated. It integrates differential top
forms, not scalar functions without a base density, and it deliberately does
not use metric fiber length.

In particular, a tempting profile-free density built from the persistent base
coframe would be

\[
\nu_H:=\frac1{2\pi}o_4\wedge d\theta,
\qquad p_!\nu_H=o_4. \tag{33a}
\]

It is a smooth top form on the open core but is not a global descended
five-form: its vertical coefficient is the nonzero horizontal four-form
`o_4`, violating (12) on `s<=0`. A descended replacement
`chi(s)nu_H` requires a smooth `chi` that is zero for `s<=0`; hence `chi` is
flat at the interface, its pushed density has zero interface limit, and the
choice of `chi` is not canonical. Normalized Haar therefore does not by itself
produce a unit-weight global quotient density.

#### 7.2 Failure to provide a coupled Stokes trace

The normalized Stokes relation is exactly (22). On the actual quotient
equalizer, (25) makes its right-hand side zero. Hence candidate (B) has

\[
\mathcal I_5^H(d\alpha_4)=0 \tag{35}
\]

for every compactly supported descended four-form. Normalizing the circle
cannot turn a zero trace into a nonzero trace.

A resolution four-form such as

\[
\widehat\alpha_4=\psi(u,s)o_\Sigma\wedge\eta,
\qquad \psi(u,0)\ne0, \tag{36}
\]

would instead obey

\[
p_!^Hj_+^*\widehat\alpha_4
=\psi(u,0)o_\Sigma. \tag{37}
\]

It is gauge invariant and gives precisely the degree needed to match an
exterior trace. But it does **not** descend: its vertical coefficient is
nonzero at the collapsed fiber. Equivalently, on `s>0`,

\[
o_\Sigma\wedge\eta
=o_\Sigma\wedge\frac{E_i^4}{\rho_i}, \tag{38}
\]

so using it as a quotient boundary trace requires a singular `1/rho_i`
coframe field or retention of the blown-up boundary circle. Equations
(36)--(38) exhibit an available enriched route, not a form in the bare
equalizer.

#### 7.3 Data absent from normalized Haar integration

Even when (33) is retained, it does not choose:

- a base density or coframe on `X_5`, `X_4`, or `Sigma`;
- a physical circumference or the relative normalization of four- and
  five-dimensional terms;
- an orientation if none has been supplied;
- a marked projection and group action on an unmarked final diffeological
  space;
- an interface trace beyond the zero regular equalizer trace; or
- matching, boundary, corner, and moving-interface variation rules.

Candidate (B) is therefore a useful conditional operation, but not by itself
an action calculus.

### 8. Candidate (C): a stratified incidence functional

#### 8.1 Independent top degrees and exact Stokes formula

Let an action-density candidate be a triple

\[
(\mathcal L_5,\mathcal L_4,\mathcal L_\Sigma)
\in\Omega_c^5(M_+)\oplus\Omega_c^4(M_-)
\oplus\Omega_c^3(\Sigma), \tag{39}
\]

and define, with normalized core convention,

\[
\mathcal I_C(\mathcal L_5,\mathcal L_4,\mathcal L_\Sigma)
:=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{M_+}\mathcal L_5
+\int_{M_-}\mathcal L_4
+\int_\Sigma\mathcal L_\Sigma. \tag{40}
\]

Relative couplings or weights may multiply each term; they are suppressed in
(40) only to display incidence signs. For compactly supported test potentials
`(alpha_4,beta_3,gamma_2)`, equations (16) and (23) give the exact closure
formula

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal I_C(d\alpha_4,d\beta_3,d\gamma_2)
=\int_\Sigma
\left(p_!^Hj_+^*\alpha_4-j_-^*\beta_3\right).} \tag{41}
\]

Thus the core and exterior have opposite incidence signs. A Stokes-closed
test triple can be selected by the additional compatibility condition (24),
or an interface contribution can cancel the mismatch. Explicitly, the
top-degree completion

\[
\mathcal L_\Sigma^{\rm inc}
=d\gamma_2-p_!^Hj_+^*\alpha_4+j_-^*\beta_3 \tag{42}
\]

obeys

\[
\mathcal I_C(d\alpha_4,d\beta_3,
\mathcal L_\Sigma^{\rm inc})=0. \tag{43}
\]

Equation (42) is only the exact top-degree Stokes completion. A full
all-degree differential with square zero, products, pullbacks, and field-space
variation would require a declared shifted/mapping-cone complex and is not
silently claimed here.

With weights `c_5,c_4,c_Sigma`, the uncancelled right-hand side of (41) is

\[
\int_\Sigma
\left(c_5p_!^Hj_+^*\alpha_4-c_4j_-^*\beta_3\right), \tag{44}
\]

so even the matching normalization is extra data. Using ordinary rather than
normalized fiber integration replaces `p_!^H` by `p_!` and restores the
explicit `2pi`.

#### 8.2 What the equalizer permits

For regular descended `alpha_4`, (25) reduces (41) to

\[
\mathcal I_C(d\alpha_4,d\beta_3,d\gamma_2)
=-\int_\Sigma j_-^*\beta_3. \tag{45}
\]

Therefore the bare equalizer supplies only the trivial core matching
condition `j_-^*beta_3=0`. It does not generate a nonzero exchange between
the disappearing circle and the four-dimensional exterior.

This does not make (40) useless. An independently supplied interface density
can couple the horizontal one-sided field traces that remain well-defined.
For a coupling specifically to the disappearing fiber flux, however, one must
enlarge the trace data beyond regular descended forms, for example by (36) or
one of the mechanisms listed in section 10.

#### 8.3 Exact status of candidate (C)

Candidate (C) is the only one of the three that has enough independent degrees
to *host* a general mixed-dimensional action and its interface term. But
(39)--(44) are a chosen stratified current/incidence structure. The final
diffeology supplies the de Rham pullback and `d`; it does not select the three
fundamental currents, their weights, or the nonzero trace map. Thus (C) evades
an all-actions no-go while failing to be canonical from the bare quotient.

### 9. No canonical measure follows from the final diffeology alone

There is already an obstruction on either regular stratum. Suppose a
nonzero smooth density were assigned naturally by the diffeology alone. At a
point of a `d`-dimensional regular chart, a local diffeomorphism fixing the
point can have derivative `lambda I` for any `lambda>0`. Naturality would
require the density value `mu_x` to satisfy

\[
\mu_x=|\det(\lambda I)|\mu_x=\lambda^d\mu_x. \tag{46}
\]

For `lambda != 1`, equation (46) forces `mu_x=0`. A reflection similarly
shows that an orientation is not selected. Therefore an unmarked smooth or
diffeological structure has no nonzero diffeomorphism-natural local measure
or orientation.

This does not deny the standard fact that an **oriented** manifold integrates
compactly supported top forms without a metric. It says that orientation and
the top form being integrated are extra inputs. At changing dimension one
additionally needs separate fundamental currents and their incidence. The
final diffeology fixes none of the independent rescalings

\[
(\mathcal I_5,\mathcal I_4,\mathcal I_\Sigma)
\longmapsto(c_5\mathcal I_5,c_4\mathcal I_4,
c_\Sigma\mathcal I_\Sigma). \tag{47}
\]

Nor does it, after the presentation `q:M->X` is forgotten, canonically retain
the particular circle projection used in (8). Conditional Haar uniqueness on
a marked compact group fiber is not a canonical scalar measure on the final
unmarked quotient.

### 10. Minimum extra data for a nontrivial coupled variational principle

The exact benchmark identifies the following minimum choices. Different
enriched theories may realize them differently.

1. **Oriented/cooriented stratification and supports:** orientations of
   `X_5`, `X_4`, and `Sigma`, a choice of which side is the core, fundamental
   integration currents, and outer boundary/falloff conditions.
2. **Stratum densities or physical coframes:** enough data to turn scalar
   Lagrangians into top forms on every stratum. If `rho` enters the physical
   five-coframe, its profile dependence is retained as geometry rather than
   mislabeled resolution gauge.
3. **Relative dimensional couplings and `2pi` convention:** coefficients for
   five-, four-, and three-dimensional terms, including any length scale
   needed to compare their boundary variations, and an explicit choice of
   ordinary versus normalized circle integration.
4. **Marked incidence geometry:** an oriented blow-up boundary
   `Sigma x S^1`, its projection `p`, and a declared transformation law under
   admissible changes of resolution. Without this, `p_!` is not intrinsic.
5. **An interface density and field content:** an independent
   `L_Sigma in Omega^3(Sigma)` can couple regular horizontal traces and carry
   surface stress, charge, or canonical data. Its dynamics are not fixed by
   Stokes' theorem.
6. **A nonzero fiber-trace mechanism, if fiber coupling is desired:** at
   least one of
   - a specified **blow-up/renormalized trace**, such as
     `Tr_rho(b):=lim_(s downarrow 0)b/rho`, with its domain fixed;
   - a controlled singular field proportional to `1/rho`, as in (38), with
     finite-action and variation conditions;
   - a finite-regularity, one-sided, polyhomogeneous, edge, or distributional
     calculus retaining prescribed normal coefficients; or
   - an independent boundary-circle field before collapse.

   The smooth equalizer itself supplies none of these.
7. **Resolution-independence law for generalized traces:** the ideal of flat
   functions has no preferred generator. In particular,
   `rho_2/rho_1 -> 0`; a trace normalized by `rho_1` and one normalized by
   `rho_2` need not agree. The selected defining scale must be physical or its
   change must have a compensating transformation rule.
8. **Complete variational data:** admissible one-sided field variations,
   interface displacement, corner terms, and whether incidence matching is
   imposed, derived from an interface variation, or replaced by flux into
   interface fields.
9. **Gauge and charge data:** the gauge group, allowed singular gauge
   transformations, boundary charges, and a proof that any forgotten
   direction is a presymplectic null direction. Ordinary `U(1)` covariance of
   (33) does not establish this.

Items 1--5 suffice to write a nontrivially coupled *horizontal* stratified
functional. Item 6 is additionally required for a nonzero regularized flux of
the disappearing fiber sector. Items 7--9 are required before calling the
result resolution-independent and variationally complete.

## Dimensional check

The basic dimensions are

\[
[u^\alpha]=[s]=[L]=[\ell]=[\rho_i]=\mathsf L,
\qquad [\theta]=[d\theta]=1. \tag{48}
\]

The arguments `(L/s)^2` and `(L/s)^4` are dimensionless. From (28),

\[
[\operatorname{vol}_{5,i}]=\mathsf L^5,
\qquad [o_4]=\mathsf L^4,
\qquad [o_\Sigma]=\mathsf L^3. \tag{49}
\]

Fiber integration over dimensionless `theta` does not change length
dimension, so both sides of
`p_!^H vol_(5,i)=rho_i o_4` have dimension `L^5`. This is **not** the ordinary
four-volume `o_4`; dividing by a length would be required to obtain one.
Conversely, the profile-free topological form `nu_H` in (33a) has form degree
five but physical dimension `L^4`; a dimensionful coupling is required if it
is to replace a coframe five-volume in an action.

If an action is written as

\[
S=\int_{X_5}\mathscr L_5\operatorname{vol}_5
+\int_{X_4}\mathscr L_4 o_4
+\int_\Sigma\mathscr L_\Sigma o_\Sigma, \tag{50}
\]

then dimensionlessness requires
`[mathscr L_5]=L^-5`, `[mathscr L_4]=L^-4`, and
`[mathscr L_Sigma]=L^-3`, after all gravitational or matter couplings are
included. Likewise, a raw coframe-built core boundary four-form has one more
length dimension than a raw exterior three-form; the relative couplings or an
interface scale must make (24) dimensionally meaningful. Normalized Haar
provides no length scale. The factor `2pi` is dimensionless and is accounted
for explicitly in (20), (29), and (44).

\boxed{p_!\operatorname{vol}_{5,i}=2\pi\rho_i o_4,
\qquad p_!^H\operatorname{vol}_{5,i}=\rho_i o_4,} \tag{53}
