# D005: Gauge-covariant boundary-circle action and edge-mode test

## Question addressed

What is the smallest gauge-covariant enriched boundary-circle action that
integrates the finite interface momentum, and when do its reference choices
admit or destroy a nondegenerate matched triad?

## Branch charter

Retrospective lifecycle metadata: test field-space closure of the desired
momentum one-form, derive the complete companion variations, and compare
reference-free, fixed-reference, freely varied, and gauge-orbit branches.

## Distinction from sibling branches

This artifact predates breadth-first exploration and was not a sibling branch.
It constructs and varies an enriched action rather than deriving the bare
boundary mismatch of D004 or attempting the cut presymplectic classification
reserved for D006.

## Target claim

This artifact performs the next fixed-interface test requested at the end of
`D004`. The proposed claims `C014` and `C015` are not inserted into the claim
ledger here.

**Proposition A (field-space integrability; proposed C014).** In the basic
persistent sector defined below, the desired finite connection one-form on
field space,

\[
 \mathcal A_Q(\delta)
 =\frac{\gamma}{4}\int_{\widetilde\Sigma}
 \epsilon_{abcd}\,\delta\omega_+^{ab}\wedge e_+^c\wedge e_+^d
 \wedge\eta , \tag{1}
\]

is not closed, and hence is not by itself the variation of a boundary action,
when the coframe or the vertical normalization of `eta` is freely varied. It
is closed on a coframe- and `eta`-fixed polarization. On the full field space
it occurs as one term in the exact variation of

\[
 B_a=\frac{\gamma}{4}\int_{\widetilde\Sigma}
 \epsilon_{abcd}(\omega_+^{ab}-\bar a^{ab})
 \wedge e_+^c\wedge e_+^d\wedge\eta , \tag{2}
\]

where `bar a` is a connection in the core frame. The same primitive
unavoidably also varies the coframe, `bar a` (and therefore its dressing edge
field), and an unconstrained `eta`. These companion variations cannot be
dropped while calling (2) an action.

**Proposition B (minimal matching action; proposed C015).** Let the core and
exterior persistent Lorentz bundles be independent, let `g` be a bundle
isomorphism between their interface restrictions, and impose connection and
coframe matching with covariant two-form multipliers. There are two sharply
different cases.

1. In the reference-free ansatz, the connection `a` in (2) is the same
   dynamical exterior connection that is matched to `omega_+`. The complete
   action is local, field-space integrable, and invariant under independent
   persistent `SO(1,3)` gauges, but it fails: its core connection equation
   absorbs the entire polarization momentum into the matching multiplier, and
   the exterior connection equation then requires `P^-_{ab}=0`. This is
   incompatible with a rank-three induced triad. No coupling choice repairs
   this cancellation.
2. The smallest algebraic repair is to distinguish a reference connection
   `a` from the dynamical exterior connection `varpi`. If `a` is fixed in
   field space but transforms as a connection, the same single action yields

   \[
   Q^+_{ab}= (g\cdot P^-)_{ab},\qquad
   Q^+_{ab}=\frac{2\pi\gamma}{4}\epsilon_{abcd}
       e_+^c\wedge e_+^d . \tag{3}
   \]

   With coframe matching and a rank-three triad this requires

   \[
   2\pi\gamma=\frac1{\kappa_4}. \tag{4}
   \]

   Identifying `gamma=ell_*/kappa_5` gives
   `2pi ell_*/kappa_5=1/kappa_4`. The repair is not free: it introduces the
   reference/edge datum and the exact companion equation

   \[
   \epsilon_{abcd}(\omega_+-\bar a)^{ab}\wedge e_+^d=0. \tag{5}
   \]

   Equation (5) restricts the common connection but neither forces a
   rank-three triad to degenerate nor forces the connection to be flat. If
   `a` is instead varied as an unrestricted connection, its equation is
   `Q^+=0`, and the repair fails. Restricting `a` to a gauge orbit avoids that
   algebraic degeneracy only by adding an edge equation and possible edge
   charge.

For a normalized marked circle, `eta` may be fixed or varied within the affine
space of principal `U(1)` connections without changing the action in this
basic sector. Varying its vertical normalization produces the additional
equation `epsilon K e e=0`; thus the period/vertical normalization must be
fixed if that equation is not intended. The construction contains no collapse
profile `rho`, but it necessarily retains a marked circle current, `g`,
multipliers, a relative length/coupling, and in the repaired case a reference
or further edge field. It is therefore profile-independent only on an enriched
state space, not on the bare quotient of `D002`.

These propositions are exact for the restricted fixed-interface action and
variation class below. They do not address a moving `Sigma`, Hamiltonian
closure, or a quantum path integral.

## Notation

The independent Lorentz gauges are denoted by local bundle automorphisms
`h_+` and `h_-`. With the convention `e -> h e` and
`omega -> h omega h^-1-dh h^-1`, the edge field transforms as

\[
 \boxed{g\longmapsto h_+g h_-^{-1}.} \tag{11}
\]

Exterior fields dressed into the core frame are

\[
 \bar e:=g e_-,\qquad
 \bar\varpi:=g\varpi g^{-1}-dg\,g^{-1},\qquad
 \bar a:=g a g^{-1}-dg\,g^{-1}. \tag{12}
\]

The tensorial matching differences and the polarization difference are

\[
 \Delta e:=e_+-\bar e,\qquad
 \Delta\omega:=\omega_+-\bar\varpi,\qquad
 K:=\omega_+-\bar a. \tag{13}
\]

Under (11), `bar varpi` and `bar a` transform as connections on `P_+`, while
`Delta omega` and `K` transform homogeneously in `ad P_+`; `bar e` and
`Delta e` transform as core vectors. Thus every difference in (13) is a
globally meaningful associated-bundle form.

Set

\[
 b:=2\pi\gamma,\qquad
 Q_{ab}:=\frac b4\epsilon_{abcd}e_+^c\wedge e_+^d,\qquad
 H_c(K):=-\frac b2\epsilon_{abcd}K^{ab}\wedge e_+^d . \tag{14}
\]

`Q` is the finite core connection momentum and `H` is the companion coframe
Euler form after ordinary circle integration. For normalized Haar integration,
one instead has `b=gamma`; no `2pi` is then hidden in `gamma`.

For a core adjoint two-form `X`, `g^{-1}\cdot X` denotes the same coadjoint
form in the exterior frame. Define the Lorentz moment map of a vector-valued
two-form and vector-valued one-form by

\[
 (\mu\diamond v)_{ab}:=\mu_{[a}\wedge v_{b]},\qquad
 \mu_a\wedge\xi^a{}_b v^b
 =(\mu\diamond v)_{ab}\xi^{ab}, \tag{15}
\]

where brackets have unit weight. Pairings of adjoint forms are denoted by
angle brackets when this avoids component clutter.

For the edge variation use

\[
 \xi:=\delta g\,g^{-1}\in\Omega^0(\Sigma,\operatorname{ad}P_+). \tag{16}
\]

## Derivation

### 1. Bundle covariance of the dressing

Apply (11) and the independent gauge transformations

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \omega_+'&=h_+\omega_+h_+^{-1}-dh_+h_+^{-1},
 &e_+'&=h_+e_+,\\
 \varpi'&=h_-\varpi h_-^{-1}-dh_-h_-^{-1},
 &e_-'&=h_-e_-,\\
 a'&=h_-a h_-^{-1}-dh_-h_-^{-1}.
\end{aligned} \tag{17}
\]

Direct substitution gives the exact relations

\[
 \bar\varpi'=h_+\bar\varpi h_+^{-1}-dh_+h_+^{-1},
 \qquad
 \bar a'=h_+\bar a h_+^{-1}-dh_+h_+^{-1},
 \qquad
 \bar e'=h_+\bar e . \tag{18}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \Delta\omega'=h_+\Delta\omega h_+^{-1},\qquad
 K'=h_+K h_+^{-1},\qquad
 \Delta e'=h_+\Delta e . \tag{19}
\]

Equations (18)--(19) are exact bundle identities. They show why subtracting
bare local connection representatives without `g` would be meaningless.

The infinitesimal field-space versions needed later follow from (12) and
(16):

\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \delta\bar\varpi
   &=g\,\delta\varpi\,g^{-1}-D_{\bar\varpi}\xi,\\
 \delta\bar a
   &=g\,\delta a\,g^{-1}-D_{\bar a}\xi,\\
 \delta\bar e&=g\,\delta e_-+\xi\bar e,\\
 \delta\Delta\omega
   &=\delta\omega_+-g\delta\varpi g^{-1}
      +D_{\bar\varpi}\xi,\\
 \delta K&=\delta\omega_+-g\delta a g^{-1}
      +D_{\bar a}\xi,\\
 \delta\Delta e&=\delta e_+-g\delta e_- -\xi\bar e .
\end{aligned}} \tag{20}
\]

The minus sign in `delta bar varpi = ...-D xi` follows from the connection
convention in (17); it will fix the edge equation and corner charge.

### 2. Bare bulk actions and their fixed-interface trace

For completeness, the bulk part of the candidate is exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
 S_5={}&\int_{M_+}\left[
 \frac1{12\kappa_5}\epsilon_{ABCDE}R^{AB}\wedge E^C\wedge E^D\wedge E^E
 -\frac{\Lambda_5}{120\kappa_5}\epsilon_{ABCDE}
 E^A\wedge E^B\wedge E^C\wedge E^D\wedge E^E\right],\\
 S_4={}&\int_{M_-}\left[
 \frac1{4\kappa_4}\epsilon_{abcd}{\cal R}^{ab}\wedge e_-^c\wedge e_-^d
 -\frac{\Lambda_4}{24\kappa_4}\epsilon_{abcd}
 e_-^a\wedge e_-^b\wedge e_-^c\wedge e_-^d\right].
\end{aligned} \tag{21}
\]

Their complete bulk Euler forms are equations (25)--(26) of `D004`. In the
present regularity class their only nonzero common-interface potential is

\[
 \boxed{\left.\delta(S_5+S_4)\right|_\Sigma
 =-\int_\Sigma\delta\varpi^{ab}\wedge P^-_{ab}.} \tag{22}
\]

There is no bare boundary term proportional to `delta e_-`, and the pushed
five-dimensional potential is exactly zero. Equation (22), not a small-radius
approximation, is the input that the boundary-circle term must balance.

### 3. Circle reduction of the polarization term

Let `L_3` be any basic three-form on `Sigma`. With fiber forms on the right,

\[
 \int_{\widetilde\Sigma}p^*L_3\wedge\eta
 =2\pi\int_\Sigma L_3. \tag{23}
\]

The horizontal part `A` in `eta=dtheta+A` gives a horizontal four-form on the
three-dimensional base and vanishes identically. Hence (23) is independent of
`A` and of every collapse profile `rho`.

Use the connection difference `K` in (13) and define

\[
 \boxed{B_a
 =\frac{\gamma}{4}\int_{\widetilde\Sigma}
 \epsilon_{abcd}K^{ab}\wedge e_+^c\wedge e_+^d\wedge\eta
 =\frac b4\int_\Sigma\epsilon_{abcd}K^{ab}\wedge e_+^c\wedge e_+^d.}
 \tag{24}
\]

This term is strictly Lorentz invariant because `K` is tensorial; it is
transgression-like in its use of two connections, but no Chern--Weil
transgression theorem is being claimed.

For fixed normalized `eta`, direct variation gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \delta B_a
 =\int_\Sigma\left[
   \delta K^{ab}\wedge Q_{ab}
   +\delta e_+^c\wedge H_c(K)\right].
\end{aligned} \tag{25}
\]

To verify the coframe coefficient, the two coframe variations combine as

\[
 \frac b4\epsilon_{abcd}K^{ab}\wedge
 (\delta e_+^c\wedge e_+^d+e_+^c\wedge\delta e_+^d)
 =-\frac b2\epsilon_{abcd}\delta e_+^c\wedge K^{ab}\wedge e_+^d,
 \tag{26}
\]

which is precisely `delta e_+^c wedge H_c`. This is the companion variation
that was only noted, not incorporated, in `D004`.

For a general variation of the circle form there is additionally

\[
 \left.\delta B_a\right|_{\delta\eta}
 =\frac\gamma4\int_{\widetilde\Sigma}
 \epsilon_{abcd}K^{ab}\wedge e_+^c\wedge e_+^d\wedge\delta\eta .
 \tag{27}
\]

### 4. Exact field-space integrability test

Consider only the desired connection one-form (1). For two commuting
variations, its field-space exterior derivative is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 (\mathbb d\mathcal A_Q)(\delta_1,\delta_2)
 =\frac\gamma4\int_{\widetilde\Sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}
 \big[&\delta_2\omega_+^{ab}\wedge
       \delta_1(e_+^c\wedge e_+^d\wedge\eta)\\
      -&\delta_1\omega_+^{ab}\wedge
       \delta_2(e_+^c\wedge e_+^d\wedge\eta)\big].
\end{aligned} \tag{28}
\]

This is generically nonzero: choose `delta_1 e_+` and `delta_2 omega_+` with
overlapping support while setting the other two components to zero. Therefore

\[
 \boxed{\mathbb d\mathcal A_Q\ne0
 \quad\hbox{on the free }(\omega_+,e_+,\eta)\hbox{ field space}.} \tag{29}
\]

It becomes closed if `delta e_+=delta eta=0`. More generally, (24) is a
primitive only for the **complete** one-form (25), (27), and the `bar a/g`
terms generated by (20). Equation `mathbb d^2 B_a=0` then guarantees that the
mixed curls cancel. Thus the finite momentum component is integrable as part
of one action, not as an isolated free-coframe potential.

**Abelianized finite-dimensional lemma (illustration only).** For one mode,
`alpha=q^2 dA` has `d alpha=2q dq wedge dA`, while
`d[(A-A_0)q^2]=q^2dA+2q(A-A_0)dq`. The second term is the finite-dimensional
analogue of (26). This lemma is not used in place of (28).

There is a second, independent integrability fact relevant to matching. If
`K=Delta omega`, then `B_a` vanishes on the connection-matching surface and
its differential annihilates every tangent variation with
`delta Delta omega=0` and fixed coframe. Its connection gradient is normal to
the matching surface. A multiplier can absorb such a normal gradient, but it
cannot turn it into momentum along the common connection. Section 7 displays
this obstruction in the complete equations.

### 5. Gauge-covariant master action

The minimum algebraic matching functional is

\[
 S_{\rm match}=\int_\Sigma\left[
 \mu_a\wedge\Delta e^a+\lambda_{ab}\wedge\Delta\omega^{ab}\right].
 \tag{30}
\]

Both terms are globally defined and invariant when the multipliers transform
coadjointly in the core frame. The master family is

\[
 \boxed{S[a]=S_5+S_4+B_a+S_{\rm match}.} \tag{31}
\]

No equality of bundle representatives is imposed before variation. The
multiplier equations will derive it.

### 6. Complete first variation of the master family

First treat `a` and `varpi` as independent so that every companion term is
visible. Substitute (20) into (25) and vary (30). Covariantly integrating the
`D xi` terms gives

\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \left.\delta S[a]\right|_\Sigma
 =\int_\Sigma\Big\{&
 \delta\omega_+^{ab}\wedge(Q_{ab}+\lambda_{ab})\\
 &-\delta\varpi^{ab}\wedge
    \big[P^-_{ab}+(g^{-1}\!\cdot\lambda)_{ab}\big]\\
 &-\delta a^{ab}\wedge(g^{-1}\!\cdot Q)_{ab}\\
 &+\delta e_+^a\wedge\big[H_a(K)+\mu_a\big]
 -\delta e_-^a\wedge(g^{-1}\!\cdot\mu)_a\\
 &+\delta\lambda_{ab}\wedge\Delta\omega^{ab}
 +\delta\mu_a\wedge\Delta e^a\\
 &-\left\langle\xi,
 D_{\bar a}Q+D_{\bar\varpi}\lambda
     +\mu\diamond\bar e\right\rangle\Big\}
 +\left.\delta B_a\right|_{\delta\eta}\\
 &+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\langle Q+\lambda,\xi\rangle .
\end{aligned}} \tag{32}
\]

Here the `delta a` line is omitted when `a` is fixed. If `a=varpi` is the same
field, the `delta a` and `delta varpi` coefficients must instead be added;
they must not be varied independently and then counted twice.

The signs in the edge line follow from

\[
 \int_\Sigma\langle Q,D_{\bar a}\xi\rangle
 =\int_{\partial\Sigma}\langle Q,\xi\rangle
 -\int_\Sigma\langle D_{\bar a}Q,\xi\rangle, \tag{33}
\]

and the identical identity for `lambda`. Equation (32) contains the complete
variation with respect to both boundary connections, both coframes, both
multipliers, `g`, the reference connection when it is varied, and `eta`.

The bulk equations from (21) accompany (32) but are unchanged. No field-space
term has been discarded to obtain (32).

### 7. Reference-free minimal ansatz: exact failure

Set

\[
 a=\varpi,\qquad \bar a=\bar\varpi,
 \qquad K=\Delta\omega. \tag{34}
\]

The connection variations in (32) combine. With

\[
 \Lambda_{ab}:=Q_{ab}+\lambda_{ab}, \tag{35}
\]

the complete interface equations are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\Delta\omega=0,\qquad \Delta e=0, &&\text{(multipliers)}\\
 &\Lambda=0, &&\text{(core connection)}\\
 &P^-+g^{-1}\!\cdot\Lambda=0, &&\text{(exterior connection)}\\
 &H(\Delta\omega)+\mu=0, &&\text{(core coframe)}\\
 &g^{-1}\!\cdot\mu=0, &&\text{(exterior coframe)}\\
 &D_{\bar\varpi}\Lambda+\mu\diamond\bar e=0,
 &&\text{(`g` equation).}
\end{aligned} \tag{36}
\]

The last three equations are consistent with matching: `mu=0`,
`H(Delta omega)=0`, and the `g` equation is then automatic. The connection
equations, however, give

\[
 \boxed{P^-_{ab}=0.} \tag{37}
\]

This conclusion is independent of `gamma`, its sign, or the circle
normalization. The polarization current enters the exterior equation only in
the same combination `Lambda` already set to zero by the core equation.

The internal Hodge map on bivectors is invertible, so (10) and (37) imply

\[
 e_-^c\wedge e_-^d=0\quad\hbox{for every }c,d. \tag{38}
\]

All one-forms `e_-^a` are then mutually proportional at each point and the
induced coframe has rank at most one. In particular, (37) is incompatible with
the intended rank-three triad. The reference-free, gauge-covariant
two-connection ansatz therefore fails exactly; it does not produce a momentum
matching law.

This is not a sign accident. Restricted to `Delta omega=0`, any differentiable
functional depending on the connections only through `Delta omega` has zero
derivative along the common-connection tangent. The uncancelled exterior
Palatini derivative in (22) is along that tangent.

### 8. Smallest algebraic repair: split reference and dynamical connection

Now take `a` to be a distinct connection held fixed in field space, while
retaining its connection transformation law in (17). The `delta a` equation
is absent, but every other line of (32) remains. Stationarity gives

\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
 &\Delta\omega=0,\qquad \Delta e=0,\\
 &Q+\lambda=0,\\
 &P^-+g^{-1}\!\cdot\lambda=0,\\
 &H(K)+\mu=0,\qquad g^{-1}\!\cdot\mu=0,\\
 &D_{\bar a}Q+D_{\bar\varpi}\lambda
      +\mu\diamond\bar e=0.
\end{aligned}} \tag{39}
\]

The two connection equations now yield the genuine incidence relation

\[
 \boxed{Q=g\cdot P^-.} \tag{40}
\]

Using `Delta e=0`, proper-Lorentz invariance of `epsilon`, and (10), equation
(40) becomes

\[
 \frac14\left(b-\frac1{\kappa_4}\right)
 \epsilon_{abcd}e_+^c\wedge e_+^d=0. \tag{41}
\]

For a rank-three triad at least one of these two-forms is nonzero. Therefore

\[
 \boxed{b=2\pi\gamma=\frac1{\kappa_4}.} \tag{42}
\]

If the boundary coefficient is written in core units,

\[
 \gamma=\frac{\ell_*}{\kappa_5}, \tag{43}
\]

then

\[
 \boxed{\frac{2\pi\ell_*}{\kappa_5}=\frac1{\kappa_4},
 \qquad \ell_*=\frac{\kappa_5}{2\pi\kappa_4}.} \tag{44}
\]

For normalized Haar integration, (42)--(44) have no `2pi`: the effective
definition is `b=gamma`. Equation (44) is extra incidence normalization; it is
not derived from the vanishing bare core potential.

The coframe equations in (39) give

\[
 \mu=0,\qquad
 \boxed{H_c(K)=-\frac b2\epsilon_{abcd}K^{ab}\wedge e_+^d=0.} \tag{45}
\]

Thus the integrable polarization does not merely supply (40); it also imposes
the companion connection/coframe relation (45).

After using `lambda=-Q`, `mu=0`, and `bar varpi=omega_+`, the `g` equation is

\[
 D_{\bar a}Q-D_{\omega_+}Q=-[K,Q]=0. \tag{46}
\]

This equation is not an independent flatness condition. Lorentz invariance of
`B_a` gives the exact algebraic Noether identity

\[
 [K,Q]+H\diamond e_+=0. \tag{47}
\]

Consequently (46) follows from (45). This is a check that the dynamic
isomorphism `g` has not introduced an inconsistent additional Lorentz
equation in the fixed-reference repair.

### 9. What the companion coframe equation does and does not force

To determine the rank of (45), choose an adapted core frame at one interface
point. Let `n` be the internal normal to the rank-three image and let
`i,j,k=0,1,2` label the tangential internal directions:

\[
 e_+^n=0,\qquad (e_+^0,e_+^1,e_+^2)
 \text{ is a coframe on }T^*\Sigma. \tag{48}
\]

The tangential and mixed parts of (45) are, up to nonzero common factors,

\[
 \epsilon_{jik}K^{nj}\wedge e_+^k=0,
 \qquad
 \epsilon_{ijk}K^{ij}\wedge e_+^k=0. \tag{49}
\]

Expand

\[
 K^{ni}=M^i{}_j e_+^j,
 \qquad
 k^i:=\frac12\epsilon^i{}_{jk}K^{jk}=S^i{}_j e_+^j . \tag{50}
\]

Applying the invertible three-dimensional Hodge map to the first equation in
(49) gives

\[
 M^m{}_i-(\operatorname{tr}M)\delta^m_i=0. \tag{51}
\]

Taking its trace gives `tr M=3 tr M`, hence

\[
 M=0. \tag{52}
\]

The second equation in (49) says exactly that the antisymmetric part of `S`
vanishes:

\[
 S_{ij}=S_{ji}. \tag{53}
\]

Therefore (45) removes all nine mixed components and three combinations of
the tangential components, but leaves the six components of a symmetric
`S_ij`. In particular,

\[
 \boxed{H(K)=0\ \not\Rightarrow\ K=0
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad H(K)=0\ \not\Rightarrow\ R(\omega_+)=0.} \tag{54}
\]

For example, on a planar triad with a flat reference `bar a=0`, a nonzero
constant choice `K^{ij}=c\epsilon^{ij}{}_k e_+^k`, `K^{ni}=0`, obeys (53) and
has generically nonzero `K wedge K` curvature. Thus no flat-connection claim
is hidden in (45).

There are, however, two exact degeneracy branches:

1. if (42) is not satisfied, (41) forces all pairwise coframe wedges to vanish
   and the triad rank is at most one;
2. if the reference connection `a` is varied as an unrestricted independent
   one-form, the third line of (32) gives

   \[
   g^{-1}\!\cdot Q=0, \tag{55}
   \]

   which again forces rank at most one.

Thus a nondegenerate repaired branch requires both the coupling relation and a
reference that is fixed or whose variations are restricted by additional edge
structure.

### 10. Fixed reference versus a dynamical edge reference

A fixed `a` is the smallest algebraic repair, but its gauge status must be
stated carefully.

- If `a` transforms as in (17), (31) is covariant under both Lorentz bundles
  as a family of variational problems with background connection data.
- If one freezes the local coefficients of `a` and refuses to transform them,
  the exterior Lorentz group is reduced to the stabilizer of that
  representative. Calling the result invariant under arbitrary `h_-` would
  be false.
- If every connection datum is required to be dynamical, unrestricted
  variation fails by (55).

The smallest Stueckelberg alternative restricts `a` to the gauge orbit of a
seed connection `a_0`. Locally write

\[
 a=u a_0u^{-1}-du\,u^{-1}, \tag{56}
\]

where `u` is an additional exterior edge frame. With
`zeta=delta u u^-1`,

\[
 \delta a=-D_a\zeta. \tag{57}
\]

For a fixed seed frame, `u -> h_-u` makes (56) transform as the exterior
connection in (17); a separate seed-frame gauge may equivalently act on the
right.

Substituting (57), rather than an arbitrary `delta a`, into (32) gives the
edge equation and corner potential

\[
 \boxed{D_{\bar a}Q=0,\qquad
 \Theta_u^{\rm corner}=\int_{\partial\Sigma}
 \langle Q,g\zeta g^{-1}\rangle.} \tag{58}
\]

This avoids the algebraic condition `Q=0`, but adds a differential equation,
the seed/orbit data, and a possible nonzero Lorentz edge charge because `Q` is
nonzero on the desired branch. It is not the same theory as a fixed
reference.

The `g` integration by parts in the master action similarly exposes

\[
 \Theta_g^{\rm corner}=\int_{\partial\Sigma}
 \langle Q+\lambda,\delta g\,g^{-1}\rangle. \tag{59}
\]

The core connection equation sets `Q+lambda=0` on the complete boundary shell,
but (59) is the off-shell moment map that must be included in a canonical
analysis. With the compact-support boundary condition it contributes no term
to the present Euler equations.

Because all Lorentz connections occur in tensorial differences and no
Chern--Simons form of a single connection is used, the local classical action
has no Lorentz gauge anomaly. Whether transformations nonzero on temporal
corners are gauge null or carry charges is not decided by Lagrangian
invariance alone; equations (58)--(59) identify the candidate charges for the
next canonical test.

### 11. `eta` variation and vertical `U(1)`

The three `eta` choices in assumption 10 give distinct answers.

#### 11.1 Fixed `eta`

If `delta eta=0`, equation (27) is absent. The marked orientation and period
are external incidence data. This is the smallest variational domain.

#### 11.2 Dynamical normalized principal connection

For a normalized connection, `delta eta=p^*delta A` is horizontal. The other
three factors in (27) are a basic horizontal three-form, so

\[
 p^*\!\left(\epsilon_{abcd}K^{ab}\wedge e_+^c\wedge e_+^d
 \wedge\delta A\right)=0 \tag{60}
\]

by the dimension of `Sigma`. Thus `A` can be varied without an Euler equation:
the action depends only on the fixed fiber period in this restricted basic
sector.

Under the active local shift `eta -> eta+p^*d alpha`, the change of (24) is the
same horizontal four-form and vanishes identically. Under the simultaneous
section change `theta -> theta-alpha`, `A -> A+d alpha`, `eta` is unchanged.
Therefore there is no vertical `U(1)` anomaly and no `A`-charge from (24) in
this sector.

#### 11.3 Varying the vertical normalization

If `delta eta` contains `(delta nu)eta`, equation (27) gives

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal L_K:=\frac b4\epsilon_{abcd}K^{ab}\wedge
 e_+^c\wedge e_+^d=0.} \tag{61}
\]

This is an additional Euler equation. It is related to the free-coframe
equation by the exact identity

\[
 e_+^c\wedge H_c(K)=2\mathcal L_K. \tag{62}
\]

Hence (61) is redundant after all four equations (45) are imposed in the
present free-coframe action, but it is not absent from the variation and would
be independent in a coframe-fixed polarization. To avoid introducing it by
definition, the vertical normalization/period must be fixed. It is not
necessary to fix the horizontal `U(1)` connection `A`.

In the failed reference-free ansatz, `K=Delta omega=0` also makes (61)
automatic, but does not cure (37).

### 12. Profile independence and unavoidable enrichment

Neither (24) nor (30) contains the collapse profile `rho`. For two resolutions
with the same persistent traces, marked circle current, and edge/reference

\[
 B_a[\rho_1]=B_a[\rho_2] \tag{63}
\]

exactly. This is stronger than equality of interface jets and does not use a

The equality is conditional on data absent from the bare final quotient:

1. the oriented circle bundle and normalized fiber current in (23);
2. independent persistent Lorentz bundles and the existence/choice of `g`;
3. the multiplier fields and their variational domain;
4. the dimensionful coefficient `gamma`, or equivalently `ell_*`;
5. in the nondegenerate repaired action, a fixed reference connection or a

Forgetting the marked circle removes the operation in (23); forgetting `g`
makes (12)--(13) undefined; varying an unrestricted reference forces (55).
Thus profile independence is obtained by enrichment, not by deriving (24)
from the quotient equalizer.
