## Derivation

### 1. Exact first-order equations on the open core

D004 (25) gives, with all ordered antisymmetric labels included,

\[
 {\cal C}^{(+)}_{AB}={1\over4\kappa _5}\epsilon_{ABCDE}
 T^C\wedge E^D\wedge E^E=0,                                   \tag{5}
\]

\[
 {\cal G}^{(+)}_A={1\over4\kappa _5}\epsilon_{ABCDE}
 R^{BC}\wedge E^D\wedge E^E
 -{\Lambda _5\over24\kappa _5}\epsilon_{ABCDE}
 E^B\wedge E^C\wedge E^D\wedge E^E=0.                        \tag{6}
\]

These are exact equations of motion.  Since (2) is invertible for every
`s>0`, expand `T^A=(1/2)T^A{}_{BC}E^{BC}` in (5).  Contraction with the dual
frame reduces (5) to the standard injective Cartan map

\[
 T^A{}_{BC}+\delta^A_C T^D{}_{BD}-\delta^A_B T^D{}_{CD}=0.       \tag{7}
\]

Tracing `A=C` in five dimensions gives the torsion trace zero; substitution
back into (7) gives

\[
 \boxed{T^A=0\quad\hbox{on }s>0.}                              \tag{8}
\]

Thus, and only now, the connection is the unique metric-compatible
torsion-free spin connection of the nondegenerate coframe.

### 2. Unique connection and curvature, including signs

The only nonzero coframe derivative is

\[
 dE^4=\rho' ds\wedge d\theta={\rho'\over\rho}E^{34}.            \tag{9}
\]

The equations `dE^A+omega^A{}_B wedge E^B=0`, antisymmetry, and uniqueness
therefore give

\[
 \boxed{\omega^{43}=+{\rho'\over\rho}E^4=+\rho'd\theta,
 \qquad\omega^{34}=-\rho'd\theta,}                            \tag{10}
\]

with every other independent `omega^{AB}` zero.  The sign is checked directly:
`omega^4{}_3 wedge E^3=rho'dtheta wedge ds=-dE^4`.

There is no quadratic connection term because only one internal plane is
occupied.  Hence all curvature forms vanish except

\[
 \boxed{R^{43}=+\rho''ds\wedge d\theta
       =+{\rho''\over\rho}E^{34},\qquad
 R^{34}=-\rho''ds\wedge d\theta=kE^{34}.}                     \tag{11}
\]

Equations (10)--(11) are coordinate-form identities on `s>0`; no division by
`rho` is needed in their first expressions.  Smooth flatness gives the
one-sided form limits `omega^{34}->0` and `R^{34}->0`, although the orthonormal
coefficients `rho'/rho` and `rho''/rho` need not remain bounded for an arbitrary
off-shell flat profile.  This distinction is the normal/mixed caveat emphasized
by D016.

### 3. Independent five-dimensional coframe equations

Define the nonzero oriented four-forms

\[
 \Xi_A:={1\over4!}\epsilon_{ABCDE}E^B\wedge E^C\wedge E^D
 \wedge E^E.                                                   \tag{12}
\]

For each persistent `a=0,1,2`, the four ordered placements of `(34)` and of
the remaining persistent pair in the curvature term of (6) combine to give
`k Xi_a/kappa_5`; the 24 cosmological placements give
`-Lambda_5 Xi_a/kappa_5`.  Thus

\[
 \boxed{{\cal G}^{(+)}_a={1\over\kappa_5}(k-\Lambda_5)\Xi_a=0,
 \qquad a=0,1,2.}                                             \tag{13}
\]

For example `epsilon_03412=+1`, so the `A=0` curvature term is
`R^{34} wedge E^1 wedge E^2=k Xi_0`, fixing the sign in (13).

For `A=3` or `A=4`, the only curvature `R^{34}` repeats an epsilon index and
the curvature term is zero.  Therefore the independent normal and circle
equations are

\[
 \boxed{{\cal G}^{(+)}_3=-{\Lambda_5\over\kappa_5}\Xi_3=0,
 \qquad {\cal G}^{(+)}_4=-{\Lambda_5\over\kappa_5}\Xi_4=0.}    \tag{14}
\]

Since every `Xi_A` is nonzero on `rho>0`, (14) first implies

\[
 \Lambda_5=0,                                                  \tag{15}
\]

and (13) then implies

\[
 k=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \rho''=0.                   \tag{16}
\]

For clarity about component labels: after (8), equations (6) are equivalent
on the open core to `G_AB+Lambda_5 eta_AB=0`.  The equivalence follows by
expanding (6) in the invertible basis (12); it is not used at rank loss.  In
that language

\[
 R_{33}=R_{44}=k,\quad R=2k,\quad
 G_{ab}=-k\eta_{ab},\quad G_{33}=G_{44}=0,\quad G_{03}=0.       \tag{17}
\]

Thus the literal mixed `AB=03` equation is identically satisfied; the
load-bearing **persistent diagonal** equations are (13), while the
`AB=34` normal-circle curvature sector feeds them through `R^{34}` and the
normal/circle diagonal equations (14) force `Lambda_5=0`.  The connection
equations `C_03` and `C_34` are both already satisfied by (8).  This explicit
separation prevents an off-diagonal equation from being misidentified as the
ODE.

Had (14) not been imposed, (13) alone would give the exact linear ODE

\[
 \rho''+\Lambda_5\rho=0,                                      \tag{18}
\]

with sine/cosine, affine, or hyperbolic integration constants according as
`Lambda_5` is positive, zero, or negative.  The independent normal/circle
equations remove those nonzero-`Lambda_5` branches.

### 5. ODE, smooth-flat collapse, and the affine polar cap

\[
 \rho(s)=c s+d.                                                \tag{21}
\]

Continuity of the zero extension gives `d=rho(0)=0`; its `C^1` smoothness gives

\[

flat-jet conditions are needed.  Equivalently, uniqueness for (18) with

one-sided endpoint are retained.

The tempting affine solution

\[
