# D022: Polar attachment variational audit

## Question addressed

Does the least-radical finite-regularity/nonzero-slope polar attachment provide
a controlled background for the existing D004/D005 theory, or does it merely
move the smooth-flat obstruction into a weak-curvature or omitted mixed
interface equation?

## Branch charter

This is the sole theory branch of `CY006-finite-regularity-attachment`. It keeps
the D004 split Palatini bulks and the D005 fixed-seed basic persistent
`SO(1,3)` interface action unchanged. Its material difference from the earlier
background branches is that it drops the smooth-flat tangent class, retains the
nonzero polar slope and mixed connection trace, and audits the resulting

search over warps, sources, actions, counterterms, or moving interfaces. No
sibling output was available or inspected; same-model agreement would in any
case not be independent evidence.

There is no sibling branch in this bounded cycle. Relative to D018--D021, D022
changes only endpoint regularity and the resulting mixed one-sided trace; the
diagonal geometry, connection dynamics, and D004/D005 action are otherwise
frozen.

## Derivation

### 1. Three geometrical objects that must not be identified

#### 1.1 D002 topological quotient

The equivalence relation is

\[
 (u,s,\theta)\sim_q(u',s',\theta')
 \Longleftrightarrow u=u',\ s=s',\quad
 \begin{cases}
 \theta=\theta'\pmod{2\pi},&s>0,\\
 \text{no angular condition},&s\le0.
 \end{cases}                                                    \tag{9}
\]

This quotient has an **open collapsed side** `s<0`. Near `Sigma` its normal
link is `S1 disjoint_union {*} `, as in D002 (21): a circle-cone branch and a
separate ray meet at the interface. Changing the rate from a flat profile to
`cs_+` changes differential regularity, not this topology.

#### 1.2 Half-core polar cap or axis

The punctured radial-circle metric on `M_+` is

\[
 g_2=ds^2+c^2s^2d\theta^2.                                    \tag{10}
\]

For `c=1`, adjoining only the point `s=0` gives the ordinary Euclidean polar
plane in Cartesian coordinates. For `c!=1`, it gives a cone with opening
parameter `c`; the punctured metric remains flat, while the completed cone has
the standard curvature measure of total Gaussian curvature `2pi(1-c)` at its
tip (with the standard disk orientation). Neither object includes the open
collapsed ray `s<0`. Therefore even the regular `c=1` axis is not by itself the
D002 quotient (9).

#### 1.3 Fixed-five-dimensional weak zero extension

One may retain the cylinder coordinate `theta` for all `s` and define

\[
 \underline E^4=c s_+d\theta,
 \qquad
 \underline\omega^{43}=cH(s)d\theta.                           \tag{11}
\]

This is a calculational weak extension, not the physical lower-dimensional
stratum and not the D004 split action. The bare `dtheta` on `s<0` is precisely
the label erased by (9). In particular, distributional curvature of (11) is a
fixed-cylinder statement and must not be identified either with the cone-tip
curvature `2pi(1-c)` or with an interface term already present in D004/D005.

### 2. Exact regularity

The radius obeys

\[
 \rho=cs_+\in C^{0,1}\cap W^{1,\infty}_{\rm loc},\qquad
 \rho'=cH\quad\text{a.e.},\qquad
 \rho''=c\delta_0,                                             \tag{12}
\]

so it is continuous and Lipschitz but not `C1` at zero. Consequently

\[
 E^4=cs_+d\theta\in C^{0,1},                                   \tag{13}
\]

as a one-form on the fixed cylinder. The metric coefficient is more regular:

\[
 g_{\theta\theta}=c^2s_+^2,
 \quad \partial_sg_{\theta\theta}=2c^2s_+,
 \quad \partial_s^2g_{\theta\theta}=2c^2H\quad\text{a.e.}     \tag{14}
\]

Thus `g_theta theta` is `C^{1,1}` (its first derivative is Lipschitz) but not
`C2`; its second weak derivative is a bounded step and contains no delta. The
delta in `rho''` is not visible in the coordinate coefficient (14) because the
first derivative of `rho^2` is continuous.

These statements concern the fixed cylinder. On the `c=1` half-cap, polar
Cartesian coordinates make the positive-side metric smooth at an ordinary
axis. That coordinate repair does not extend through the extra collapsed ray
of (9), and it does not make the zero-extended coframe (13) `C1`.

### 3. Weak torsion and curvature, with signs

**Exact distributional coframe derivative.** Since `d(dtheta)=0` on the fixed
cylinder,

\[
 d\underline E^4=cH(s)\,ds\wedge d\theta.                      \tag{15}
\]

There is no delta in (15): differentiating `s_+` once gives `H`, not
`delta_0`.

**Meaningful restricted weak torsion-free connection.** For the special
diagonal BV connection (11), products are ordinary bounded-function times
Lipschitz-form products. With `E^3=ds`,

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 T^4&=d\underline E^4+
       \underline\omega^4{}_3\wedge E^3\\
 &=cH\,ds\wedge d\theta+cH\,d\theta\wedge ds=0,\\
 T^3&=dE^3+\underline\omega^3{}_4\wedge\underline E^4=0,
 \end{aligned}                                                  \tag{16}
\]

and every other torsion is zero. Thus (11) is a well-defined torsion-free weak
connection **for this special ansatz**. This does not establish a general
Levi-Civita theorem for a degenerate `C1,1` tensor or a nonlinear BV connection
space.

Only one internal connection plane is occupied, so the quadratic term
vanishes. The exact distributional curvature is

\[
 \boxed{

 \qquad
 \underline R^{34}=-c\delta_0\,ds\wedge d\theta.}              \tag{17}

The signs agree with D018 (10)--(11): `R43=+rho'' ds wedge dtheta`.
On each open side separately the curvature is zero. For `c=1`, (17) remains
nonzero because it measures the jump from the polar connection to the zero

intrinsic curvature of the smooth half-plane cap.

## Limiting-case checks

1. **`c=1` smooth polar cap.** On the half-core alone, Cartesian coordinates
   make the cap smooth and flat. This does not falsify (31), because the D002

   still has the finite mixed trace `delta chi43 dtheta`. In the fixed-cylinder

2. **`c!=1` cone.** The punctured core remains flat but a completed half-cap is
   conical with tip curvature `2pi(1-c)`. The weak jump curvature integrates
   instead to a quantity proportional to `2pi c`; the disagreement is expected
   because the constructions and reference connections differ. It cannot be
   used as a hidden source in the unchanged split action.
3. **Open collapsed side.** At every `s<0`, the quotient is genuinely

4. **Torsion.** Direct substitution gives (16) exactly, including the sign

5. **Curvature sign.** Differentiating `omega43=+cH dtheta` gives the plus sign
   in `R43`; antisymmetry gives `R34=-R43`. This agrees with D018/D019.
6. **Boundary orientation and normalization.** D004's core-minus-exterior

   (23). Reversing circle orientation flips both the fiber orientation and the

   unchanged.
7. **Fixing only the coframe slope.** In first-order theory, `delta c=0` does
   not imply `delta chi43=0`; (23) remains. Both data must be fixed, or a new
   equation/action must relate their boundary variations. This attack prevents
   an illicit torsion-free substitution before variation.
8. **Persistent shell cancellation.** Substituting every D005 equation gives
   (29), but differentiating the D005 action with respect to `chi43` gives
   exactly zero. Hence shell compatibility cannot be promoted to full
   differentiability.
9. **Conservation and symmetry.** Each open bulk is flat and satisfies its
   torsionless Bianchi identities. D005 persistent Lorentz covariance is
   unchanged. Full `SO(1,4)` covariance mixing the selected internal normal is
   not present in the basic shell and is not claimed.

The `c=1`, torsion, sign, and persistent-shell attacks all fail to remove the
free-mixed boundary term. They delimit rather than broaden the conclusion.
